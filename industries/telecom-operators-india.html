01 Digest short summary · full detail in the sections below
Snapshot

Telecom operators — Jio Platforms, Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea and BSNL — are the demand layer that funds the whole network equipment chain. Services architecture, not a factory BOM: inputs → platform → delivery → segments. Scope India, NSE/BSE, re-run 2026-11-03; Jio Platforms has been listed since 28-Oct-2026, so the largest operator is now a direct listed exposure.

Process & equipment
  • There is no factory BOM: the process is spectrum → capex → network build → traffic → billing → cash.
  • Spine: RAN and 5G core (Nokia, Ericsson, Samsung, Tejas), optical transport, fibre, towers, OSS/BSS, data centres.
  • The build is now densification, not coverage — ~593,000 5G BTS at Sep-2026, 99.9% of districts covered, ~30,000 BTS added in the quarter.
  • Equipment is imported at the radio and core layer; the domestic share sits in fibre/cable, towers, optical transport and the BSNL 4G stack.
Bottlenecks
  • Spectrum: no auction in FY26 or FY27; TRAI’s Feb-2026 blueprint (~11,700 MHz across nine bands) awaits a DoT back-reference, and Vi’s renewals ride on administrative assignment.
  • RAN and core import dependence — domestic value addition under the telecom PLI is 27.8%; MONTHS to YEARS to requalify an alternate radio vendor.
  • ARPU vs data: ₹180.73 industry wireless ARPU against ₹8.02 per GB, with per-GB realisation falling 10.59% a year.
  • Vi leverage: ₹2.01 lakh cr of statutory dues, negative net worth of ₹35,363 cr, and a ₹35,000 cr bank facility not yet drawn.
  • Tower tenancy at 1.63x, with tower additions at a four-year low for a second quarter.
Value pools
  • The annuity — subscription revenue — stays with operators: AGR of ₹3.34 lakh cr in FY26, and the state takes 7.6% of gross revenue as licence fee and SUC.
  • Value leaks to imports at the RAN/core layer: India’s RAN market is $1.36 bn a year and almost all of it is foreign-designed.
  • Domestic capture: towers (Indus, 25.3% pre-tax ROCE), fibre/cable (HFCL, Sterlite), optical transport (Tejas), revenue assurance (Subex).
  • Adjacent pools: enterprise and cloud (Tata Communications), CPaaS (Tanla, Route Mobile), satcom (licensed, not yet commercial).
  • Capex is rotating to fibre, FWA CPE and data centres as 5G coverage build completes; fixed broadband is the fastest-growing pool (21.8% implied CAGR to FY31) but the thinnest on pricing power.
Market & growth
  • Value: AGR ₹3,34,659 cr in FY26, +10.44% YoY [REPORTED-T1]; FY25 was +12.02% and FY24 +8.24%; 3-year CAGR FY23→FY26 10.2% nominal [CALCULATED from TRAI yearly series]. 5-year CAGR [UNAVAILABLE] — the FY21 base was not fetched this pass.
  • Forward value: mobile connectivity market ₹2.9 trn FY26 → ₹5.2 trn FY31, 12.4% CAGR nominal [CALCULATED from the industry report cited in the Jio RHP, REPORTED-T3]; fixed broadband ₹340–350 bn → ₹920–930 bn, 21.8% [CALCULATED].
  • Volume: wireless data 2,85,376 PB in FY26, +24.74% YoY, 3-year CAGR 21.3% [CALCULATED]; wireless subscribers 1,282.33 mn, +10.19%, 3-year CAGR 3.9% [CALCULATED].
  • Price/mix is negative, not positive: wireless ARPU ₹180.73 (+3.59%) and revenue per GB ₹8.02 (−10.59%), so essentially all value growth in FY26 was physical volume, not price.
  • Third-party sizes are not comparable and are not averaged here: Mordor puts 2026 MNO service revenue at $159.80 bn while Reports Insights puts total telecom services at $115.19 bn for 2025 including pay-TV [CONFLICTING DATA — different scopes].
Watch items
  • 5-Nov-2026: Bharti Airtel Q2FY27 results (and Bharti Hexacom), the read on India mobile ARPU against Jio’s ₹218.8.
  • Mid-Nov-2026: Vodafone Idea Q2FY27 results, with the ₹35,000 cr facility and the ₹45,000 cr capex plan the swing items.
  • Tariff timing: 15% expected either Nov–Dec 2026 (Nomura, Ambit) or deferred to Mar-2027/Q1FY28 (Motilal Oswal, JP Morgan); Jio has publicly declined to lead it.
  • Satcom: Cabinet approval of the 5% AGR spectrum charge and MHA security clearance — both pending, both dated to this quarter in press reporting.
  • Spectrum: any DoT back-reference to TRAI on reserve price would signal an FY28 auction; upper 6 GHz waits on WRC-27.
Coverage gaps

No listed pure-play for spectrum (state-allocated), retail and distribution, or operator talent and managed services. BSNL is unlisted; the nearest listed proxies are noted in the tree.

06 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Spectrum renewal cliff, no auctionNo auction was held in FY26 and reporting on 15-Oct-2026 confirmed none this fiscal — the second consecutive miss of the Cabinet’s annual-sale timeline, with TRAI’s 24-Feb-2026 recommendations still awaiting a DoT back-reference on reserve price. Vodafone Idea’s renewals are the live exposure, bridged (if at all) by administrative assignment. Resilience: policy-led, multi-year.

Opportunity

Cheaper capacity when the sale comesTRAI proposed reserve prices ~19% below the 2022 round (7–37% cuts across 5G bands), halved net-worth entry requirements to ₹50 cr per circle, easier 600 MHz terms (24-year term, four-year payment holiday, 5% upfront) and a 10% discount for building sites in coverage gaps. Lower cost of raw capacity for whoever is solvent enough to bid.

Risk

RAN & core import dependenceIndia’s RAN market was $1.36 bn in 2025, down 5%, and the leadership board is unchanged: Nokia, then Ericsson, then Samsung. Average domestic value addition under the telecom PLI is 27.8% — the radios are assembled here, the design and silicon are not. Resilience: MONTHS to YEARS for requalification at network level.

Opportunity

India-made network gear, now with a component policyPLI 2.0 is proposed at ₹6,000–7,000 cr with a component (not assembly) focus, alongside a dedicated telecom manufacturing zone targeting ₹8,000–9,000 cr of investment. Tejas, HFCL, Dixon, ITI and VVDN scale local RAN, FWA CPE and optical transport; BSNL’s 98,557-site indigenous 4G stack is the proof of concept.

Risk

ARPU vs the data explosionIndustry wireless ARPU is ₹180.73 while data volume grew 24.74% and realisation fell 10.59% to ₹8.02 per GB. Jio added 22% more traffic for 11% more revenue. Volume grows faster than revenue, and Jio has said it will not lean on tariff hikes alone. Resilience: STRUCTURAL.

Opportunity

Tariff repair, mix and digital servicesNomura and Ambit expect a 15% hike in Nov–Dec 2026; India Ratings argues 5% ARPU growth is achievable without any hike, from broadband mix and data growth. Digital services already grow at twice connectivity at Jio (16% vs 11%), and postpaid/FWA premiumisation lifts ARPU without adding spectrum.

Risk

Vodafone Idea funding gapVi carries ~₹2.01 lakh cr of statutory dues (₹1.27 lakh cr deferred spectrum, ₹64,046 cr finalised AGR), negative net worth of ₹35,363 cr at 31-Mar-2026, and a ₹35,000 cr SBI-led facility that is agreed in principle but not drawn. Q2FY27 results are due mid-November. Resilience: uncertain, but materially de-risked by the April 2026 AGR recast.

Opportunity

Vi’s restart flows down the chainThe AGR reassessment to ₹64,046 cr and the shift of near-term payments to FY32–FY41 freed cash for a ₹45,000 cr three-year plan; ₹9,000 cr of orders are already placed with Nokia, Ericsson and Samsung. Vi added subscribers in Q1FY27 for the first time since merger, and every site it builds is tenancy for Indus and revenue for the vendors.

Risk

Tower tenancy dilutionIndus’ tenancy ratio improved only to 1.63x, on ~2,900 tower adds — the lowest second quarter in four years — with lean-site additions collapsing to 13. A three-private-operator market caps co-location upside, and rental growth has run at 4–5% for several quarters. Resilience: cyclical.

Opportunity

Densification, in-building and AfricaCoverage build is over but densification is not: ~30,000 5G BTS a quarter, small cells, metro/tunnel/airport in-building systems and a large single-tenant base available for a second tenant. Indus has also begun deploying in Zambia, Uganda and Nigeria, exporting the Indian shared-infrastructure model.

Risk

5G monetisation gap5G is 56% of Jio’s wireless traffic and 300 mn of its subscribers, but carries no price premium; 5G usage grew 79% while 5G revenue is not separately monetised. Resilience: manageable, but it is the reason sector ROCE stays below cost of capital.

Opportunity

Slicing, FWA and network APIsFixed wireless is the first paid 5G product: 13.61 mn 5G-FWA subscribers at Sep-2026, with Jio at 30 mn fixed broadband and AirFiber at 15.1 mn. Network slicing (Airtel’s Fast Lane), programmable network APIs and enterprise private 5G are the next paid layers — and Subex, Tanla and Route Mobile monetise the complexity.

Risk

Satellite substitution at the edgeStarlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite all hold GMPCS licences and provisional spectrum, but none has launched: the Cabinet has yet to clear the DCC’s 5% AGR charge, and the Home Ministry’s security review is unresolved across the sector. Starlink’s IN-SPACe clearance was refused in August on security grounds. Resilience: regulatory-gated, near-term.

Opportunity

Satcom as a partnership, not only a threatBoth incumbents hold their own licences: Jio is building a sovereign ~1,600-satellite LEO constellation with 23 ground stations, and Airtel-backed OneWeb has an operational constellation. Ground segment, gateways and CPE are an unclaimed domestic pool, and the 13% combined regulatory levy (8% licence fee plus 5% SUC) is a revenue line for the state.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the design and silicon inside the radio. India buys roughly $1.36 bn of RAN a year from Nokia, Ericsson and Samsung, and the PLI’s 27.8% domestic value addition measures assembly, not design. Domestic RAN and optical transport — Tejas, ITI, HFCL, Dixon — is the one capex line where localisation moves the value pool rather than just the assembly margin, and PLI 2.0’s component focus is the first policy aimed at exactly that.
07 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • The annuity layer — subscription revenue. AGR ₹3.34 lakh cr in FY26, +10.44%, of which access services are 83.95%.
  • Cash conversion is the sector’s strength even where returns are not: Jio EBITDA margin 54.1%, Airtel 57.4% (Q1FY27), Indus 54.4%.
  • The state is the largest single claimant: licence fee ₹26,763 cr plus SUC ₹4,040 cr = 7.6% of gross revenue, before spectrum instalments [CALCULATED].
  • Adjacencies: OSS/BSS and assurance (Subex), CPaaS (Tanla, Route Mobile), undersea and enterprise (Tata Communications).
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Jio Platforms NSE/BSE: JPL — 542.4 mn subscribers and 300 mn on 5G at Sep-2026; Q2FY27 revenue ₹40,475 cr, PAT ₹8,389 cr, EBITDA margin 54.1%, ARPU ₹218.8 [REPORTED]
  • Bharti Airtel NSE/BSE: BHARTIARTL — ~494 mn wireless subscribers (Sep-2026); ARPU ₹264, the industry high; Q1FY27 revenue ₹58,539 cr, EBITDA ₹33,599 cr; Q2 due 5-Nov-2026 [REPORTED]
  • Vodafone Idea NSE/BSE: IDEA — 193.1 mn customers, first positive net adds since merger; customer ARPU ₹195; GoI holds ~48.99% [REPORTED]
  • Bharti Hexacom NSE/BSE: BHARTIHEXA — Airtel subsidiary operating only Rajasthan and the North-East circles; listed Apr-2024 [REPORTED]
  • Indus Towers NSE/BSE: INDUSTOWER — 270,480 towers and 440,153 co-locations at Sep-2026; tenancy 1.63x; Q2FY27 revenue ₹8,520 cr, EBITDA margin 54.4%, ROCE 25.3% [REPORTED]
Cost waterfall — ₹100 of gross revenue [DERIVED]
  • ₹7.6 to the government as licence fee and spectrum usage charge [CALCULATED from TRAI FY26: ₹30,803 cr of ₹4,04,067 cr].
  • ₹11.3 of pass-through interconnection charges, down from ₹14.2 in FY25 as OTT substitution shrinks carriage [REPORTED-T1].
  • Network opex and energy next — the tower layer alone books an energy margin of −3.6% at Indus, and diesel is being designed out (49,000 solar sites, 297 MW).
  • Spectrum and capex amortisation is the largest fixed claim and the reason ROCE sits below cost of capital; the ordering here is directional, not an audited split [INFERENCE].
  • EBITDA is what remains — 54–57% at the two large operators, ~21% cash EBITDA at Vi.
Pools that leak to imports
  • RAN, 5G core and transport equipment — $1.36 bn of RAN a year, led by Nokia, Ericsson and Samsung, with domestic value addition at 27.8% [REPORTED-T2: Téral Research / government PLI data].
  • FWA customer-premises equipment, IoT modules and the silicon inside every radio.
  • Satellite constellations and terminals, once satcom launches.
  • Coverage gaps: no listed pure-play for spectrum, retail/distribution or operator talent; BSNL is unlisted.
