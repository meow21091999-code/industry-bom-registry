Snapshot

India's wastewater industry builds and runs the plants that treat municipal sewage and industrial effluent: sewer networks and STPs under AMRUT 2.0 and the state jal nigams, ETPs, CETPs and zero-liquid-discharge trains for industry, faecal sludge plants for towns without sewers, and a fast-growing reuse layer that sells treated water back to industry and power plants. The binding reality is not technology — it is that roughly seven of every ten litres of urban sewage still reach a river untreated.

Process & equipment
  • Spine: collection → screening and grit removal → primary settling → biological secondary treatment (ASP, SBR, MBBR, UASB or MBR) → tertiary filtration and disinfection → reuse or discharge; sludge goes to thickening, dewatering and drying.
  • Industrial ZLD adds chemical precipitation, ultrafiltration, reverse osmosis, multi-effect evaporation (MEE) and an agitated thin-film dryer or crystalliser to convert liquid effluent into recoverable water and a disposable salt.
  • Machinery that decides the outcome: non-clog sewage pumps, aeration blowers and diffusers, decanter centrifuges and filter presses, MEE/ATFD heat exchangers, and the analysers that prove compliance.
Bottlenecks
  • The sewer network, not the plant — roughly two-thirds of a sewerage scheme's cost is pipes, manholes, house connections and utility shifting, all local-government-executed. Resilience: months to years.
  • Membranes — RO/UF/MBR modules are imported at the high-spec end; only two Indian makers exist. Resilience: months, because requalification is slow.
  • Energy and blowers — aeration is the single largest operating cost and the blower market is dominated by imported or licensed technology. Resilience: weeks on price, years on capability.
  • O&M capability and utilisation — plants are built faster than they are staffed or loaded; Jal Hi AMRIT now pays for effluent quality, which is the first incentive that rewards operation rather than construction. Resilience: years, because it is a skills problem.
  • ZLD's salt problem — mixed salt from thermal ZLD has few takers, so a plant can meet the letter of zero discharge and still create a landfill problem. Resilience: structural.
Value pools
  • EPC is where the revenue is and the margin is not: WABAG earns 13.3% EBITDA on ₹3,944 cr, and the smaller municipal contractors earn less, because awards are L1-priced and paid late.
  • O&M is the annuity — 10-to-20-year contracts now make up 38% of WABAG's order book; the same asset, operated, is worth more than the margin on building it.
  • Consumables capture value quietly: membranes, PAC and polyelectrolyte, ion-exchange resin, diffusers, spares. This is the pool that recurs without a tender.
  • Pipes are the largest materials pool in the country's sewerage programme and are almost entirely domestic — the clearest listed exposure to the ₹71,133 cr of approved sewerage work.
  • Leaking abroad: membranes, aeration blowers, decanter centrifuges, MEE/ATFD internals, online BOD/COD analysers and UV/ozone disinfection. Staying home: civil works, pipes, pumps, fabrication, EPC and O&M.

Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play in aeration blowers, online water-quality analysers, UV/ozone disinfection, decanter centrifuges or FSSM technology — every one of them a recurring-revenue node.
  • Municipal water utilities and STP operators are public bodies, so the operating layer itself has no listed exposure; the closest is the O&M book inside WABAG and Ion Exchange.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

The sewer network, not the treatment plantRoughly two-thirds of a sewerage scheme's cost sits in pipes, manholes, house connections and utility shifting — and that work is executed by local bodies with limited capacity. Under AMRUT 2.0, ₹71,133 cr buys 7,098 MLD of treatment but also 40,021 km of sewer; the plant is the smaller half. Resilience: months to years, and it is a civil-execution constraint, not a technology one.

Opportunity

Pipes, precast and trenchless capabilityThis is the most investable localisation story in the industry because it is already domestic: listed pipe makers, precast manhole producers and trenchless contractors capture the largest materials pool in the country's sewerage programme, and the ₹71,133 cr approved pipeline is the addressable market.

Risk

Membranes — imported at the high endReverse-osmosis, nanofiltration, UF and MBR modules for municipal reuse and ZLD come from DuPont, Toray, Hydranautics, LG Chem and Pentair X-Flow. India has two makers — Ion Exchange's HYDRAMEM plant and AdiMem — and neither yet covers the high-pressure seawater and tough-industrial grades. Resilience: months, because requalification of a membrane in a municipal plant is slow and failure is public.

Opportunity

Membrane manufacture and element assemblyMembrane elements are a recurring consumable replaced every 3–7 years, so the prize is an installed-base annuity, not a one-off sale. AdiMem's stated plan — RO/UF at Sayakha with in-house rolling and performance testing, targeting 25% of topline — is the template; Ion Exchange's MANN+HUMMEL technology transfer is the other.

Risk

Energy and the aeration blowerAeration dominates a biological plant's electricity, and electricity dominates its O&M cost. The blower — helical-screw, twin-lobe or turbo — is where the efficiency is won, and the Indian market runs on imported or licence-built machines while the domestic blower makers remain unlisted. Resilience: weeks on tariff, years on capability.

Opportunity

Blower manufacture, diffusers and energy-efficient retrofitsEvery STP built under AMRUT 2.0 needs blowers, and every under-performing STP is a retrofit candidate. A domestic blower line with VFD packaging and dissolved-oxygen control sells into both the new-build and the efficiency market; no listed Indian player currently owns that position.

Risk

Operation, utilisation and the skills gapOnly about 28% of urban sewage is actually treated, and much of the installed capacity runs below design load because the network does not reach it. Operators are few, trained staff fewer, and until Jal Hi AMRIT there was no money tied to effluent quality — only to construction. Resilience: years, because it is a human-capital constraint that cannot be bought.

Opportunity

O&M contracts and performance-linked operationJal Hi AMRIT and the Water Resource Recovery Cells in 25 states convert compliance into a paid service. That is precisely the business WABAG is building (O&M 38% of its order book) and where an operator with instrumentation, remote monitoring and chemical know-how earns a margin the EPC contractor cannot.

Risk

ZLD's salt problemA thermal ZLD train can meet the letter of zero liquid discharge and still create a solid-waste problem: mixed salt from MEE/ATFD has few takers, and the evaporator itself is energy-hungry. CPCB's draft all-industry ZLD notification — partial compliance 2026, full 2028 — would multiply the installed base before the salt market exists. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Salt recovery, co-processing and brine concentrationValue sits in making the salt saleable or the brine disposable: sodium sulphate recovery already works at textile CETPs, and co-processing salt in cement kilns is the practical outlet. A plant designed for recoverable salt rather than mixed salt is a different, better product — and no listed Indian player sells that as a package.

Risk

Sludge and biosolids — no standard, no marketAbout 60% of urban India is on on-site sanitation, so septage matters as much as sewage. Over 1,500 FSTPs have been built, but utilisation is low (40% in Uttar Pradesh, under 20% in some plants), plants are routinely oversized, and there is no national standard for biosolid reuse — so dewatered sludge is dumped. Resilience: structural until standards exist.

Opportunity

Biosolid standards, co-composting and offtakeThe moment a biosolid quality standard exists, dried sludge becomes a product with a price. Until then the bankable play is the machine and the service: dewatering, drying, and scheduled institutional desludging that gives a plant a baseload — the same discipline that turned municipal solid waste into a business.

Deepest hidden import dependency: not the treatment process — the components that decide whether it works. India can build the tanks, lay the sewers, fabricate the pumps and run the plant; it buys the membranes that polish the water, the blowers that keep the biomass alive, the decanter that dewaters the sludge, the evaporator internals that make ZLD possible and the analyser that proves compliance. A ₹10 crore-per-MLD sewerage scheme is mostly domestic civil work with a small, foreign, high-margin core — and the O&M contract that follows is where that core is paid for again, every few years.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • O&M and DBO annuities — 10–20-year contracts at better margins than construction; 38% of WABAG's ₹17,235 cr book, ₹950 cr at Enviro Infra.
  • Consumables — membranes, resins, PAC and polymer, diffusers, spares: recurring, tender-free, and priced on performance.
  • Pipes and civil materials — the largest physical pool in a sewerage scheme, and almost entirely domestic.
  • Rotating equipment — pumps are a genuine Indian strength and export line; blowers are the gap.
  • ZLD and thermal — high ticket, few credible players, energy-intensive to run.
  • Energy and water recovery — biogas and bio-CNG from sludge, and treated-water sales to industry and power.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • VA Tech Wabag NSE: WABAG — FY26 revenue ₹3,944 cr, EBITDA ₹524 cr, PAT ₹370 cr, order book ₹17,235 cr
  • Ion Exchange NSE: IONEXCHANG — engineering 58% / chemicals 29% / consumer 13%; order book ₹2,643 cr
  • Enviro Infra Engineers NSE: EIEL — FY26 revenue ₹1,145.6 cr, order book ₹6,813.6 cr
  • Thermax NSE: THERMAX — ₹10,694 cr group revenue; water and ZLD inside Industrial Products, chemicals ₹758 cr
  • Praj Industries NSE: PRAJIND — ₹3,167.9 cr revenue; ZLD and high-purity water
  • EMS Ltd NSE: EMSLIMITED — ₹732.7 cr revenue, sewerage-EPC pure-play, FY26 hit by payment timing
  • Gujarat Alkalies NSE: GUJALKALI — 1,03,150 MTPA of PAC, the coagulant that every plant buys monthly
Cost waterfall — where a rupee of sewerage capex goes
  • Network, manholes and house connections — the majority. AMRUT 2.0 approved ₹71,133 cr for 7,098 MLD and 40,021 km of sewer; at ₹10.0 cr per MLD network-inclusive against a CPCB benchmark of ₹0.6–3 cr per MLD for the plant alone, the plant is a minority of the spend. [CALCULATED]
  • Civil works (RCC tanks, buildings) — large in value, low in margin, local.
  • Electro-mechanical package (pumps, blowers, diffusers, mixers, panels) — the equipment margin.
  • Process and instrumentation — small in value, decisive in performance, mostly imported.
  • Design, commissioning and margin — 8–13% EBITDA at the top of the industry, thinner below.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Membranes (RO/NF/UF/MBR) at the high-spec end, plus membrane housings and spacers.
  • Aeration blowers, decanter centrifuges, screw presses and thermal dryers.
  • MEE/ATFD internals, forced-circulation pumps and crystalliser hardware.
  • Online BOD/COD, ammonia and heavy-metal analysers; UV and ozone disinfection.
  • Process licences and guarantees for advanced biological and ZLD trains.
