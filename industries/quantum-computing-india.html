DDigestshort summary · full detail below
Snapshot
  • Quantum computing is the machine layer of India's ₹6,003.65 crore National Quantum Mission — processors, the cryogenics that keep them cold, the electronics that address them, and the algorithms layer the IT services firms sell.
  • India has built a full stack once: QpiAI's 25-qubit Indus (Apr-2025) and 64-qubit Kaveri chip (Nov-2025). IBM's Quantum System Two is being installed at Amaravati for a Feb-2027 inauguration.
Process & equipment
  • Spine: wafer and film deposition → Josephson-junction lithography → flip-chip / indium-bump packaging → dilution-refrigerator cooldown → RF characterisation → calibration and error-correction tuning.
  • Machinery: sputterers and e-beam evaporators, mask aligners and e-beam lithography, ICP-RIE etchers, dicing saws, flip-chip and wire bonders, dilution refrigerators, VNAs and AWGs, helium leak detectors, ISO 5–6 cleanrooms.
  • India's first dedicated line is QpiAI's Bengaluru quantum foundry (Aug-2026, ~$20–25 M, lithography through packaging, 70,000 sq ft).
Bottlenecks
  • Dilution refrigerator — Bluefors/Oxford/Janis hold the market; India's first ~70%-indigenous unit was assembled for Amaravati. Resilience: years.
  • Helium-3 — US DOE-dominated, $1,000–20,000 per litre, no substitute. Resilience: structural.
  • Laser and photonics chips — Ti:sapphire entirely imported, combs from 3–4 firms, export-controlled. Resilience: years.
  • Control electronics and cryo wiring — FPGAs, microwave generators and sub-4 K LNAs imported; domestic work is at prototype. Resilience: months to years.
  • Quantum-grade silicon, films and EDA — imported; India has no commercial fab. Resilience: years.
Value pools
  • Cryogenics is the single largest line in a small superconducting system — often 40–60% of installed cost — and it leaks abroad almost entirely.
  • Control and readout electronics is the second pool and scales with qubit count; also imported.
  • India captures facility EPC (L&T built the Amaravati centre), cryostats and gas handling (INOX India, Linde India), and the algorithms/services layer (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Mphasis, Tech Mahindra).
  • The services pool is real but thin — quantum is not a disclosed revenue line for any listed Indian company.
  • Localisation opportunity sits in dilution refrigerators, cryo wiring, precision power supplies and single-photon detectors, each a ₹500–1,000 crore pool by 2030–35 [SCENARIO].
Market & growth
  • India: USD 98.4–120.8 M (2025) to USD 465.8 M (2032) at 24.9%, or USD 922.1 M (2030) at 50.2%. Value, nominal. [CONFLICTING DATA — scope]
  • 5-year CAGR 24.9–50.2% [REPORTED-T2]; 3-year (2025→2028) 49.0% [CALCULATED]; last year +48.9% [CALCULATED].
  • Global: USD 869.9 M (2026), +22.9% YoY, USD 1.1 bn in 2027 and USD 2.3 bn by 2030 (Gartner); USD 1.4 bn (2025) to ~3 bn (2028) on the Hyperion/QED-C series. [CONFLICTING DATA]
  • Volume: India has two installed indigenous systems plus one IBM system from Feb-2027; no published unit series, so volume CAGR is [UNAVAILABLE]. Growth is system size and services attach, not unit count.

Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play in any hardware node — qubit fab, dilution refrigerator, control electronics, cryo wiring, photonics, quantum materials or fab equipment. Nearest proxies: INOX India (cryostats), Linde India (helium), Astra Microwave and Centum (RF), and the IT-services practices for applications.

Discrete-manufacturing architecture with a process-industry core: Tier-4 (materials: quantum-grade silicon and SOI, niobium, aluminium, sapphire, helium-3, Ti:sapphire, rare earths) → Tier-3 (Josephson-junction wafers, cryogenic coax, LNAs, lasers and combs, precision power supplies) → Tier-2 (QPU package, dilution refrigerator, control rack) → Tier-1 (IBM, Google, Quantinuum, IonQ, QpiAI) → operator (cloud access, national lab, Amaravati service model). The cryogenic and photonic layers behave as process inputs — helium and vacuum are consumed, not assembled — so bottlenecks concentrate there rather than in final integration.

Helium recovery and reliquefaction, cryostat service and spares, recalibration, system refurbishment, quantum-safe migration services mostly unbuilt in India

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitysame facts, read both ways
Risk
Dilution refrigerator — a two-supplier chokepoint
Bluefors and Oxford Instruments hold over 70% of the quantum cryogenics market; lead times run 6–9 months and IBM's Quantum System Two uses Bluefors' KIDE platform. India imported every unit until 2026. Resilience: years.
Opportunity
An indigenous dilution refrigerator at real volume
The Amaravati unit was ~70% indigenous and DRDO's ₹500 crore TDF is funding a 20 mK design at Zero mK India. The gap is demand aggregation, not physics — five units a year supports no factory, so a national procurement pool is the unlock.
Risk
Helium-3 and the cold chain
He-3 comes mainly from dismantled nuclear weapons; spot runs $1,000–20,000 per litre and a single dilution refrigerator needs 10–50 litres. India produces neither isotope. Bluefors has contracted lunar sourcing from 2028. Resilience: structural.
Opportunity
Helium recovery, recycling and He-3-free cooling
Closed-loop recovery and reliquefaction are engineering problems India can solve now; magnetic and on-chip cooling are funded research fronts (DARPA and Aalto). Linde India is the distribution layer, INOX India the vessel layer.
Risk
Photonics and control electronics under export control
Ti:sapphire lasers are entirely imported, frequency combs come from three or four firms, and Indian founders report vapour cells, precision lasers and even UHV parts now requiring end-use justification. FPGAs and microwave chains are imported too. Resilience: years.
Opportunity
Component indigenisation with a buyer already in the room
PrenishQ's diode laser (Nov-2025), QuPrayog's frequency comb, Dimira's cryogenic cables, TIFR's 4 K LNAs and QUTE's power supplies are all NQM-supported prototypes with a domestic customer base forming at Amaravati and the 23 new quantum teaching labs.
Risk
The funding and mortality cycle
Demand is grant-led: ₹699.5 crore released against ₹2,330.68 crore sanctioned, and startup support is milestone-linked. Gartner expects over 50% of quantum startups to fail by 2030 as the market rejects losing modalities. Resilience: months.
Opportunity
Modality-agnostic component supply
Cryogenics, cryo wiring, precision power, vacuum and photonics are needed whichever qubit wins — the same hedge the global component houses run. India's four T-hubs already span superconducting, trapped-ion and photonic platforms, which spreads the modality bet.

Structural attractiveness52.0 / 100
economics20%
India's quantum activity is grant- and programme-funded, not a revenue industry: the National Quantum Mission carries Rs 6,003.65 cr over eight years and only Rs 699.5 cr of Rs 2,330.68 cr sanctioned across the four hubs had been released by Feb-2026. The commercial pool is small (India quantum computing at USD 98-121 M in 2025 depending on scope) and the hardware value - dilution refrigerator, control electronics, qubits - accrues to foreign suppliers. QpiAI reports EBITDA-level profitability at a small base [REPORTED-T3], which is the exception rather than the industry.

The global structure is an oligopoly of IBM, Google, Quantinuum, IonQ, PsiQuantum and D-Wave, with cryogenics and control electronics held by a handful of European and US firms. India's credible positions are early: QpiAI's 25-qubit Indus (Apr-2025) and 64-qubit Kaveri chip (Nov-2025) plus a Bengaluru foundry (Aug-2026), a 1,000 km QKD link from QNu Labs, and prototype components (Dimira cables, PrenishQ lasers, TIFR LNAs). That is a pilot position, not a competitive one.

Risk & capability40.0 / 100
capital5%
Capital intensity is high relative to revenue: QpiAI has invested about USD 20-25 M in its foundry alone, on top of a USD 32 M Series A and a Rs 50 cr NCD at 13.85%. Public capital is the main non-dilutive route (NQM seed up to Rs 5 cr, scale-up up to Rs 25 cr, DRDO's Rs 500 cr TDF corpus), and a dilution refrigerator costs USD 600k-1 M before any qubit exists.
2
technology5%
The technology is migrating on several fronts at once - transmon to tunable-coupler and flip-chip architectures, superconducting versus trapped-ion versus photonic modalities, and the shift from NISQ to error-corrected logical qubits. India is at 25-64 physical qubits against NQM targets of 50-1,000 in eight years, and its own hardware roadmap (Ganges 128 qubits in Q1-2027) is being built against a moving target.
2
risk15%
Four structural risks stack: helium-3 scarcity (US DOE-dominated, USD 1,000-20,000 per litre, no substitute), a cryogenics duopoly with 6-9 month lead times, export controls on lasers, vapour cells and UHV parts that Indian founders describe as tightening, and a deep dependence on imported FPGAs and microwave electronics. Any one of these can slip a programme by quarters.
