Snapshot

India’s launch chain — PSLV, GSLV, LVM3 and SSLV, their solid, liquid and cryogenic stages, avionics, structures and the private entrants — is the most indigenous node in the space economy and the one where the constraint is cadence, not content. Scope: launch vehicles and propulsion only, India, NSE/BSE universe, investment-research interest.

Process & equipment
  • Solid stages: maraging-steel casing fabrication, welding, proof-pressure testing, propellant casting and nozzle bonding — the most forging-intensive work on the vehicle.
  • Liquid stages: precision machining of turbopump and injector hardware, then long hot-test campaigns at IPRC Mahendragiri before a stage is cleared.
  • Integration and flight: vehicle assembly at SDSC SHAR, checkout, and a two-pad spaceport that caps cadence independently of vehicle supply.
Bottlenecks
  • Cadence — roughly four to six launches a year against a stated requirement of 50; MONTHS–YEARS.
  • Cryogenic and semi-cryogenic engines — narrow fabricator base, SCE-200 pre-flight, and a Russian semi-cryogenic engine import in approval; MONTHS–YEARS.
  • Motor casings and proof-pressure capacity — three to four qualified shops absorbing five SSLV missions plus Gaganyaan; MONTHS.
  • Private launch scale-up — one orbital success, commercial service from 2027; YEARS.
  • High-end and rad-hard electronics — imported, and the deepest component dependency; MONTHS–YEARS.
Value pools
  • The vehicle-production pool is being privatised in three separate transfers, which converts a state monopoly into the largest contestable pool on the page — HAL’s ₹511 cr SSLV licence, the ₹860 cr five-PSLV consortium, and an open LVM3 ToT with six to eight bidders.
  • Domestic capture is deepest in casings, structures, tanks and avionics — Walchandnagar, L&T, MIDHANI, Data Patterns, BEL and MTAR all earn launch revenue today.
  • Two pools still leak: high-end electronics and rad-hard parts, and — newly this run — the semi-cryogenic engine for the LVM3 upgrade, where a Russian procurement is in approval while SCE-200 remains pre-flight.
  • Spaceport and mission operations is the tightest physical pool and has no listed owner at all.
Market & growth
  • Value, long horizon: launch services US$720 mn (2022, IN-SPACe Decadal Vision) to a US$3.5 bn potential by 2033 — ~15.5% a year nominal [CALCULATED]; a potential, not committed revenue.
  • Value, 3-year (proxy, different scope): NSIL revenue ₹321.77 cr (FY22) to ₹3,000+ cr (FY25), ~110% a year [CALCULATED] — the commercial arm including capacity sales, not launch alone.
  • Value, last year: the whole space economy moved US$8.4 bn (2022) to US$9.0 bn (Aug-2026), ~1.6% a year [CALCULATED]; the 2033 target implies 15–19% a year for a decade.
  • Volume: 12 launch-vehicle missions in the three years to Jun-2026 (~4 a year) [REPORTED-T1], 5–6 a year per IN-SPACe, 7 targeted in FY2026-27, 50 a year stated as the requirement. No vehicle-level volume series exists, so volume CAGR is [UNAVAILABLE]; growth to date is programme timing and price, not units.
  • [CONFLICTING DATA] IMARC puts India space launch services at US$572.3 mn in 2025 against IN-SPACe’s US$720 mn for 2022 — different definitions and scopes, shown side by side rather than reconciled.
Watch items
  • Gaganyaan-1 (LVM3, uncrewed) no earlier than 30-Nov-2026 — the first flight of the human-rated vehicle with an escape tower.
  • LVM3 transfer of technology: bids opened October 2026 with six to eight groups reported interested; an award would redraw the integrator pool.
  • HAL’s first SSLV and the HAL–L&T industry PSLV, now expected around March 2027.
  • SCE-200 first flight expected 2027; integrated LME-1100 methalox engine targeted mid-2027 for NGLV; Skyroot commercial operations from early 2027.
Coverage gaps

No NSE/BSE company derives revenue from a private launch vehicle (Skyroot, Agnikul), operates the spaceport (SDSC SHAR is government), supplies flight-qualified carbon-fibre tow, or owns cryogenic engine design (LPSC, with Godrej unlisted). Those four nodes carry most of the chain’s import and single-source exposure.

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable
Risk

Cadence, not contentIndia flew 12 launch-vehicle missions in the three years to June 2026 — about four a year — and IN-SPACe puts current capability at five or six against a stated need for 50. Two pads, one range and a single design authority set the ceiling. Resilience: MONTHS–YEARS.

Opportunity

The order book already exists; the vehicle supply does notSeven launches are targeted in FY2026-27 and 27 missions listed, five of them SSLV. HAL is licensed to build 6–8 SSLVs a year from 2027, the HAL–L&T consortium holds five PSLVs, and Skyroot targets monthly cadence by end-2027. Every one of those is a purchase order for casings, structures and avionics rather than a hope.

Risk

Cryogenic and semi-cryogenic enginesLPSC owns the design and a handful of fabricators execute it, so a single supplier slip moves a launch. SCE-200 has not flown, and a draft contract to procure Russian RD-191M-class semi-cryogenic engines for the LVM3 upgrade is under approval — an import entering the performance-critical path of the country’s heaviest vehicle. Resilience: MONTHS–YEARS.

Opportunity

Three engine families, all in test at onceSCE-200 hit 200 tonnes at 100% thrust on 5-Sep-2026 after eight lower-thrust tests, the LME-1100 LOX-methane gas generator was ignited on 9-Oct-2026 with an integrated engine due mid-2027, and Agnikul has fired a single-piece 3D-printed semi-cryogenic engine. MTAR (₹2,581.9 cr order book, FY27 growth guided at 80%+), L&T and Godrej are the industrial base that scales with them.

Risk

Motor casings and proof-pressure capacityEvery launch consumes a full set of casings, and only a few shops hold the maraging-steel welding and proof-pressure qualification. Five SSLV missions at three solid stages each, plus PSLV, GSLV and LVM3 booster sets, lands on that same small base in one year. Resilience: MONTHS.

Opportunity

Volume that justifies a second and third qualified shopWalchandnagar’s ₹30.53 cr HS200 order for Gaganyaan (20-Aug-2026, four-year execution) shows the work moving to listed industry. This is the least import-exposed node on the page — the substitution opportunity is capacity, not capability, and it is the cheapest place in the chain for a new entrant to qualify.

Risk

Private launch has flown onceVikram-1 reached orbit on 18-Jul-2026, but commercial operations begin only in early 2027 and Agnikul’s Agnibaan orbital debut has moved to 2027. Reliability at cadence, not a first flight, is what sells a launch. Resilience: YEARS.

Opportunity

The only genuinely new listed exposure in this chainNo NSE/BSE company owns a private launcher today, but the LVM3 ToT bid list — L&T, JSW, Adani, Mahindra, Solar Industries, Bharat Forge, Inox India — is a queue of listed groups positioning for exactly this capability. A vehicle business inside a listed group is the missing piece of the investable map.

Risk

High-end and rad-hard electronicsBoards are built in India; the processors, FPGAs and space-qualified discretes on them are not. A single export-control decision can strand a vehicle that is otherwise complete, and the dependency is invisible in a domestic-content percentage. Resilience: MONTHS–YEARS.

Opportunity

Build-to-specification is already the domestic strengthData Patterns earns a 40.1% EBITDA margin on ₹925 cr and BEL runs a ₹74,000 cr order book on exactly this kind of work. Moving one layer down — qualifying Indian-designed rad-hard parts against ISRO specifications — is the highest-value component substitution left, and it serves defence demand at the same time.

06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Vehicle integration and serial production is the largest pool and has just become contestable: SSLV is licensed to HAL for ₹511 cr, full PSLV technology transfer has been offered to industry, and LVM3 ToT went out for bid in 2026 with six to eight groups interested.
  • Solid propulsion is the pool that scales most directly with cadence — every launch consumes a full set of casings, and casings are made by three to four qualified Indian shops.
  • Avionics and build-to-specification electronics carry the best margins and the best cash conversion in the chain: Data Patterns at 40.1% EBITDA, BEL at a ₹74,000 cr order book.
  • Spaceport and mission operations is the tightest physical pool in the chain, and it is government-owned with no listed operator at all.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • HAL: SSLV licence signed 10-Sep-2025, 6–8 vehicles a year targeted from 2027, but space is under 2% of ₹32,250 cr of FY26 revenue [CALCULATED] — a strategic option, not a revenue line.
  • L&T: integrator of five PSLV-XLs under an ₹860 cr contract and a launch-vehicle hardware supplier since 1975; the carrying Hi-Tech Manufacturing segment had ₹8,366 cr of FY26 order inflows [REPORTED-T1].
  • MTAR: FY26 revenue ₹876 cr, EBITDA ₹171.2 cr, PAT ₹94 cr, order book ₹2,581.9 cr, FY27 growth guided at 80%+ — the purest listed propulsion exposure [REPORTED-T1].
  • Walchandnagar: ₹30.53 cr HS200 motorcase order for Gaganyaan, the clearest single evidence of launch work landing with listed industry [REPORTED-T1].
  • MIDHANI: space was about ₹93 cr of ₹1,206 cr FY26 sales, roughly 7.7% — material, not dominant [REPORTED-T1].
Pools that leak to imports
  • High-end and rad-hard semiconductors, which sit inside otherwise Indian avionics.
  • The semi-cryogenic engine for the LVM3 upgrade — a Russian RD-191M-class procurement is in approval, which would move spend abroad while SCE-200 is still pre-flight.
  • Carbon-fibre precursor and aerospace-grade tow for composite structures and nozzle liners.
  • Some high-strength and aluminium-lithium grades; and, as queue time rather than cash, the two-pad spaceport.

Structural attractiveness68.6 / 100
economics20%
Launch is a low-cadence business with one state customer, so margins come from the work rather than the market. The listed exposures show the spread: MTAR earned a 19.5% EBITDA margin on ₹876 cr in FY26, Data Patterns 40.1% on ₹925 cr, while MIDHANI's space segment was only ₹93 cr of ₹1,206 cr and HAL's space work is under 2% of ₹32,250 cr. Value migrates to industry through fixed-price ToT fees (₹511 cr SSLV, ₹860 cr for five PSLVs), which cap upside per programme.
3
opportunity15%
The forward demand signal is explicit and now contractual: three separate technology transfers in train (SSLV signed Sep-2025, full PSLV ToT offered May-2026, LVM3 EoI opened Jun-2026 with bids in Oct-2026), seven launches targeted in FY2026-27 against roughly four to six a year actually flown, 50 a year stated as the requirement, Gaganyaan-1 no earlier than 30-Nov-2026 and NGLV approved with the LME-1100 engine. Historical growth is the weak half — the whole space economy moved only ~1.6% a year between 2022 and Aug-2026 — so the opportunity is forward, programme-driven and not yet in the revenue line.
4
competitive15%
Flight heritage is a structural barrier: HAL won SSLV against an Adani-backed bidder, and only HAL, L&T and the two private entrants hold any Indian orbital launch capability. The counter-trend this run is that LVM3 ToT drew six to eight bidders including JSW, Adani, Mahindra, Solar Industries, Bharat Forge and Inox India, so the integrator pool is widening and incumbency is worth less than it was. Barriers protect qualification, not pricing — the state sets both price and schedule.
4
value_chain10%
Launch is the most indigenous node in the Indian space chain — casings, structures, tanks, engines, avionics and propellant are all made in-country and ISRO's chairman puts industry content at 80–85% of systems. Two dependencies keep it at 3 rather than 4: high-end electronics and rad-hard parts are imported, and a draft contract to procure Russian RD-191M-class semi-cryogenic engines for the LVM3 upgrade is under approval, which would put an import inside the vehicle's performance-critical path.
3
capital5%
Development runs years before first revenue, test stands and launch pads are state-provided rather than private, and demand is anchored by one customer. The capital that is required is patient and public: NGLV, Chandrayaan-4 and the first Bharatiya Antariksh Station module were approved as government programmes, and the private entrants are venture-funded. Affordable only for large balance sheets or state backing.
2
technology5%
India holds a capability only a handful of nations have and is extending it on three fronts at once: cryogenic (CE-7.5, CE-20), semi-cryogenic (SCE-200 at 200-tonne full thrust, Sep-2026) and LOX-methane for NGLV (LME-1100 gas-generator ignition Oct-2026, integrated engine targeted mid-2027). Agnikul has flown a single-piece 3D-printed semi-cryogenic engine, and TDB is funding reusable-vehicle work from TRL-4 upward. Not 5 because none of the next-generation engines has yet flown.
