D Digest short summary · full detail in the sections below
Snapshot
  • Four segments: space (bus & payload) · launch · ground · downstream services.
  • ~$8.4 bn economy, targeted $44 bn by 2033; 400+ startups and $618.5 mn private funding since the 2020 reforms.
Process & equipment
  • Satellite build is assembly-and-test — cleanroom AIT and environmental testing (TVAC, vibration); most qualification facilities stay with ISRO.
  • Launch vehicles serial-produced by HAL and L&T (SSLV ToT ₹511 cr; PSLV consortium ₹860 cr).
  • Equipment base is precision machining, composites, cryogenic-engine assembly and RF/electronics build.
Bottlenecks
  • Space-grade solar cells — 70–80% imported; zero domestic Ga/Ge refining (years).
  • ADCS, star trackers, rad-hard electronics — imported (years).
  • Atomic clocks — failed on 5 of 11 NavIC satellites (structural).
  • Launch cadence — ~12/yr vs 50–60 needed (months–years).
  • Ground-segment indigenisation — 20% mandated in 5 years.
Value pools
  • Value leaks upstream to imported solar cells, ADCS, atomic clocks and rad-hard electronics.
  • Domestic capture: launch production (HAL, L&T); satellite electronics & payloads (Data Patterns, Astra Microwave, Centum, BEL); materials (MIDHANI).
  • Demand-side pools: EO data & geospatial (MapmyIndia, Genesys, Ceinsys) and satcom subscription (Jio, Bharti); NSIL holds transponder leasing.
Watch items
  • Satcom spectrum: DCC cleared a 5% AGR charge (Sep 2026); Cabinet + security clearance pending.
  • The 5-year ground-segment indigenisation clock.
  • Vikram-1 reached orbit (Jul 2026); IN-SPACe authorisation and investment counts (fast-moving).
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play for space-grade solar cells, ADCS, atomic clocks, private small launch vehicles, GSaaS, navigation services or transponder leasing.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Space-grade solar cells (III-V)70–80% of materials imported (US, EU, Japan); ITAR/ECCN licensing can delay procurement 6–12 months. India has zero gallium and germanium refining capacity. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

MOCVD epitaxy + array qualificationDhruva’s Solis+ rollable arrays and BEL’s panel work sit on a domestic gap in epitaxial wafers; a five-year ISRO offtake could seed TRL-7/8 domestic cell and anti-radiation-coating capacity.

Risk

ADCS & precision sensorsStar trackers (Sodern, France), reaction wheels, gyros and radiation-hardened OBCs are imported. “Made in India” satellites often assemble foreign subsystems. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Five-year subsystem ordersPlacing 5-year orders with 2–3 Indian firms per category (gyros, star trackers, reaction wheels, atomic clocks, optical payloads, OBCs) is the model to build the missing component base.

Risk

Atomic clocks (NavIC)First-generation NavIC clocks were imported from SpectraTime (now Safran); clocks have failed on 5 of 11 NavIC satellites — a navigation-integrity risk. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Indigenous rubidium clocksCSIR-CEERI / ISRO have demonstrated indigenous clocks on TDS-01; qualifying a domestic supplier is a strategic, high-margin prize.

Risk

Launch cadenceIndia flies ~12 missions/yr against a stated need for 50–60 to reach the 2033 target. Skyroot’s Vikram-1 reached orbit on 18 Jul 2026 — India’s first private orbital launch. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

Industry-built launchersHAL’s ₹511 cr SSLV ToT and the HAL–L&T PSLV consortium (₹860 cr, 5 vehicles) shift serial production to industry; Skyroot’s Vikram-1 and Agnikul’s Agnibaan open the private launch market.

Risk

Satcom ground-segment localisationDoT security norms mandate 20% ground-segment indigenisation within 5 years, yet high-end RF, modems and antennas are still largely imported. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Gateway & VSAT build-outNSIL’s rate-contract RFPs for C/Ku/Ka gateways and VSAT terminals, plus L&T’s GSAT gateway execution, are a recurring domestic integration market for BEL, Avantel and Nelco.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the radiation-hardened silicon (OBCs, FPGA, memory) and precision electro-mechanical subsystems — star trackers, reaction wheels, atomic clocks and space-grade solar cells — that sit inside almost every Indian satellite and launch vehicle, regardless of how Indian the final integration looks.
