Snapshot

Pumped storage hydropower in India — the electro-mechanical long-duration store: two reservoirs, a water conductor system, an underground powerhouse and a reversible pump-turbine coupled to a generator-motor. 7.43 GW operates at Aug-2026 against a 100 GW roadmap for FY36, and the machine at the centre of it is imported.

Process & equipment
  • The build is civil-first: drill-and-blast or TBM tunnels, a steel-lined pressure shaft, a machine-hall cavern and a tailrace, then hydro-mechanical gates and penstocks, then the E&M package.
  • E&M is awarded as one integrated package — model-tested runner, pump-turbine, generator-motor, main inlet valve, governor, excitation and step-up transformer. Hydraulic model testing precedes the order, at BHEL Bhopal’s NABL laboratory or the OEM’s own rig abroad.
  • Unit size has settled at 250–350 MW. Variable-speed DFIM units (Tehri, 4×250 MW) need a back-to-back rotor converter that no Indian plant makes.
Bottlenecks
  • Reversible pump-turbine and motor-generator — GE Vernova, Andritz and Voith hold every flagship unit; BHEL can build to 350 MW but had won no PSP package as of Oct-2026. Resilience: years.
  • Hydraulic design and model testing — the runner is site-specific and the qualified design pool is tiny. Years.
  • Geology and gestation — 7–8 years per scheme, Himalayan thrust zones, ₹5.4–6.0 crore/MW. Structural.
  • Underground construction plant — TBMs, raise borers and jumbos are imported. Months.
  • Offtake — the first SECI auction cleared ₹1.35 crore/MW/year on a 40-year PSPPA, so revenue is now discoverable. Months.
Value pools
  • Civil works are 48–65% of capex and stay domestic; HCC and Patel Engineering are the listed beneficiaries with hydro track record.
  • The E&M package is 29–38% of capex and leaks almost wholly abroad — and it is where operating margin concentrates.
  • Hydro-mechanical (gates, penstocks, hoists) is a domestic, fragmented pool at roughly 8–12% of capex.
  • Developer is a 40-year annuity: JSW, Tata Power, Torrent, Adani Green and NHPC hold the visible pipeline; Greenko holds the largest private share but is unlisted.
  • O&M and R&M are thin per MW but sticky; model testing is the highest-barrier service in the chain.
Market & growth
  • Volume, 5-year forward: 7.43 GW (Aug-2026) → 26.69 GW (FY32) = 25.7% CAGR [CALCULATED]; to 100 GW by FY36 = 31.4% [CALCULATED]. These are projections off a policy roadmap, not a historical trend.
  • Value: about ₹5.8 lakh crore cumulative capex to FY36 at ₹6 crore/MW, nominal [REPORTED-T2]. Value and volume move together because the unit is a physical asset, so there is no price/mix wedge — the only real variable is cost per MW, which has drifted up with geology and land.
  • Last year: 6.20 GW (Dec-2025) → 7.43 GW (Aug-2026), +19.9% [CALCULATED], lumpy because Tehri’s 1,000 MW landed in FY26.
  • 3-year historical: [UNAVAILABLE] — no scope-matched commissioning series in the sources retrieved. Do not read the forward CAGR as history.
Watch items
  • NHPC’s 640 MW Indira Sagar (Omkareshwar) scheme has E&M and hydro-mechanical tenders live at Nov-2026 with civil works advanced — the nearest award to watch.
  • NHPC plans about ₹80,000 crore for 20 GW of PSP and a green-arm listing by FY27 [REPORTED-T2].
  • The 100% ISTS waiver runs to schemes awarded by 30-Jun-2028 and commissioned by 30-Jun-2030 — the decision date that governs the pipeline.
  • BHEL’s first PSP E&M order remains the single most informative event in the chain.
Coverage gaps

No NSE/BSE company makes a reversible pump-turbine for PSP service, a DFIM rotor converter, or a tunnel boring machine. The E&M pool is effectively uninvestable in the Indian universe except through BHEL’s optionality.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Reversible pump-turbine and motor-generatorEvery flagship unit is foreign. GE Vernova holds Tehri (4×250 variable speed), Kundah and Upper Sileru; Andritz Hydro holds Bhivpuri, Saidongar-Karjat, Pinnapuram and Tarali; Voith holds Bhavali (6×250) and Upper Indravati. BHEL can design and build to 350 MW and has not won one package. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

BHEL’s first order is a real option, not a sloganBHEL has 2,500 MW a year of hydro capacity at Bhopal with an NABL model-testing laboratory and a robotic HVOF coating line, and the state is the buyer of first resort on NHPC and SJVN schemes. One reference unit converts the whole domestic pool; until then developers pay a premium and take OEM schedule risk.

Risk

Hydraulic design and model testingThe runner is designed for one head range at one site; model testing precedes every award. The qualified Indian design pool is a handful of engineers, and the test rigs sit with BHEL and abroad. Resilience: years — this cannot be bought at any price quickly.

Opportunity

The design and qualification layerModel testing, CFD, transient and water-hammer studies are high-margin, low-capex services that Indian engineering firms and the IITs already partly perform. As the pipeline reaches 26.69 GW by FY32, the testing queue itself becomes a bottleneck and therefore a business.

Risk

Geology, land and seven-year gestationSchemes run seven to eight years from award, in Himalayan thrust zones and Deccan trap, with land, forest clearance and water allocation as live risks. Capex has drifted to ₹5.4–6.0 crore/MW. Resilience: structural — no substitute for the site.

Opportunity

Contractors with a hydro record get priced for scarcityOnly a handful of Indian contractors have executed an underground machine hall. HCC and Patel Engineering carry that record, and a 100 GW roadmap against three or four credible bidders is a seller’s market for civil capability.

Risk

Underground construction plantTBMs, raise borers, drill jumbos and shotcrete robots are imported from a handful of European and Chinese OEMs. A single machine delay moves a cavern programme by months. Resilience: months on cost, structural on availability.

Opportunity

Equipment services, not equipment makingThe realistic Indian play is ownership, refurbishment and operation of the fleet, plus consumables — drill bits, disc cutters, grout — rather than an OEM entrant. Every civil package needs it.

Risk

Offtake and tariff discoveryUntil July 2026 no PSP in India had a competitively discovered storage charge. SECI’s first auction cleared ₹1.34–1.35 crore/MW/year for 1,344 MW across Greenko, Torrent and Tata, on a 40-year PSPPA, with 36 months to supply. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

A bankable annuity reframes the assetA discovered 40-year fixed charge turns pumped storage from a merchant bet into an infrastructure annuity, which is what pulls in the lenders and the listed developers. The risk moves from offtake to execution, which is exactly where the civil scarcity sits.

Risk

Grid integration and variable-speed controlVariable-speed units need rotor-side converters, fast excitation and grid-forming behaviour; Tehri’s drives came from GE Power Conversion. No Indian plant makes the converter, and protection settings for a machine that swings between pump and turbine are specialist work. Resilience: months to years.

Opportunity

The power-electronics pool is domestic, the product is notHitachi Energy India, Siemens Energy India and CG Power already build HVDC and drive electronics in India. A PSP rotor converter is an adjacent product for them, not a new capability — the cheapest route to localising the last imported electrical node.

Deepest hidden import dependency: not the steel in the penstock but the design of the runner. A pumped-storage unit is a one-off hydraulic design validated on a model test rig before a single plate is cut, and India has one accredited rig and a handful of engineers against a pipeline that is supposed to reach 26.69 GW by FY32. The machine follows the design; if the design capability stays thin, buying the turbine abroad is not a procurement choice but the only option.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • The E&M package (29–38% of capex) is where operating margin concentrates, because it is a three-supplier oligopoly selling a site-specific machine into a must-build schedule.
  • Civil works (48–65% of capex) carry the largest revenue pool and the thinnest margin — competitive bidding, seven-year execution, geology risk absorbed by the contractor.
  • Hydro-mechanical fabrication is a steady domestic pool at roughly 8–12% of capex, split among several fabricators with limited pricing power.
  • The developer holds a 40-year regulated-style annuity; the pool is large and durable but competed away at auction to ₹1.35 crore/MW/year.
  • O&M and R&M is small per MW but recurring, and it is where the OEM’s installed base converts into a moat.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Civil: HCC and Patel Engineering, with L&T adjacent — the only listed contractors with an underground hydro record.
  • E&M optionality: BHEL alone, and only as an option until it books a package.
  • Grid interface: Hitachi Energy India, CG Power, Siemens Energy India, TARIL — domestic, profitable, and only a slice of project value.
  • Developer: JSW Energy, Tata Power, Torrent Power, Adani Green, NHPC, SJVN — the layer where the pipeline is actually owned.
  • Materials: JSW Steel and SAIL on plate, with CRGO concentrated in the unlisted JSW JFE venture.
Pools that leak to imports
  • The pump-turbine, the generator-motor and the DFIM converter — call it 25–35% of total project value — leave the country in every scheme now under construction.
  • CRGO electrical steel, roughly 90% imported, sits behind even the domestic transformer pool.
  • Tunnel plant, HVOF coating powders, hydraulic seals and IGBT modules are smaller leaks that nevertheless gate schedule.
Cost waterfall — ₹ crore per MW
  • Reference capex ₹5.4–6.0 crore/MW [REPORTED-T2: CEA roadmap at ₹6 crore/MW; ₹5.40 crore/MW average for environment-cleared schemes].
  • Civil and hydro-mechanical 48–65% → ₹2.9–3.9 crore — the domestic pool [REPORTED-T3: project pre-feasibility reports].
  • Electro-mechanical 29–38% → ₹1.7–2.3 crore — the imported pool.
  • Interest during construction, pre-operative and contingency 6–14% → ₹0.4–0.8 crore.
Value capture — which node keeps the money
  • Reversible pump-turbine — 4.40 / 5 (≈88 / 100).
  • Generator-motor and excitation — 4.05 / 5 (≈81 / 100).
  • Developer / owner — 3.65 / 5 (≈73 / 100).
  • O&M and model testing — 3.25 / 5 (≈65 / 100).
  • Hydro-mechanical 2.85 (≈57) · civil works 2.70 (≈54) · grid interface 2.60 (≈52). The largest revenue pool is not the most attractive one.
