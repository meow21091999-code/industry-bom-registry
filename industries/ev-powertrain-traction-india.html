Snapshot

Everything between the battery and the wheel: traction motor, inverter/motor controller, reduction gear or e-axle, control software and the thermal and test layers around them. In a battery-electric vehicle these systems plus thermal management are 25–30% of vehicle value, and 60–70% once the battery is included. India assembles most of what it sells but still imports the two inputs that decide performance — rare-earth magnets and power semiconductors.

Process & equipment
  • Motor build: lamination stamping and annealing → stator winding or hairpin insertion → varnish/potting → rotor magnet insertion and magnetisation → balancing → end-of-line dyno.
  • Inverter build: SMT board assembly → power-module mounting and sintering → cold-plate and HV connector fitment → potting → firmware flash → high-voltage and EMC test.
  • E-axle: gear cutting and grinding, heat treatment, shaft and bearing fitment, then motor + inverter + reducer assembly and a system-level dyno run.
  • Almost all of this machinery — winding lines, stamping presses and dies, magnetisers, vacuum potting, EOL dynos — is imported.
Bottlenecks
  • Rare-earth magnets: ~90% of world output and ~80% of India's imports from China; resilience YEARS for a new magnet grade, STRUCTURAL for a licence embargo.
  • Power modules and control ICs: 60–70% of module consumption imported; no Indian traction-grade module line at scale — MONTHS to YEARS.
  • Thin high-grade CRNO electrical steel: domestic capacity arriving, top grades still imported — MONTHS.
  • Test capacity: high-voltage dyno and high-power EMC slots are the quiet constraint on launch timing — MONTHS.
  • Design IP: controller and motor platforms are licensed from Gtake, Inovance, EVR Motors and AEM — a commercial dependency, not a physical one.
Value pools
  • Power module and control silicon is the largest single value pool in the inverter and it leaks abroad almost entirely.
  • Magnets are under ~5% of vehicle cost but gate the whole motor — the cheapest bottleneck in the industry and the hardest to substitute.
  • Gear, shaft and differential forging/machining is where Indian suppliers already capture real value — Sona Comstar's BEV revenue was ₹1,154 crore in FY26, 35% of automotive product revenue.
  • Motor and controller assembly is domestic and competitive, with thin margins and heavy OEM price pressure.
  • Integrated e-axle assembly is the migrating pool: whoever assembles motor + inverter + reducer captures the system margin, which is why Bosch–Tata AutoComp, Uno Minda and Valeo are all building it.
  • Localisation is not the same as value capture — a motor assembled in India from imported magnets and modules can be 50–60% local by process and far less by value.
Market & growth
  • Value: India's EV powertrain components and traction-motor manufacturing was ~USD 1.82 billion in 2024, forecast to grow at 22–28% CAGR over 2026–2035 (nominal) [REPORTED-T3].
  • 5-year: [UNAVAILABLE] — no reliable FY21 base in the sourced set; do not quote a five-year CAGR for this layer.
  • 3-year volume: e-2W registrations 7,83,400 (FY23) to 14,53,953 (FY26) is a 22.9% CAGR [CALCULATED].
  • Last year: 2.45 million EVs retailed in FY26, +24.6% — e-2W +21.8%, e-3W +19.0%, e-PV +83.6%, e-CV +122% [REPORTED-T2].
  • Price versus physical: EV penetration rose only to 8.3% of all vehicles because ICE price resets after GST 2.0 in Sep-2025 narrowed the gap — value growth is outrunning unit growth.
Watch items
  • 1 Apr 2027 — PMP full-domestic traction motor and inverter for e-bus and e-truck; already slipped three times.
  • China REPM export licensing — no Indian automotive licence approved as of Sep-2026 [LIVE]; re-check each run.
  • REPM scheme — 6,000 MTPA target, RFP issued Mar-2026; watch bidder selection as the first real localisation signal.
  • FY2027-28 — last PLI-Auto performance year, with under 10% of the outlay disbursed so far.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Rare-earth magnets (REPM)China holds ~90% of global sintered-magnet output and supplied over 80% of India's ~540 tonnes of magnet imports. Export licensing since Apr-2025 has left Indian automotive applications unapproved; Bajaj halved Chetak output in Jul-2025 and redesigned motors onto light rare-earth grades. Resilience: YEARS to requalify a new grade, STRUCTURAL against a licence embargo.

Opportunity

Magnet-free and light-rare-earth motor designSynchronous reluctance and ferrite-assisted designs are now a commercial route, not a science project — Sona Comstar developed a RE-free SynRM in Q2 FY26, Sterling licensed AEM's magnet-free technology, and Greaves partnered Chara Technologies. Whoever qualifies a magnet-free traction motor for Indian duty cycles owns the substitution.

Risk

Power modules and control ICs60–70% of module consumption is imported; SiC comes from Europe and the US, low-cost IGBT from China. No Indian line makes traction-grade modules at scale, and automotive-qualified MCUs remain allocation-constrained. Resilience: MONTHS for a re-source, YEARS for a domestic qual.

Opportunity

Module packaging and OSATIndia's semiconductor mission is funding assembly, testing and packaging rather than fabs, which is exactly the right entry point for power modules: Kaynes Semicon, CG Power's packaging venture and RIR's SiC line in Odisha can capture module-level value without needing a leading-edge fab.

Risk

Design IP licensed, not ownedController platforms, motor topologies and even firmware come from Gtake, Inovance, EVR Motors and AEM. Localisation of assembly without ownership of the design caps both margin and the ability to redesign around a supply shock. Resilience: STRUCTURAL until IP is built.

Opportunity

IP as the last localisation stepL&T's ₹5,000 crore auto-electronics entry with EVR-licensed traction motors claims ~98% component localisation and ~70% indigenous value by cost — the template for how licensed IP converts into domestic value. Design ownership is the next, and hardest, layer.

Risk

Test capacity and homologation slotsHigh-voltage traction dynos, high-power EMC chambers and abuse-test rigs are scarce and booked; ARAI, ICAT and NATRAX are the gate. A launch can slip on a test slot rather than on a part. Resilience: MONTHS.

Opportunity

Test infrastructure as a serviceThe ₹780 crore PM E-DRIVE allocation for upgrading testing agencies, plus private dyno capacity, is a small but real services pool — and a moat for whoever owns certified capacity.

Deepest hidden dependency: the two smallest-value items in the stack — a few hundred grams of sintered magnet and a palm-sized power module — decide whether an Indian EV can be built at all. Everything else in the powertrain can be re-sourced in months; these two take years.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Power module and control silicon — the largest single pool in the inverter, and it leaks abroad almost entirely.
  • Integrated e-axle assembly — the migrating pool; system margin accrues to whoever assembles motor + inverter + reducer.
  • Gears, shafts and differentials — where Indian suppliers already earn real margin; Sona Comstar BEV revenue ₹1,154 crore in FY26.
  • Motor and controller assembly — domestic, competitive, thin-margin, exposed to OEM price pressure.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Sona Comstar — the only listed company with traction motors and e-axle driveline both in the map, and 35% of automotive product revenue from BEV.
  • Uno Minda — EV systems and EDUs inside a ₹1,388 crore Green Mobility segment, 7% of group revenue.
  • Sterling Tools — India's largest MCU maker through a 100%-owned subsidiary, a small slice of a fastener-led listed entity.
  • JSW Steel and Precision Wires India — the two materials pools that stay domestic.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Sintered NdFeB magnets — ~80% imported, no listed Indian maker.
  • Power modules and control ICs — 60–70% and near-total import respectively.
  • Thin high-grade CRNO steel, DC-link capacitors, resolvers and thermal interface materials.
  • Licensed design IP — royalty value that never enters the Indian BOM.
