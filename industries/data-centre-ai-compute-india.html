DGDigest — at a glanceshort summary; full detail below
Snapshot

India’s data-centre and AI-compute stack — land, power, cooling, servers and the subsea/fibre connectivity that ties them to the world. Installed IT capacity has gone from ~375 MW (2020) to ~1.8 GW (H1 2026), with a committed pipeline several times that, driven by hyperscalers and AI. The build is India’s largest capital-formation story, but the supply side stays import-heavy: GPUs, HBM memory, liquid cooling and high-end electricals are the deep dependencies. Scope: India, NSE/BSE.

Process & equipment
  • A data centre is built power-first: grid feed → HV/MV substation & transformers → switchgear → UPS → rack PDUs, with gensets + BESS for backup. Electrical systems are ~40–45% of capex.
  • Cooling is the second spine: air (chillers / CRAH) for conventional racks, liquid (direct-to-chip / immersion, CDU, cold plates) for AI racks above ~25–40 kW.
  • Compute is assembled in India (SMT, integration, burn-in) around imported GPU/memory — Netweb, Dixon, Syrma and Kaynes are the domestic integration layer.
  • Builds are vertical and complex (land cost): a DC goes live in 12–18 months, but the transmission that must feed it takes 3–4 years.
Bottlenecks
  • Power & transmission: 26.3 GW of DC load by FY32 vs ~1.8 GW today; HV substations/transmission take 3–4 years, DCs 12–18 months. Resilience: years.
  • Transformers / switchgear / HV GIS: global queue, 2–5-year lead times; CRGO steel mostly imported until ~FY28. Resilience: years.
  • GPU accelerators + HBM memory: import-only (Nvidia/AMD; Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron); prices repriced sharply in 2026. Resilience: structural.
  • Liquid cooling: largely import-dependent, thin domestic base (Refroid, KuhlTherm nascent). Resilience: months–years.
  • Subsea: ~95% of international bandwidth lands in a ~6 km Mumbai stretch; repair vessels/depot absent. Resilience: months.
Value pools
  • Power equipment (transformers, switchgear, UPS, gensets, cables) is the largest domestic capture — ~40–45% of capex, served by listed Indian makers. Value stays home.
  • The compute pool (GPU + memory) leaks almost entirely to imports — up to ~90% of an AI server’s value.
  • Colocation/operator economics are annuity-like and high-margin (FY26 EBITDA: Nxtra 40%, Sify 45%, AdaniConneX 63%) but almost entirely unlisted.
  • Localisation plays: liquid cooling (Refroid, KuhlTherm), DCIM/orchestration software and optical interconnect (Dixon–Gemtek, Tejas, HFCL).
Market & growth
  • India DC market (scope: DC services, India): ~$1.7 bn (FY26) → ~$6.8 bn (FY30), implied ~41% value CAGR (nominal) [CALCULATED from KPMG]. Volume/capacity series below.
  • Installed capacity (scope: IT load): ~1.9 GW (FY26) → 7.0–7.5 GW (FY30) [REPORTED-T2, KPMG] — capacity CAGR ~35–40%. [CONFLICTING DATA]: Axis Capital sees only ~2.8–3 GW operational by 2030; C&W pipeline 3.9 GW; Economic Survey ~8 GW by 2030; govt target 9 GW.
  • Operator revenue (scope: third-party colo): ~20–22% CAGR to ~₹20,000 cr by FY28 [REPORTED-T1/T2, CRISIL].
  • Last-year: FY26 saw ~500+ MW added; H1 2026 absorption 101 MW, 82% pre-committed hyperscale [REPORTED-T2, JLL/CBRE]. Growth is mostly physical volume; the value CAGR is lifted by the AI/GPU mix.
Watch items
  • DPDP Rules notified 14-Nov-2025; full compliance deadline 13-May-2027 — a data-sovereignty tailwind for domestic capacity. [LIVE]
  • IndiaAI Mission recalibration — ~30,000 of 45,000 GPUs; fresh 10,000-GPU bid; global resale allowed (Oct-2026). [LIVE]
  • DC IPOs: Sify Infinit ’s ₹3,700 cr IPO on hold (valuation gap); Yotta ~₹6,000–8,000 cr issue planned. [LIVE]
  • UP Data Centre Policy 2026 (Aug-2026): ₹2 lakh cr / 2 GW target, AI-compute booster, dual-grid power. [CYCLICAL]
Coverage gaps
  • No listed pure-play for GPU/AI silicon, HBM memory, liquid cooling, DCIM software or the colocation operator layer — nearest proxies are the EMS/ODM integrators (Netweb, Dixon, Syrma, Kaynes), industrial-cooling names (Blue Star, Voltas, Amber) and the conglomerates (Reliance, Adani, Airtel).

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Power & transmissionThe power ministry projects data centres adding 26.3 GW of grid load by FY32 against ~1.8 GW of operational capacity today. Transmission infrastructure takes 3–4 years to build versus 12–18 months for a DC; Mumbai’s next 5 GW would need new 400/765 kV substations and lines. In Q1 FY26 India curtailed ~300 GWh of renewables the grid could not carry. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

A ₹9.6 lakh cr transmission cycleIndia has committed ~₹9.6 lakh crore of transmission capex through 2032; data centres are being added to the Harmonised Master List of infrastructure. Substation, transformer, GIS, HVDC and grid-automation orders flow to Hitachi Energy India, CG Power, GE Vernova T&D, ABB and Siemens — a structural multi-year order book that sits almost entirely with listed Indian makers.

Risk

Transformers, switchgear & CRGO steelGlobal lead times for high-power transformers have stretched to ~5 years with prices doubling; HV gas-insulated switchgear is predominantly imported. CRGO electrical steel — the transformer core material — is almost entirely imported from Japan, Korea and China. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Domestic capacity build-outSiemens Energy approved a 30,000 MVA transformer plant (~₹2,060 cr, by FY30–32); JSW Steel’s Thyssenkrupp Nashik CRGO expansion (~3.5 lakh t) lands ~FY28; CG Power, Voltamp and Atlanta Electricals are adding dry-type/PDU-transformer capacity. Localisation of the electrical stack is the deepest near-term value shift.

Risk

GPU accelerators & HBM memoryAI silicon is import-only (Nvidia/AMD/Intel) and accounts for up to ~90% of an AI server’s value; HBM memory is a three-player global oligopoly. GPU and memory prices repriced sharply through 2026 (Blackwell +15–23%, HBM doubling in Q1), directly hitting the IndiaAI Mission and every server build. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Integration, not fabricationIndia cannot fab GPUs, but it can capture the integration layer: Netweb (Nvidia OEM partner), Dixon and Syrma (server JV/boards) and Kaynes (OSAT). MeitY is redesigning the IT-hardware PLI because GPUs make up ~90% of AI-server cost — shifting incentives toward design, system integration and thermal engineering rather than assembly.

Risk

Liquid coolingAI racks at 25–40 kW (rising past 100 kW) make air cooling insufficient; direct-to-chip and immersion cooling are almost entirely import-dependent, with a thinner domestic base than transformers and far less policy attention. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

A sovereign thermal stackRefroid (DLC/immersion, BPCL dielectric fluids, Technavious/TierX partnerships) and KuhlTherm (RDHx/CDU, India’s first liquid-cooling validation lab, 300 MW→2 GW target) are building an indigenous loop; Navin Fluorine is making two-phase immersion fluid with Chemours at Surat. An SSRN screen flags liquid-cooling balance-of-plant as structurally reachable by small entrants.

Risk

Subsea concentration~95% of India’s international bandwidth lands within a ~6 km stretch of Mumbai’s coast; India has ~1% of the world’s cable landing stations and no domestic cable-repair vessel or depot. Cable planning takes 3–4 years plus up to 2 years to lay. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

A second and third coastGoogle’s America-India Connect and Meta’s Waterworth are adding Visakhapatnam; Airtel is building a Vizag landing station; Tata Communications is investing $152 mn in MIST/Project CS. New landing points, TRAI’s call for cable depots and the 2026 telecom-authorisation reform all widen the connectivity pool.

Deepest hidden dependency: the two inputs that decide whether an Indian data centre can run at all — power delivered at the site (transmission, transformers, HV switchgear, CRGO steel) and silicon delivered into the rack (GPU accelerators and HBM memory). Both are gated abroad or by grid build-out, and both reprice the whole industry when they tighten.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Power & electrical equipment is the largest domestic capture — ~40–45% of facility capex, served by listed Indian makers (transformers, switchgear, cables, gensets, UPS). Value stays home.
  • Compute (GPU + memory) is the biggest pool and leaks almost entirely to imports — up to ~90% of an AI server’s value.
  • Colocation/operator economics are annuity-like and high-margin but almost entirely unlisted; the listed exposure is via conglomerates.
  • Liquid cooling, DCIM software and optical interconnect are the emerging localisation pools.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Hitachi Energy India NSE/BSE: POWERINDIA — transformers, GIS, grid automation; DC orders visible in quarterly disclosures
  • CG Power NSE/BSE: CGPOWER — transformers/switchgear; won a ~₹900 cr US DC transformer order; flags demand shortfall to FY30
  • Netweb Technologies NSE/BSE: NETWEB — Nvidia OEM partner; AI Systems +459.6% YoY in FY26 (43% of revenue); 7,000+ GPU systems deployed
  • Cummins India NSE/BSE: CUMMINSIND — DC gensets ~30–35% of domestic powergen sales FY26
  • Polycab / KEI / Havells / Apar — cables and LV distribution
  • Tata Communications NSE/BSE: TATACOMM · Bharti Airtel · Reliance (Jio) — connectivity and the operator layer
  • E2E Networks NSE/BSE: E2E — GPU-cloud / AI-infra-as-a-service; IndiaAI-empanelled
  • Larsen & Toubro NSE/BSE: LT — DC EPC and construction
Pools that leak to imports
  • GPU accelerators, HBM/DRAM memory and AI ASICs — import-only; the single largest value leak.
  • HV gas-insulated switchgear and CRGO electrical steel; high-end liquid-cooling hardware; busway; DCIM software.
  • Bottleneck nodes: GPU Accelerators & AI Silicon, Memory & HBM, Liquid Cooling, HV/MV Transformers & Switchgear, Subsea Cables & Landing Stations.
