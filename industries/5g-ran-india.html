DGDigest — at a glanceshort summary; full detail below
Snapshot
  • The access layer of India’s mobile networks: radios, Massive-MIMO active antenna units and baseband, plus the RF front-end and silicon inside them.
  • India now assembles and designs the equipment but still imports the parts — 4–5% of a RAN BOM is domestic while 52% of parts imports come from China. Scope: India, NSE/BSE universe.
Process & equipment
  • SMT assembly of radio and baseband boards, RF alignment and power testing, then environmental burn-in — the same spine at Tejas, ITI, Dixon and at Nokia Chennai and Ericsson’s Pune line.
  • Key machinery: SMT lines, RF test benches, anechoic chambers and OTA testers, environmental chambers; RAN silicon is bought in, not made.
  • AAUs are the harder node: the antenna array and the radio are one part, so assembly, calibration and beamforming test happen together.
  • Certification test capacity (TSTLs under MTCTE and ITSAR) is itself a queue that gates every launch.
Bottlenecks
  • RF front-end and power amplifiers — GaN/LDMOS devices and transceivers from a US/Japan/EU oligopoly, no Indian fab. Resilience: YEARS.
  • RAN silicon — L1-PHY FPGAs, SoCs and ASICs (AMD/Xilinx, Altera, Marvell, Qualcomm). Resilience: YEARS to STRUCTURAL.
  • Parts-level China dependence — 52% of parts imports, antennas 69%, populated PCBs 55%, even as finished Chinese gear is excluded. Resilience: MONTHS–YEARS.
  • Demand concentration — 98% of demand is private operators who buy global OEMs; the BSNL order that built Indian RAN capability was a one-off. Resilience: CYCLICAL.
  • Backhaul — only ~44–46% of base stations are fibre-connected, capping 5G-Advanced and densification. Resilience: YEARS (right-of-way bound).
Value pools
  • Value sits with the RF device makers, the silicon vendors and the global OEM brands; India captures assembly, integration and an increasing share of design.
  • Tejas is the proof point and the warning: it built a 1-lakh-site network, then revenue fell 87% in FY26 when that order ended, and it is loss-making on ₹2,315 cr of backlog.
  • The export seam is real: Ericsson now ships India-made antennas to Europe with over half the antenna content local, and Tejas has its first international 5G wins.
  • Component localisation is the next pool — ECMS was doubled to ₹40,000 cr in Budget 2026 and now covers antennas, filters and coils.
  • The annuitisation pool (AMC, software upgrades, managed services) is under-monetised on a 5.6-lakh-site installed base.
Market & growth
  • India TANE market $25 bn (FY25) → $50 bn (FY32F): 10.4% 7-year CAGR, value nominal, no volume series [CALCULATED]. 5-year and 3-year CAGR [UNAVAILABLE].
  • Last year is not one number: HFCL revenue +21.8% (FY26) while Tejas fell 87% on the BSNL base effect — growth is being reallocated, not just added. [REPORTED-T1]
  • Global equipment market 5.3% CAGR (FY23–FY30F) — India grows about twice as fast, but off a 4–5% localisation base.
  • 5G subscriptions: 430 mn (Ericsson, Oct 2026) vs 465 mn (tele.net, Mar 2026) — [CONFLICTING DATA]; scopes differ, and both exceed the 5G BTS count in per-site terms.
Watch items
  • Dec 2026 — BSNL’s commercial 5G target, on software upgrade of the indigenous 4G stack (Kerala and AP circle CGM statements, Aug 2026).
  • 31 Dec 2026 — NCCS security-certification exemption for cloud IP routers and Wi-Fi CPEs expires; MTCTE relaxation package also ends.
  • 31 Mar 2027 — the ₹12,195 cr telecom PLI ends; the successor (reported ₹6,000–7,000 cr, component-focused) is targeted for Q1 FY28 with Cabinet approval still pending as of 08-Oct-2026.
  • Rolling — whether any private operator awards a material 5G-Advanced or Open-RAN refresh to an Indian OEM; that, not BSNL, is the demand question.
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE-listed company exists in RF front-end devices, filters, RAN silicon, RAN power supplies or antennas as a pure-play.
  • Nearest listed exposures: Tejas (RAN OEM), HFCL (antennas, cable, telecom products), ITI (contract manufacture, ODC, turnkey), Dixon (Open-RAN PLI), TCS (integration).

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

The dependence moved inside the boxTrusted-source rules cut Chinese finished equipment to 13.4% of equipment imports, but 52.3% of telecom parts imports are still Chinese — antennas 69.3%, populated boards 55.4%. Localisation of a 4G RAN BOM is 4%, of a 5G RAN BOM 5%.

Opportunity

A component policy now existsECMS was doubled to ₹40,000 cr in Budget 2026 and covers PCBs, passives, antennas, filters and coils; the proposed PLI successor (₹6,000–7,000 cr, reported) is explicitly component- and design-focused. That is the first policy aimed at the 52%, not the assembly.

Risk

RAN silicon dependencyL1-PHY FPGAs, SoCs and ASICs are almost fully imported (AMD/Xilinx, Altera, Marvell, Qualcomm). India imported ~$30 bn of electronic ICs in FY26 and its first fab is targeted for 2028.

Opportunity

Disaggregation lowers the silicon lock-inJio virtualised L1 on general-purpose processors; C-DoT with WiSig and VVDN built an indigenous 5G RAN on BSNL spectrum, live-tested at Ambala. Software-defined radio is the one route that does not need a domestic fab.

Risk

Demand concentration, not capability98% of equipment demand is private operators buying global OEMs. Tejas proved world-scale delivery, then revenue fell 87% in FY26 and losses ran to ₹909 cr once the BSNL order ended.

Opportunity

Exports are the second customerEricsson now ships India-made antennas to Europe with over half the antenna content local; Tejas has an NEC Massive-MIMO supply contract and an end-to-end 5G network order in South America. Global imports of antennas, RRHs and BBUs alone exceeded $219 bn in 2024 against under $1 bn of Indian exports.

Risk

Cost disability at the component layerNITI Aayog puts Indian manufacturers 26–29% above global peers on fiscal disability, and Nokia reports a 20–80% price gap on locally sourced components versus Vietnam or China.

Opportunity

The narrow, under-capitalised seamAntennas, RF front-ends and populated boards are where a domestic substitute is most plausible, and Ericsson’s local antenna line shows the 50%-plus local-content threshold is reachable. The next ₹40,000 cr of ECMS money is aimed here.

Risk

Certification gatekeeping is tighteningMTCTE and the ITSAR/ComSec regime gate every product; a graded ComSec 2026 scheme is being notified and the Pro Tem relaxation runs to 31 Dec 2027. Huawei and ZTE remain excluded. Every exemption expiry is a queue at India’s limited TSTL labs.

Opportunity

A certification moat in India’s favourApproved Indian vendors — Tejas, HFCL, Dixon — and foreign OEMs that manufacture locally clear the gate first, and NITI Aayog explicitly recommends stronger domestic testing and certification capacity as a build-out.

Risk

The 5G capex cycle has turnedCoverage is largely built (5.63 lakh BTS, 99.9% of districts); Ericsson expects incremental, not 2023-style, capex. Only ~44–46% of sites are fibre-connected, so the next spend is backhaul and intelligence, not more radios.

Opportunity

Annuitising the installed baseAMC, managed services, 5G-Advanced software and RIC apps convert 5.6 lakh 5G sites plus ~1.75 lakh BSNL 4G BTS into a recurring stream, and the fibre gap is itself a multi-year order book for HFCL and Sterlite.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the RF front-end and RAN silicon — power amplifiers, filters/duplexers and the L1-PHY FPGAs/SoCs that do the baseband maths — plus the populated boards and antennas that carry them. These sit below the OEM assembly layer India has localised, are almost entirely imported, and remain majority-Chinese by value even as the trusted-source wall keeps Chinese finished gear out.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Value sits with RF device makers, silicon vendors and the global OEM brands.
  • India captures assembly, integration, and a rising share of design and software.
  • Indian RAN value-add is lumpy: one BSNL order built the capability, its completion produced a ₹909 cr loss.
  • Export and component localisation are the two pools growing fastest.
  • Aftermarket (AMC, software upgrades, RIC apps) is the least-monetised pool.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Tejas Networks NSE/BSE: TEJASNET — RAN OEM for ~1 lakh BSNL sites; FY26 revenue ₹1,103 cr, PAT −₹909 cr; Q2FY27 order book ₹2,315 cr [REPORTED]
  • HFCL NSE/BSE: HFCL — FY26 revenue ₹4,949 cr (+21.8%), PAT ₹329 cr, order book ₹21,206 cr, exports 41% [REPORTED]
  • ITI Limited NSE/BSE: ITI — FY26 revenue ₹2,184 cr; ODC, RRU/BBU contract manufacture, West Zone turnkey ₹856 cr [REPORTED]
  • Tata Consultancy Services NSE/BSE: TCS — BSNL consortium lead, ~1 lakh sites integrated plus ₹2,903 cr add-on PO [REPORTED]
  • Dixon Technologies NSE/BSE: DIXON — PLI-approved for Open-RAN (RU, DU, CU, RIC); no disclosed RAN revenue yet [INFERENCE]
  • Reliance Industries (Jio Platforms / Radisys) NSE/BSE: RELIANCE — in-house 5G SA stack, virtualised L1, RAN software licensing [REPORTED]
  • Sterlite Technologies NSE/BSE: STLTECH — optical fibre and cable into fronthaul and backhaul [REPORTED]
Pools that leak to imports
  • RF front-end devices, filters, RAN silicon and RAN power supplies: no listed Indian pure-play at all.
  • 52.3% of telecom parts imports by value are Chinese; antennas 69.3%, populated PCBs 55.4%.
  • Nokia’s own read: locally sourced components cost 20–80% more than Vietnam or China.
