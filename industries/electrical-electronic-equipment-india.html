Snapshot

Two chains in one sector: heavy electrical (grid-linked, policy-driven) and electronic (export-led, assembly-anchored). FY26 imports of electrical equipment rose 33.9% to ₹2.03 L cr while exports grew 11.5% — demand is de-risked by a decade-long ₹9.16 L cr T&D plan, but the incremental rupee is still largely spent abroad.

Process & equipment
  • Transformer spine: CRGO core cutting → coil winding → core-coil assembly → oil/vacuum impregnation → tank & bushing fitment → routine + impulse testing.
  • Cable spine: wire drawing → stranding → XLPE/PVC extrusion → armouring → HV/EHV type-testing; electronics spine: SMT → AOI/X-ray → box-build → burn-in, with OSAT behind it.
  • Core machinery — core-cutting lines, HV winding machines, CCV extrusion, SMT lines, OSAT handlers — is imported from Europe, Japan, Korea and China.
  • Machine tools quantify the gap: India produced ₹17,387 cr in FY26 and imported ₹21,628 cr — 59% of consumption imported, China the largest source at 30% [REPORTED-T2: IMTMA, Aug 2026].
Bottlenecks
  • CRGO steel — ~90% imported; DGTR probe open since 22-Jun-2026 on China/Japan/Korea/Russia, no duty notified; 25–30% of transformer cost. Resilience: months–years.
  • Power semiconductors — ~100% of valves imported; domestic layer is OSAT only, 5 of 12 ISM units producing. Resilience: years.
  • PCBs and passives — ~90% of PCB demand imported; first multi-layer PCB, copper-foil and CCL approvals only landed in 2026. Resilience: months–years.
  • Machinery and certified skills — 59% of machine-tool consumption imported; HV winding, NDT and fab process engineers bind tighter than materials.
  • Rare-earth magnets — 85% sourced from China; the export-control suspension lapses 10-Nov-2026.
Value pools
  • Transformers — now the best profit pool, not the biggest: CG Power’s Power Systems earned 25.1% PBIT on sales in Q2 FY27 on an order backlog of ₹23,646 cr (+58%).
  • Cables & wires — still the largest revenue pool (Polycab Q2 FY27 revenue ₹9,899 cr, +53%) but at 14.2% W&C EBIT, and UltraTech’s entry is compressing the moat.
  • HV switchgear and GIS — thin field of qualified suppliers at 400/765 kV; value stays with the domestic builder.
  • EMS/ODM and smart metering — revenue-rich and annuity-like respectively; margin poor in EMS, visible in AMISP cash flows.
  • Leakage — CRGO, power-semiconductor dies, PCBs, passives and machinery sit offshore; ECMS and ISM are the instruments pulling them onshore, and they have begun to convert into approvals.
Market & growth
  • 5-year: consumption USD 59 bn in FY25, 11% CAGR over 2020–25, value nominal [REPORTED-T2: McKinsey with IEEMA data, May 2026]; volume proxy 9.0% CAGR from IIP 92.3 (FY21) to 141.9 (FY26) [CALCULATED].
  • 3-year: 10.9–12.8% volume CAGR [DERIVED] — the range exists because the FY23 IIP value is published only as a 99–104 band.
  • Last year: +12.6% on IEEMA’s growth index, FY26 [REPORTED-T2], with Q4 alone at +18.5%; import value grew 33.9%.
  • Forward: 11–13% CAGR to 2035 for USD 170–205 bn of consumption and USD 195–235 bn of production [REPORTED-T2: McKinsey, May 2026]; a 15.9% forecast for 2025–30 [REPORTED-T3: Technavio] sits outside that band [CONFLICTING DATA].
  • Price versus physical: most FY26 value growth is price and mix, not units — Polycab’s 47% wires-and-cables revenue growth carried only mid-to-high single-digit volume [REPORTED-T1: company Q2 FY27].
Watch items
  • 10-Nov-2026: China’s suspension of its October-2025 rare-earth package lapses; no extension published as of 9-Nov-2026, and India sources ~85% of its magnets from China.
  • CRGO: DGTR findings pending on a case that covers Apr-2025 to Mar-2026 imports; the applicant has asked for retrospective duty up to 90 days.
  • ECMS: 118 approvals worth ₹77,051 cr as of 6-Nov-2026, now including multi-layer PCB, copper foil, copper-clad laminate and rare-earth magnets.
  • Semicon 2.0: portal open for the fabs, OSAT and machines-and-materials pillars since Nov-2026; RDSS metering sunsets 31-Mar-2028 with 7.24 cr of 20.33 cr meters installed.
Coverage gaps
  • Passive components and specialty chemicals — no listed NSE/BSE pure-play; the first ECMS passives approvals (Globe Capacitors, Syrma coils, Centum filters) went to firms outside the listed universe.
  • Insulators and machinery — the largest insulator maker is unlisted, and the capital-goods layer has no listed pure-play at scale.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

CRGO electrical steel~90% imported and 25–30% of transformer cost; a DGTR anti-dumping case opened 22-Jun-2026 on China, Japan, Korea and Russia (EU excluded for negative injury margin), with no provisional duty notified as of 9-Nov-2026. A duty would raise equipment prices into a grid build-out. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

Domestic GOESJSW–JFE is the sole eligible domestic producer in the case and is scaling Nashik and Vijayanagar toward ~350 ktpa by FY28 — a protected import-substitution franchise whose entire benefit accrues to one listed parent.

Risk

Power semiconductors (IGBT / SiC)~100% of grid valves imported; the domestic layer that exists is OSAT and packaging, not wafer fabrication. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

OSAT, SiC and the machines-and-materials pillar5 of the 12 ISM-approved units were producing by Oct-2026 (Micron, Kaynes, CG Semi among them), and Semicon 2.0 offers 30% capex support plus a declining BOM-linked PLI for equipment and materials makers.

Risk

PCBs, laminates and passives~90% of PCB demand imported; laminates were the binding upstream gap; passive components have no listed domestic pure-play at all. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

ECMS is now converting into plantsKaynes’ Chennai HDI plant (up to 74 layers) ships from Q3 FY27, Wipro’s copper-clad laminate and Indo Asia’s ₹3,546 cr copper foil are approved, and multi-layer PCB went to NeST ICELR in the Nov-2026 tranche.

Risk

Manufacturing machinery59% of machine-tool consumption is imported (₹21,628 cr of imports against ₹17,387 cr of domestic output in FY26), China supplying 30%; core-cutting, HV winding, CCV extrusion and SMT lines carry multi-year lead times. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Capital goods under ECMS and Semicon 2.0Capital-goods projects were the largest single share of the Aug-2026 ECMS tranche, and Semicon 2.0 targets at least three major equipment makers and five chemicals-and-gases firms onshore — the least contested pool in the chain.

Risk

Rare-earth magnetsIndia sources ~85% of its rare-earth magnets from China; the suspension of China’s October-2025 controls lapses on 10-Nov-2026, and a re-tightening would hit motors, fans and EV drivetrains before it hits the grid.

Opportunity

Domestic magnet capacityThe ₹7,280 cr sintered-magnet scheme targets 6,000 tpa, and ECMS has already approved a rare-earth permanent-magnet project (Quantum Magnetics) — a small pool today with a policy floor under it.

Risk

Certified skills & HV test labsHV winding, NDT and fab process engineers, plus impulse/short-circuit test capacity, bind tighter than materials.

Opportunity

Training & testing infrastructureSlowest to fix, so qualification-gated roles and certified labs carry durable scarcity value.

Risk

Grid executionTransmission additions have missed NEP targets three years running (FY25 lines 42% below target); 76 TBCB projects were operational with overruns mostly of 3–18 months, gating equipment offtake timing.

Opportunity

EPC & project managementMulti-year order books for T&D EPC players; execution capability is itself scarce.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the machinery that builds the equipment — CRGO core-cutting lines, HV winding machines, CCV/VCV cable extrusion lines, SMT lines and OSAT handlers. It is the one layer where India imports more than it makes even in a record year (59% of machine-tool consumption), procurement lead times run into years, and the gap sits below every other node in the tree.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Transformers — the best profit pool in the chain: CG Power’s Power Systems earned 25.1% PBIT on sales in Q2 FY27 (up 435 bps), on a backlog of ₹23,646 cr, +58%.
  • Cables & wires — the largest revenue pool but a narrower margin: Polycab’s W&C EBIT was 14.2% on 47% growth, and UltraTech’s entry has drawn no price response yet.
  • HV switchgear / GIS — few qualified suppliers at 400/765 kV; HV GIS grew 41.8% in FY26.
  • Smart meters — AMISP annuities give multi-year visibility; 7.24 cr meters installed against 20.33 cr sanctioned.
  • EMS/ODM — revenue-rich, margin-poor; value migrates up only through backward integration into PCB, laminate and OSAT.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • CG Power NSE/BSE: CGPOWER — Q2 FY27 revenue ₹3,802 cr (+30%), PAT ₹409 cr (+44%), ROCE 25% annualised; Sehore 220–1,200 kV plant operational
  • Polycab India NSE/BSE: POLYCAB — Q2 FY27 revenue ₹9,899 cr (+53%), PAT ₹990 cr (+43%), net cash ₹4,520 cr
  • Hitachi Energy India NSE/BSE: POWERINDIA — transformers, HVDC, FACTS; FY25 order book ~₹19,246 cr
  • Transformers & Rectifiers NSE/BSE: TARIL — pure-play transformer maker
  • Dixon Technologies NSE/BSE: DIXON — largest EMS; FY25 revenue ~₹38,860 cr
  • Genus Power NSE/BSE: GENUSPOWER — smart metering AMISP; order book ~₹28,750 cr
Where the FY26 trade gap sits
  • LT switchgear & panels — imports ₹25,706 cr (+17%) against exports ₹12,339 cr (−28%): the single largest deficit.
  • Cables — the export success: ₹12,125 cr shipped (+31%) against ₹3,173 cr imported.
  • Transformers — power-transformer imports fell 60% to ₹177 cr as domestic capacity came on, but distribution-transformer imports nearly doubled to ₹2,085 cr.
  • Batteries and power electronics — ₹41,670 cr and ₹29,139 cr of imports respectively; East Asia supplied ~66% of the total by value.
Pools that leak to imports
  • CRGO steel — ~90% imported; domestic ~10–12% of demand.
  • Power semiconductors — ~100% of valves; no domestic front-end fab.
  • PCBs, laminates and passives — ~90% of PCB demand imported, and no listed passive pure-play.
  • Machinery — 59% of machine-tool consumption imported, China 30% of it.
