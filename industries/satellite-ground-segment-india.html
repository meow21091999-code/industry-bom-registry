DGDigest — at a glanceshort summary; full detail below
Snapshot
  • The terrestrial half of Indian satcom: earth-station and gateway antennas, hub RF and baseband, VSAT terminals, TT&C and Ground-Station-as-a-Service.
  • Demand is policy-shaped and currently paused: three GMPCS licensees hold authorisations, Starlink has built 20 gateway sites, and none can sell service until the Cabinet fixes the spectrum charge and security clearance lands.
Process & equipment
  • Antenna reflector lay-up and machining, pedestal and tracking-drive assembly, radome and foundation civil works.
  • RF chain build (BUC/HPA, LNB, up/down converters), baseband and NMS configuration, then integration, site acceptance and diversity switching.
  • Qualification needs an anechoic chamber, outdoor antenna test range and environmental test — the real capex barrier, not the antenna itself.
Bottlenecks
  • Baseband and modems are imported proprietary hardware and lock a network for its life — resilience months for GSO, years for LEO.
  • The 20% indigenisation requirement was dropped from the notified 2026 authorisation rules; it survives only as a GMPCS licence condition, so local-content demand is now negotiated rather than statutory.
  • VSAT terminals fell 21.8% in a year to 1,84,579 — the legacy GEO base is eroding before LEO revenue arrives.
  • Gateway capacity sits built but idle: 20 Starlink sites await security clearance and a Cabinet decision on the 5%-of-AGR spectrum charge.
Value pools
  • Ground RF, baseband and modems keep the most money per rupee — and almost all of that pool leaks abroad to iDirect, Gilat, Comtech and Viasat.
  • Antennas and pedestals are where Indian listed companies actually win: Avantel holds a ₹122.58 cr NSIL order for nine S/X-band Ka-ready stations and a ₹117.88 cr DRDO ground-segment hub.
  • Gateway integration is domestic but thin — L&T Smart World built the GSAT-11, -29 and -20 gateways on imported RF and baseband.
  • Services are the larger pool (68% of Indian satcom revenue in 2025) but sit with operators: Nelco holds ~40% share on ₹307 cr of FY26 revenue at a 9.5% EBITDA margin.
  • GSaaS is the fastest-growing sliver and the least defensible: Avantel and Dhruva Space hold IN-SPACe authorisations, but barriers are low and the antenna farm has to be funded by someone.
Market & growth
  • 5-year: India SATCOM equipment USD 1,014.3 mn (2026) → USD 2,258.5 mn (2031), 14.3% value CAGR (nominal) [REPORTED-T2: MarketsandMarkets, Jul 2026].
  • 3-year: USD 1,014.3 mn (2026) → USD 1,515.9 mn (2029), 14.3% CAGR [CALCULATED from the same series].
  • Last year: Indian satcom overall USD 3.25 bn (2025) → USD 3.77 bn (2026), +16.0%, of which ground equipment was 31.95% in 2025 ≈ USD 1.04 bn [CALCULATED from Mordor Intelligence, 2026].
  • Volume moves the other way: VSAT terminals 2,36,039 (Jun 2025) → 1,84,579 (Jun 2026), −21.8% [CALCULATED from TRAI]. Growth is price/mix and LEO build-out, not unit count.
Watch items
  • Cabinet decision on the 5%-of-AGR satcom spectrum usage charge (DCC cleared 3-Sep-2026; 4% effective for notified rural areas) — the gate on every commercial launch.
  • GMPCS security clearances for Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite — lawful interception, NavIC by 2029, and the phased manufacturing undertaking.
  • NSIL RFP NSIL/GS/LMA/2026-01 for four Ka-band gateway stations with diversity sites — a live, dated ground-segment tender.
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play in baseband/modems, BUC/HPA, electronically steered arrays or antenna test ranges. Hughes India, Bhawani Satcom, Dhruva Space and Zetwerk are unlisted.

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Baseband, modems and BUC/HPA are imported.The modem and baseband platform is proprietary foreign hardware (VT iDirect, Gilat, Comtech, Viasat) and is specified into a network for its life. Resilience: months for a GSO hub, years for an LEO gateway where baseband and constellation are designed together.

Opportunity

Sub-system substitution first, baseband last.Astra Microwave, BEL and Avantel can take up/down converters, RF sub-systems and Ka-band blocks now. A domestic baseband is a software-plus-DSP programme measured in years and is the single largest unaddressed pool.

Risk

The localisation anchor was quietly removed.The 20% ground-segment indigenisation clause in the draft authorisation rules does not appear in the Telecommunications (Authorisation for Provision of Principal Telecommunication Services) Rules, 2026 notified in June. It survives only as a GMPCS licence condition, so local-content demand is contractual and negotiated rather than statutory.

Opportunity

The obligation still exists, with a clock.Every GMPCS licensee must file a year-wise phased manufacturing programme targeting 20% of the ground segment within five years of commercial launch. That is a dated, bankable pipeline for whoever can qualify — and it starts only when launch happens.

Risk

The VSAT base is shrinking.Terminals fell from 2,36,039 (Q.E. Jun 2025) to 1,84,579 (Q.E. Jun 2026), a 21.8% decline, as legacy GEO closed-user-group connectivity loses ground to terrestrial networks. Resilience: structural for the CUG model, not cyclical.

Opportunity

Replacement demand is LEO-shaped.The replacement cycle is a terminal-generation change, not a like-for-like swap: hybrid GEO-LEO, mobility and NavIC-capable terminals by 2029. Indian antenna makers are already winning the government-side orders.

Risk

Gateway capacity is built ahead of revenue.Starlink reports 20 Indian gateway sites with hundreds of antennas ready, and no commercial service: the 5%-of-AGR spectrum charge still needs Cabinet approval and security clearance is outstanding. Idle capital of this scale delays the second wave of orders.

Opportunity

Diversity-site architecture is repeatable domestic work.Each Ka-band gateway carries a diversity site, RF-over-fibre link, shelter, power and monitoring — civil and integration scope that is locally executable. NSIL's four-station Ka-band RFP (2026-01) is the template.

06Value pools & cost waterfallwhere value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Baseband, modems and RF front-ends — the highest margin per rupee in the chain, and the part India does not make.
  • Antennas and pedestals — the node where domestic listed companies actually book orders; design, machining and tracking drives are largely indigenised.
  • Services, not boxes — 68.05% of Indian satcom revenue in 2025 was services; ground equipment was 31.95% [REPORTED-T2: Mordor Intelligence, 2026].
  • Annuitised operations — CAMC and GSaaS convert one-off station sales into recurring revenue; NSIL's gateway RFP bundles 3-year warranty plus 5-year CAMC.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Nelco — ~40% share of Indian satcom revenue, FY26 consolidated revenue ₹307 cr, EBITDA margin 9.5% (13.8% prior year), PAT ₹3.3 cr [REPORTED-T1: annual report and CRISIL, Aug 2026].
  • Avantel — FY26 standalone revenue ₹22,135 lakh, down 10.9%; order book ~₹1,000 cr; Q2 FY27 revenue ₹74 cr [REPORTED-T1: annual report; earnings call].
  • L&T Smart World — GSAT-11, GSAT-29 and GSAT-20 gateway programmes for ISRO/SAC, including RF-over-fibre diversity switching [REPORTED-T3: company].
  • BEL and Astra Microwave — ground RF and TT&C sub-systems inside defence and space programmes, not reported as a separate segment [INFERENCE].
Cost waterfall — where a rupee of an Indian gateway goes
  • Baseband, modems and NMS — the largest block, and imported. Ordered first on margin, not on size alone. [DERIVED: NSIL RFP scope and vendor disclosures; no public per-gateway teardown exists.]
  • RF front-end (BUC/HPA, LNB, up/down converters) — second, also imported.
  • Antenna, feed and pedestal — third, and the block that is domestic.
  • Civil, shelter, power and diversity site — large in value, low in margin, entirely local.
  • Integration, commissioning and margin — the thinnest slice, and the one the Indian Tier-1 actually keeps.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Modems and baseband platforms (VT iDirect, Gilat, Comtech, Viasat).
  • BUC/HPA and LNB/LNA blocks, and the GaN/GaAs die inside them.
  • Large-aperture, high-Ka and electronically steered (ESA) antennas.
  • Radomes and precision tracking drives above Ku-band.
  • Antenna and payload test ranges, anechoic chambers and near-field measurement systems.
