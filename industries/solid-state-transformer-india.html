Snapshot

A solid-state transformer replaces the 50 Hz iron-and-copper transformer with a power-electronic converter chain and a medium-frequency isolation transformer, taking 10–36 kV AC in and delivering 800 VDC or LV AC out in one step. The technology crossed from laboratory to first commercial units in H2 2026, driven almost entirely by AI data centres moving to 800 VDC. India has research, pilots and component capability but no commercial product, no device fab and no certification pathway of its own.

Process & equipment
  • Build is a cascade of MV converter cells into a medium-frequency transformer and a DC output stage — assembly and test work, not a smelter or a fab.
  • Silicon-carbide module mounting, sintering and cold-plate assembly set the thermal and reliability envelope; magnetics winding and vacuum potting set the insulation envelope.
  • Test is the real gate: partial-discharge, dielectric, impulse, EMC and full-power grid-simulator runs on a megawatt-class MV test bay.
  • Key machinery: SMT lines, die-attach and sinter presses, Litz and foil winding machines, vacuum potting, busbar fabrication, PD and impulse test sets, MW grid simulator.
Bottlenecks
  • Silicon-carbide modules — 30–40% of BOM, 100% imported, and the voltage class itself gates design: 1200 V ships today, 2.3 and 3.3 kV are a qualification problem. Resilience: years.
  • Certification vacuum — no BIS or CEA standard covers an SST in India; IEC 62477-2 and UL 2877 are the only routes and neither is a domestic approval. Months to years.
  • Medium-frequency transformer and MV insulation engineering — partial discharge, creepage and thermal cycling at kilohertz. Skills, not capital. Resilience: years.
  • Cost — 3–4× the kVA price of the transformer it replaces, so it only wins where DC-native output is the point. Structural until SiC falls.
  • Field record — utilities buy decades of service history; nobody has it yet. Structural.
Value pools
  • SiC modules capture 30–40% of the BOM and effectively all of the technology rent — Infineon, onsemi, Wolfspeed, Mitsubishi, ST — and leak abroad in full.
  • The medium-frequency transformer (8–12%) and DC-link film capacitors (10–15%) are the two pools India could realistically build: winding, cores and film, feeding inverters and chargers as well.
  • Integration and cabinet assembly is where a domestic entrant actually starts, and it is the thinnest-margin layer — the same trap as PCS assembly.
  • Control and grid-forming firmware is the highest-return pool per rupee of capital and sits with unlisted firms and foreign OEMs.
  • The real Indian money near-term is not the SST: it is the iron-core transformer, switchgear and cabling that an 8–10 GW data-centre build needs now, where listed names already earn margin.
  • Test and certification capacity is an unowned pool — a megawatt-class MV validation bay would serve every vendor in the country.

The ladder is the discrete-manufacturing form, which fits an SST: it is an assembled power-electronic system, not a process plant. Value capture is inverted against it — Tier 4 (SiC substrate) and Tier 3 (modules) hold the margin, Tier 2 holds the volume, and the Tier 1 integrator holds the customer relationship and the certification burden.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Silicon-carbide devices and their voltage classEvery SiC module in an SST is imported, and the design itself is gated by voltage class: 1200 V ships today, 2.3 and 3.3 kV are a module-plus-gate-driver-plus-cooling qualification problem before they are a system problem. India has no SiC fab; the listed OSATs package, they do not fabricate. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

A pilot market that buys the first indigenous modulesThe MeitY–Ministry of Power–POWERGRID programme commits to buying roughly 13,000 validated SiC MOSFET modules for VSC-HVDC over a 36-month development window, and Semicon 2.0 carries ₹1,27,500 crore with a compound-semiconductor pillar. A committed offtake is exactly what a device maker needs before a fab.

Risk

No Indian certification or grid-code routeThere is no BIS standard for an SST and no CEA technical standard that names it, so the only approval paths are IEC 62477-2 and UL 2877 through a foreign body — which adds a year or more and hands the qualification decision abroad. Resilience: months to years.

Opportunity

First-mover in an empty certification nicheA megawatt-class MV bay with partial-discharge, impulse and grid-simulator capability — at CPRI, ERDA or a private lab — would serve every SST, PCS, HVDC-valve and charger vendor in the country. The asset is capital-light by fab standards and the demand is already there from adjacent industries.

Risk

Medium-frequency magnetics and MV insulationThe MFT is the component the independent NC State–NYPA–EPRI field campaign flagged as still needing full qualification, and it is where kilohertz switching meets medium-voltage insulation: partial discharge, creepage, thermal cycling, potting voids. India's transformer industry runs at 50 Hz and its winding skill does not transfer automatically. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

The one component India could actually ownCores are imported, but winding, potting, insulation and test are not capital-intensive and India already has the transformer cluster and the copper. An MFT line would sell into SSTs, EV chargers, solar inverters and rail converters — four markets, one capability.

Risk

A 3–4× price premium against a transformer that worksAt US$80–120/kW against US$30–50/kW, and with 3× the losses in pure AC-to-AC duty, an SST loses any procurement comparison that does not value DC output, bidirectionality or footprint. Resilience: structural until SiC costs fall.

Opportunity

A market that does not compete on kVA price800 VDC data centres buy system efficiency and floor area, not transformer kVA. On a 600 kW rack the 415 VAC architecture burns roughly 160 kW more from the grid than an 800 VDC path — an SST is the cheapest way to get there, which is why every commercial launch in 2026 was aimed at AI data centres first.

Risk

No field record, and no domestic productGlobally, field-deployed commercial-class SSTs in revenue service number in the single digits as of 2026; in India the count is zero. Utilities and insurers buy decades of service history. India's own position is a 2 kV module at IIT Bombay, a 1.2 kW prototype at IISc with Delta, and one commercial-scale demonstration by Lotus Wireless. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

A domestic build for the first Indian deploymentsIndia's 8–10 GW data-centre build by 2030, megawatt charging corridors and rail substations will all be specified in the next three years. Whoever builds the first Indian reference unit — with an Indian EPC and an Indian test report — sets the specification the rest of the market copies.

competitive15%
Unattractive in the direction that decides returns: a foreign device oligopoly upstream, a commercial field downstream that consolidated fast in 2026 — Eaton, Delta with Chindata, Sungrow, TBEA at 200 units a year, Zhiguang, Siemens with Reinhausen — and three well-funded pure-plays (Amperesand US$80m, DG Matrix US$60m, Heron Power US$140m). value_chain10%
India can build the enclosure, the busbar, the PCBA, the winding and the assembly labour, and can engineer the control firmware. It cannot make the SiC module, the gate driver, the DC-link film capacitor, the nanocrystalline core, the DSP or the MFT insulation system, and it has no SST certification route. The domestic share of the bill of materials is real; the margin is not.

capital5%
Two very different capital problems. An SST integration and test line is a low-hundred-crore asset and is not the barrier - which is why entry is crowded elsewhere. The device layer costs US$1-5bn a fab, and the test layer needs a megawatt-class MV bay that nobody in India has built. Capital is available for the middle, scarce for both ends.

technology5%
Two migrations run at once and India follows on both: 1200 V SiC to 2.3 and 3.3 kV SiC modules, and line-frequency transformation to a DC-native architecture. India's own capability is a 2 kV module using 3.3 kV SiC devices at IIT Bombay with a 200 kW prototype under way, a 1.2 kW prototype at IISc with Delta, and one commercial-scale demonstration. The gap is engineering depth and field hours, not theory.
