DGDigest — at a glanceshort summary; full detail below
Snapshot
  • India assembles the package but imports the device: 5 of 12 ISM units are producing (Oct 2026), all back-end or discrete.
  • Demand ~USD 3–4 bn against ~USD 0.4 bn of domestic build capacity; imports fill the gap.
Process & equipment
  • Front-end: mature-node Si, or SiC epi plus fab; back-end OSAT: die attach, wire bond, moulding, test.
  • Material is 45–50% of OSAT cost, labour 5–6%, ~70% of equipment capex subsidised.
Bottlenecks
  • Leadframe ~95% imported, 35–40% of material cost; Tata–Enomoto MoU only explores output. Resilience: years.
  • Bonding wire held by Tanaka and Heraeus; Kinesis Chennai plant announced, unqualified.
  • Moulding compound ~90% imported and cold-chained; no Indian EMC qualified.
  • Equipment ~100% imported — the deepest dependency.
Value pools
  • Value sits above and below the OSAT: equipment 4.45/5, design IP 4.25, packaging materials 4.00; the OSAT itself 2.45.
  • Indian capture: OSAT (Kaynes, CG Semi), SiC epi and fab (RIR, SiCSem inside Archean), substrates (Amber).
  • SiC substrate and epi moved down: 6-inch prices fell from ~USD 1,500 to ~USD 500.
Market & growth
  • 12.0% CAGR 2025–2032, value (nominal): USD 1.86 bn → USD 4.05 bn; 2026 base USD 2.05 bn [REPORTED-T2: P&S Intelligence, Apr 2026].
  • Last year +10.5% [CALCULATED]; 3- and 5-year horizons and volume [UNAVAILABLE].
  • Growth is mix, not price: modules grow 16%, SiC carries the highest material CAGR (12.7%).
Watch items
  • Kaynes Q2FY27 results 14–16 Nov 2026; OSAT and PCB revenue guided from Q3FY27.
  • RIR guided ₹12–15 cr of SiC epi revenue in 4–6 months, pending statutory clearance.
  • Semicon 2.0 Pillar 2 applications open since 6 Nov 2026 — first equipment and materials approvals.
Coverage gaps
  • No listed Indian leadframe, EMC or bonding-wire maker; the equipment node has no company in the universe.
  • Global IDMs and unlisted players (Tata, SiCSem, CDIL, Kinesis, Voltix) sit outside it.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Leadframe supplyImported ~95%; 35–40% of material cost; a specific copper-alloy grade stamped to tolerances that take years to master. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Local leadframe intentTata Electronics–Enomoto MoU (22 Oct 2026) explores technology transfer and lead-frame production in Assam; Hindalco copper strip and Motherson stamping remain unqualified feeders. CG Semi puts any such supply chain at three years.

Risk

Bonding wire (Au/Cu)~100% imported; Tanaka (Japan) and Heraeus (Germany) hold the market. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

First local wire plantKinesis Semiconductor (Malaysia) is setting up India's first localised wire-bonding materials facility in Chennai, announced Aug 2026 — intent, not yet a qualified supply line.

Risk

Moulding compound (EMC)~90% imported; package-family specific and cold-chained below −40°C; no Indian EMC qualified at any OSAT line. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Upstream epoxy entryEpigral and Anupam Rasayan attack the upstream resin, not the finished compound. This is the weakest of the localisation plays.

Risk

Organic / ABF and ceramic substrate~100% imported, longest lead time, the most expensive item in advanced packages. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Ceramics and laminate nodsVoltix Semicon won the first ECMS approval for specialty ceramics (6 Nov 2026); NeST ICELR for multilayer PCB; Amber's Ascent-K targets substrate from ~FY2028.

Risk

Equipment & machinery~100% imported; 'no semiconductor equipment manufacturing is happening in India'. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Semicon 2.0 Pillar 2Applications live 6 Nov 2026 with an explicit target of three major equipment makers and five chemicals and gases firms; Rockwell Automation India and Wipro Pari already approved for capital goods under ECMS.

Risk

Qualification lead timeAEC-Q and IATF cycles run two to three quarters at best and years for automotive; capital cannot buy them. CG Semi expects semiconductor EBIT breakeven only from FY28.

Opportunity

Skills and labs70,000 students trained under the Chips to Startup programme; IIT Bhubaneswar hosts a SiC crystal-growth pilot line with Archean. Certification capacity, not headcount, is the constraint.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the materials and equipment layer beneath the OSAT. Material is 45–50% of assembly cost and 60–70% of that material is imported — leadframe (~95%), bonding wire (~100%), moulding compound (~90%), substrate (~100%), equipment (~100%). A May 2026 NITI Aayog study adds the sting: foreign suppliers' Indian arms tend to price at import parity, so an onshore plant does not cut the bill until the manufacturing is genuinely Indian and at scale.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Equipment (4.45/5) — installed base, service and qualification lock-in; the binding constraint on every new plant.
  • Design & IP (4.25/5) — a socket win locks the next generation; India's thinnest layer.
  • Packaging materials (4.00/5) — qualified, concentrated and priced with the process; the substitution pool.
  • Wafer fab (3.65/5) — the largest absolute profit pool but the heaviest capital and the weakest cash conversion.
  • OSAT (2.45/5) and module assembly (2.20/5) — where India's capacity is being built, and the thinnest margin in the chain.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Kaynes Technology NSE:KAYNES — OSAT + PCB, ~₹1,200 cr invested; revenue from Q3FY27
  • CG Power NSE:CGPOWER — CG Semi OSAT + Axiro design; ~₹2,000 cr spent
  • RIR Power Electronics NSE:RIR — SiC epi and packaging, ₹618 cr
  • Archean Chemical NSE:ACI — SiCSem SiC fab, ₹2,070 cr committed
  • Amber Enterprises NSE:AMBER — substrate / PCB JV
Pools that leak to imports
  • Device dies — ~100% of power valves and >85% of SiC content.
  • Packaging materials — leadframe, wire, EMC, substrate at 90–100% imported.
  • Equipment — ~100% imported.
