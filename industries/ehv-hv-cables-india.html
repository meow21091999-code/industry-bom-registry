Snapshot

High- and extra-high-voltage power cables above 33 kV: XLPE-insulated, land-based, with no Indian submarine capability at all. The parent wires & cables market was ₹1.0 lakh cr in FY26, up 11%, and is forecast at about 13% a year to ₹1.75 lakh cr by FY30. EHV (66 kV+) is the tight end — about ₹3,500 cr, roughly half imported, with new domestic capacity only from late 2027. The binding constraint has moved upstream, from cable assembly to compound and accessories.

Process & equipment
  • CCV or VCV triple extrusion onto a Milliken conductor, dry-cured and dry-cooled, then screen, moisture barrier, sheath and armour; joints and terminations are made in-house at EHV.
  • Every new EHV line is an imported package — Troester or Maillefer extruders, Scholz curing, Sikora X-ray gauging — and Troester says Indian manufacture is “not on our agenda”.
Bottlenecks
  • Super-clean EHV XLPE and semiconductive compound: imported; India’s ceiling is 132 kV and the first 66–400 kV line lands in 2027–28.
  • Pre-moulded joints and terminations: a ~USD 100 mn market, ~35% EHV, still largely imported with one limited-range domestic maker.
  • CV-line equipment: no Indian OEM, so capacity arrives at European delivery pace — the reason Polycab’s EHV start has slipped.
  • Submarine cable: no plant, no lay vessel, and both offshore-wind EHV schemes now on hold on cost.
Value pools
  • Cable OEMs keep the money: Polycab’s W&C EBIT was 14.2% in Q2 FY27 against ~11% industry EBIT in FY26.
  • Accessories are now the best-defended import gap — small, utility-qualified, and with no listed Indian pure-play.
  • The compound toll is eroding from below: Ddev Plastiks is qualified to 132 kV and Diamond Power’s 66–400 kV line lands 2027–28.
  • CV-line makers earn the capex cycle, and a 132 kV line costs only ₹25 cr — entry at the bottom of EHV is cheap, 220 kV+ is not.
  • The subsea pool shrinks with the schemes on hold; installation EPC and conductors pass metal through at thin margins.
Market & growth
  • Five-year (FY22–FY26): 12.5% CAGR to ₹1.0 trn, organised players 17% [REPORTED-T2: Motilal Oswal, Jun-2026].
  • Three-year forward (FY27–FY30): about 13% a year to ₹1.75 lakh cr excluding exports [REPORTED-T2: Equirus via Fortune India, 18-Sep-2026].
  • Last year (FY26): +11% in value [REPORTED-T2: Motilal Oswal].
  • Value versus volume: Q2 FY27 revenue grew 47–53% at Polycab and R R Kabel on mid-to-high single-digit volumes, so most of the value growth is metal pass-through, not physical [CALCULATED]. Copper settled USD 13,207/t on 17-Nov-2026, ~10% below the September record [REPORTED-T1: LME].
  • HV band 16.7% nominal CAGR CY2024–CY2030 [REPORTED-T2: Ken Research via Primecab, Apr-2025]; EHV (66 kV+) sized separately at ~₹3,500 cr [REPORTED-T2: Headsup B2B, 3-Nov-2026] [CONFLICTING DATA — different voltage cut-offs, not reconciled].
Watch items
  • Mar-2027 KEI Sanand Phase-II EHV; Apr–May 2027 Polycab EHV (slipped from end-CY2026); Dec-2027 Diamond’s sixth CCV line.
  • Jun–Dec 2027 Diamond Phase-1 compounding, with the 18,000 t super-clean line to follow.
  • On hold: the Tamil Nadu 500 MW and Gujarat 1 GW offshore-wind EHV cable schemes [REPORTED-T1: CEA to 31-Jul-2026].
Coverage gaps
  • No submarine-cable plant or vessel; super-clean EHV compound has no listed Indian maker (nearest: Ddev at 66–132 kV); no listed CV-line OEM or EHV-accessories pure-play.

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

XLPE insulation systemSuper-clean EHV compound is still imported. India’s domestic ceiling is 132 kV (Ddev Plastiks), the first 66–400 kV super-clean line (Diamond Power, 18,000 t) is a captive Phase-2 project commissioning after 2027, and utility qualification of a new compound runs into years.

Opportunity

XLPE insulation system — localisation playThe gap has narrowed from “nothing in India” to “nothing above 132 kV”: a merchant super-clean producer qualified at 220–400 kV would collect a toll on every EHV line in the country, and Diamond’s line proves the process is buildable here. [DERIVED]

Risk

XLPE compound (HV/EHV grade)Above 132 kV the supply is Borouge/Borealis and Hanwha, and both are adding capacity abroad rather than in India — Ruwais and Stenungsund for XLPE, Cheonan for semicon, and Hanwha’s 500 kV HVAC insulation/semicon package verified with Chinese cable makers in October 2026. Indian EHV output therefore depends on imported qualification.

Opportunity

XLPE compound (HV/EHV grade) — localisation playNow a listed exposure: Ddev Plastiks at 66–132 kV and Diamond Power’s captive line at 66–400 kV. The remaining prize is the merchant 220–400 kV grade, where the moat is qualification rather than chemistry. [DERIVED]

Risk

Semiconductive compoundThe super-smooth screen grade is the harder half of the compound problem: it must match the insulation surface at microscopic scale, and no Indian maker supplies it at EHV. Ddev makes semiconductive compounds at volume but not the super-smooth EHV screen.

Opportunity

Semiconductive compound — localisation playA narrower, later-stage play than insulation: the same customer qualification, a smaller pool, and the same single missing capability. [DERIVED]

Risk

Joints, terminations & accessoriesRoughly a USD 100 mn Indian market, about 35% of it EHV, still largely imported, with only one domestic maker covering a limited voltage and size range. Every EHV cable installation needs them, and failure at a joint is the dominant field-failure mode — so utilities qualify the accessory as tightly as the cable.

Opportunity

Joints, terminations & accessories — localisation playThe best-defended import gap left in the chain now that compound is localising: a listed Indian pre-moulded EHV accessory maker is the missing link, and Universal’s Viscas tie-up shows the technology is licensable. [DERIVED]

Risk

CCV / VCV line & equipmentNo Indian OEM: lines come from Troester, Maillefer, Supermac and Scholz, with Sikora X-ray gauges, and Troester says Indian manufacture is not on its agenda. Capacity therefore arrives at the speed of European delivery slots — which is why Polycab’s EHV start has slipped to FY28.

Opportunity

CCV / VCV line & equipment — localisation playThe barrier is voltage-specific, not absolute: Diamond Power is buying a 132 kV CCV line for ₹25 cr, so entry at the bottom of EHV is cheap, while 220 kV+ triple-extrusion still has to be imported. [DERIVED]

Risk

Submarine cable & lay vesselsNo plant, no lay vessel, no repair ship. And the demand case weakened in 2026: the Tamil Nadu 500 MW and Gujarat 1 GW offshore-wind evacuation schemes, together above ₹13,000 cr, were put on hold on cost, “especially EHV submarine cables”.

Opportunity

Submarine cable & lay vessels — localisation playThe pool exists but its timing has moved right: the 220 kV Gujarat B3 package (2029) and the 1,150 km Paradeep–Andaman HVDC link are the anchor orders, and a plant without a vessel would still be stranded. [DERIVED]

Where value sits: cable OEMs keep the money today (Polycab W&C EBIT 14.2% in Q2 FY27, R R Kabel 9.4%, Apar cable 11.0%), and EPC (KEC, L&T, Kalpataru) carries schedule without margin. The open prizes have re-ordered: EHV accessories are now the best-defended import gap, the super-clean compound toll is eroding from below as Ddev and Diamond Power scale, and submarine cable has slipped right with the offshore-wind schemes on hold.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Cable OEMs — the profit centre: record Q2 FY27 margins on a richer mix, high-teens ROIC, and pricing power in branded LV/MV distribution.
  • EHV accessories — small pool, utility-qualified, largely imported: the most defensible margin left in the chain.
  • CV-line and compound suppliers — they earn the capex cycle and the qualification toll, not the cable cycle.
  • EPC and conductors — metal and labour pass through at thin margins.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Polycab India NSE: POLYCAB — EHV commissioning now FY28; Q2 FY27 revenue ₹9,899 cr (+53%), W&C EBIT 14.2% [REPORTED-T1].
  • KEI Industries NSE: KEI — Sanand Phase-II EHV end-Mar-2027, +₹1,500–1,700 cr EHV value; Q2 EHV sales ₹217 cr [REPORTED-T1].
  • Universal Cables NSE: UNIVCABLES — EHV to 400 kV on VCV at Satna, 400 kV HVAC exports to Europe, ₹73 cr EHV modernisation [REPORTED-T1].
  • Diamond Power Infrastructure NSE: DIACABS — the fastest capacity responder: 6 CCV lines, 60% utilisation, ₹25 cr for the next 132 kV line, plus captive compounding and a 765 kV NABL lab [REPORTED-T1/T2].
  • Ddev Plastiks NSE: DDEVPLSTIK — the listed compound exposure, 66–132 kV, ~33% of Indian XLPE compounds [REPORTED-T2].
  • Apar Industries NSE: APARINDS — cable division ₹1,875 cr (+22.1%) at 11.0% EBITDA, but exports down 45% [REPORTED-T1].
  • RR Kabel NSE: RRKABEL — W&C ₹3,010 cr (+53%), segment margin 9.4%, EHV to 220 kV being added [REPORTED-T1].
Pools that leak to imports
  • ~50% of EHV cable consumption; super-clean 220–400 kV compound; EHV accessories; every CV line; all submarine cable.
  • Bottleneck nodes: XLPE insulation system, XLPE compound (HV/EHV grade), Semiconductive compound, Joints/terminations & accessories, CCV/VCV line & equipment, Submarine cable & lay vessels.
What moved in this run
  • Accessories rose from sixth to second on the value-capture ranking; submarine cable fell from fourth to sixth; the compound node fell from second to third as domestic capacity appeared below 220 kV. See the score block for parameter-level detail.
