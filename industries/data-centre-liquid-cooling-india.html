DGDigest — at a glanceshort summary; full detail below
Snapshot

India’s data-centre liquid-cooling stack — direct-to-chip cold plates and manifolds, coolant distribution units (CDUs), quick-disconnects, dielectric / water-glycol coolants and the heat-rejection loop that AI racks above ~25–40 kW require. A drill-down of Data Centres & AI-Compute Infrastructure → “Liquid Cooling — DLC, Immersion, CDU”. Demand is pulled by the AI rack-density step-change (10 kW → 40–130 kW) while supply stays import-heavy and largely unlisted. Scope: India, NSE/BSE.

Process & equipment
  • Cold plates are machined (multi-axis CNC / skiving) from C11000 copper or 6061 aluminium, with a brazed cover and a leak/pressure test — flatness across the die footprint is the hard part.
  • CDUs assemble pumps, a plate heat exchanger, manifolds, filtration and controls into a skid that isolates the rack loop from facility water.
  • Fluids are synthesised (fluorochemical two-phase / single-phase PAO-hydrocarbon-ester; water-glycol blends) — a chemistry business, not an assembly one.
  • Heat rejection uses dry coolers, chillers and cooling towers; the fluid network is SS/corrugated hose with dry-break quick-disconnects.
Bottlenecks
  • Dielectric fluids: two-phase immersion was PFAS-dependent; 3M’s Novec exit completed end-2025 and no at-scale non-PFAS two-phase fluid is qualified. Resilience: years.
  • Cold plates / CDUs / quick-disconnects: import-dependent (Boyd, CoolIT, Vertiv, Schneider/Motivair, CPC, Staubli); thin domestic base. Resilience: months–years.
  • Commissioning skills: certified liquid-cooling technicians and loop-chemistry know-how are scarce. Resilience: months–years.
  • Standards: no Indian liquid-cooling certification regime yet — specs set by OCP / ASHRAE / Nvidia. Resilience: structural.
  • Chemistry & cleanliness: mixed-metal galvanic corrosion and particulate in cold-plate channels are the dominant long-run failure mode.
Value pools
  • Fluids & chemistry is the highest-margin node and the one place a listed Indian play exists — Navin Fluorine (two-phase, with Chemours at Surat), with Castrol India and Gulf Oil Lubricants in trials.
  • Heat rejection (chillers, dry coolers, plate HX) is the largest domestic capture — Voltas, Blue Star, Thermax, with KRN Heat Exchanger as the component supplier.
  • Cold plates and quick-disconnects carry high value but are imported; the machining capability to localise exists (CNC/skiving) but is not yet dedicated to DC cold plates.
  • CDUs are integration-heavy and mid-value — foreign-led, but Indian entrants (RDP, Dynetics, Neometrix, KuhlTherm) and HVAC OEMs (Blue Star, Voltas) are building.
  • Aftermarket (fluid service, filtration, coolant recycling) is a recurring annuity and almost entirely unlisted.
Market & growth
  • India DC cooling market (scope: all cooling, India): ~$2.4 bn (2025) → ~$7.7 bn (2030), implied ~26% value CAGR (nominal) [CALCULATED from techcircle, T3]. Liquid-based segment ~$0.6 bn (2024) [REPORTED-T3, Technavio].
  • [CONFLICTING DATA]: IMARC sees the whole cooling market at only ~$0.69 bn (2025) → ~$2.23 bn (2034), ~13.5% CAGR; Stratview sees India liquid cooling at just ~$0.3 bn by 2030 — far below Technavio’s $0.6 bn for 2024. Scope differs (all cooling vs liquid only; product vs service).
  • Volume: India DC capacity ~1.4 GW (mid-2025) → ~2 GW (end-2025) → 4.5–12 GW (2030) [CONFLICTING DATA]; rack density 10 kW → 40–130 kW; ~70% liquid / 30% air in new builds [JLL, T2].
  • 3-year / last-year: liquid cooling is the fastest-growing cooling segment and rack-based cooling the fastest-growing type. Growth is predominantly physical volume (kW); the value CAGR is lifted by a price/mix shift toward immersion and two-phase.
Watch items
  • 3M Novec exit completed end-2025; US EPA TSCA 8(a)(7) PFAS reporting window opens to 31-Jan-2027. [LIVE]
  • Chemours – Navin Fluorine two-phase immersion fluid (Opteon-class) plant at Surat, Gujarat: ~US$14 mn capex (US$5 mn from Chemours), commissioning guided to Jul–Aug 2026 after slipping from 1QFY27; ~75% utilisation targeted by end-FY27, with phase-2 capex potentially ~10x. Correction from the prior run: start-up moved out of 1QFY27. [REPORTED-T2]
  • Schneider Electric Motivair liquid-cooling factory in Bengaluru (CDUs, RDHx, cold plates for 100 kW+ racks) — India’s 3rd global hub, Feb-2026. [CYCLICAL]
  • Blue Star liquid-cooling / CDU models “commercially ready within ~12 months”; Gulf Oil and Castrol fluids in India trials. [CYCLICAL]
Coverage gaps
  • No listed pure-play for cold plates, quick-disconnects, CDUs, immersion tanks, leak detection or liquid-cooling service. Nearest proxies are the fluids names (Navin Fluorine, Castrol India, Gulf Oil), the heat-rejection OEMs (Voltas, Blue Star, Thermax, KRN), the fluid-network play (Aeroflex) and HVAC components (Amber).

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Dielectric fluids & the PFAS trapTwo-phase immersion was built on 3M’s Novec fluorochemicals, which 3M withdrew over PFAS regulation and completed exiting at end-2025 — cutting approved chemistries from roughly seven to four and triggering a ~40% secondary-market price rise. No at-scale, non-PFAS two-phase fluid is qualified; Schneider and Microsoft both flag two-phase chemistries as PFAS by current definitions. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

India as a fluid-chemistry supplierNavin Fluorine is producing two-phase immersion fluid with Chemours at Surat (from ~1QFY27), and Castrol India and Gulf Oil Lubricants are in trials — putting the highest-margin node of the stack on Indian soil. A non-PFAS two-phase molecule, or a qualified single-phase hydrocarbon/ester family, is the single biggest open prize in the whole ecosystem.

Risk

Cold plates, CDUs & quick-disconnectsThe thermal-capture and distribution hardware — cold plates, manifolds, CDUs, dry-break quick-disconnects — is dominated by foreign OEMs (Boyd, CoolIT, Vertiv, Schneider/Motivair) and connector houses (CPC, Staubli, Parker, Danfoss), with essentially no listed Indian pure-play. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

A machining-and-skids localisationThe capability to localise cold plates (multi-axis CNC, skiving, brazing, leak test) and fluid-network skids already exists in India’s heat-exchanger and precision-machining base (KRN, Amber). Aeroflex already holds an exclusive India liquid-cooling contract with a US hyperscaler and has tripled skid capacity; RDP, Dynetics and Neometrix build CDUs; Blue Star and Voltas are moving in from HVAC.

Risk

Skills, commissioning & standardsAdvanced liquid loops — CDUs, RDHx, immersion tanks — need specialised commissioning and loop-chemistry expertise, and India’s certified talent pool is thin relative to the build pace. There is no Indian liquid-cooling certification regime; specs are set by OCP, ASHRAE and Nvidia (GB200 NVL72). Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

A sovereign thermal-service layerKuhlTherm has stood up India’s first liquid-cooling validation lab; RDP ships CDUs with pan-India onsite SLAs on GeM. Commissioning, validation, leak-test and coolant-service capability is a defensible services annuity — and a natural extension for the HVAC/MEP contractors (Blue Star, Voltas) and the operators building the halls.

Deepest hidden dependency: the two inputs that decide whether a high-density Indian AI hall can run at all — the dielectric fluid chemistry (PFAS-constrained, two-phase supply rebuilding after 3M’s exit) and the precision thermal hardware (cold plates, CDUs, quick-disconnects). Both are gated abroad today; both are the localisation prizes the AI build-out has made urgent.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Fluids & chemistry is the highest-margin node and the only one with a scaled listed Indian play — Navin Fluorine (two-phase, with Chemours), with Castrol India and Gulf Oil Lubricants in trials.
  • Heat rejection (chillers, dry coolers, plate HX) is the largest domestic capture by revenue — Voltas, Blue Star, Thermax, with KRN as the component supplier.
  • Cold plates & quick-disconnects carry high value and leak almost entirely to imports (Boyd, CoolIT, CPC, Staubli).
  • CDUs are integration-heavy and mid-value; foreign-led, with Indian private entrants and HVAC OEMs building.
  • Aftermarket (fluid service, filtration, recycling) is a recurring annuity and essentially unlisted.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Navin Fluorine NSE/BSE: NAVINFLUOR — two-phase immersion fluid with Chemours (Surat, from ~1QFY27)
  • Castrol India NSE/BSE: CASTROLIND — DC liquid-cooling fluids for immersion & DTC; India trials
  • Gulf Oil Lubricants NSE/BSE: GULFOILLUB — two cooling products in testing/validation
  • Voltas NSE/BSE: VOLTAS · Blue Star NSE/BSE: BLUESTARCO — chillers, MEP and liquid-cooling/CDU development
  • Thermax NSE/BSE: THERMAX — hot-water-driven chillers; ~45,000 TR US DC order
  • KRN Heat Exchanger NSE/BSE: KRN — heat-exchanger cores inside chillers/dry coolers; ~50% India DC heat-exchanger target
  • Aeroflex Industries NSE/BSE: AEROFLEX — liquid-cooling skids / fluid network; exclusive India hyperscaler contract
  • Netweb NSE/BSE: NETWEB — Vertiv liquid-cooling partnership and DCIM software
Pools that leak to imports
  • Cold plates, manifolds and precision quick-disconnects — foreign-led; the deepest hardware leak.
  • Two-phase dielectric fluids (fluorochemical) — rebuilding post-3M; the highest-value chemistry leak.
  • CDUs and immersion tanks — foreign OEMs dominate; Indian entrants nascent.
  • Bottleneck nodes: Dielectric & Coolant Fluids, Cold Plates & Manifolds, CDUs, Quick-Disconnects & Fluid Network.
