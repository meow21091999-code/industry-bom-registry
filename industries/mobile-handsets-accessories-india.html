Snapshot

India is the world’s second-largest mobile-phone maker by volume and now its top exporter of finished phones — ₹6.27 lakh cr produced and ₹2.60 lakh cr exported in FY26, with 99.2% of domestically used phones made locally. But the value sits in imported components: domestic value addition is only ~23% against ~38% in China, and non-localised parts (chipset, memory, display, camera) are ~55–60% of a phone’s bill of materials. Scope: India, NSE/BSE.

Process & equipment
  • Assembly spine: SMT/PCBA → sub-assembly (display, camera, battery bonding) → final assembly–test–pack (FATP), across 300+ Indian assembly units.
  • Key machinery: SMT lines (printer, pick-and-place, reflow, AOI, X-ray), display bonding lines (COG/COF), camera-module lines (COB/CSP), injection moulding for enclosures, automated optical/electrical test.
  • Component fabs are only now starting: display module sub-assembly (Dixon–HKC, Minda), camera modules (Dixon Q-Tech), Li-ion cells (ATL; Munoth–Cosmx), PCB (Kaynes, AT&S, BPL) — all ECMS-funded.
Bottlenecks
  • Semiconductors — SoC, memory, PMIC, RF front-end: ~fully imported; ICs are 23.7% of India’s electronics import basket (~$30 bn in FY26). Resilience: structural.
  • Display assembly — no front-end panel fab; ~$4.4 bn imported; module sub-assembly only just starting. Resilience: years.
  • Camera modules — ~25% localised; module sub-assembly beginning (Dixon Q-Tech). Resilience: months–years.
  • Memory price shock — DRAM/NAND +40–50% YoY on AI demand is the live cost driver. Resilience: structural (global).
  • Li-ion cells & chargers — consumer cells ~fully imported; charger leader (Salcomp) is unlisted.
Value pools
  • Final assembly is high-volume, thin-margin (Dixon’s mobile & EMS: ~₹44,257 cr revenue at ~3.5% operating margin) — value leaks upstream.
  • Biggest import pools: chipset/memory/RF (structural), display assembly (~13.5% of BOM) and camera (~10%) — together ~55–60% of BOM, mostly imported.
  • Value that stays home: PCBA (96% DVA), battery packs (95%), vibration motors (80%), USB cables (60%), mechanics (20%), chargers (20%).
  • Localisation wave (ECMS): display module (Dixon–HKC, ~₹5,500–6,000 cr potential), camera modules (Dixon Q-Tech, ₹2,500 cr target), enclosures (Tata, Motherson, Hindalco), PCB, passives, magnets.
  • Policy now pays for localisation: MPMS gives +1.5% for domestic sourcing and +3% for Indian-brand design — the first scheme rewarding components, not just assembly.
  • Accessories (~$7.5 bn, growing) and the ~1 bn-device installed base are the durable domestic pools.
Market & growth
  • India smartphone shipments: ~153 mn CY2025 → forecast ~125–133 mn CY2026 (−13 to −18%), Q3 CY2026 −26% YoY (decade low) — a memory-driven price shock, not lost demand. ASP $282 (2025, +8%); replacement cycle 3.5→4.3 yrs [REPORTED-T2, Omdia/IDC].
  • Production & export (value): mobile production ₹6.27 lakh cr FY26; exports ₹2.60 lakh cr [REPORTED-T1, PIB].
  • Accessories: USD 6.86 bn (2025) → 14.66 bn (2034); 5-yr CAGR ~8.5% [CALCULATED] from USD 4.56 bn (2020) [REPORTED-T3, IMARC].
  • [CONFLICTING DATA] on the handset trajectory (different scopes): IMARC 163.72 mn (2025)→295.92 mn (2034), 6.53% CAGR vs Omdia/IDC/Counterpoint forecasting a 2026 decline.
Watch items
  • MPMS notified Aug-2026 — FY27–31, ₹62,500 cr; first claims from FY27. [LIVE]
  • ECMS outlay raised to ₹40,000 cr (Budget 2026-27); 106 projects; further tranches expected. [CYCLICAL]
  • PLI-LSEM ended 31-Mar-2026 — margin headwind as benefits phase out (Dixon guided ~50–70 bps). [CYCLICAL]
  • Memory price cycle — normalisation not expected before H1-2027 (Omdia). [LIVE]
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play for: SoC/chipset, memory, RF front-end, consumer Li-ion cell, charger/adapter, image sensor, front-end display panel, and test/certification. Nearest listed proxies are the EMS/component integrators (Dixon, Kaynes, Syrma, Centum); the rest are foreign or private (Foxconn, Tata Electronics, Bhagwati, Salcomp, ATL, Munoth).

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Semiconductors — SoC, memory, PMIC, RFThe highest-value parts of every handset are ~fully imported; ICs are 23.7% of India’s electronics import basket (~$30 bn in FY26), and industry estimates put Chinese firms at 70–75% of components used in India. No Indian listed pure-play exists. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Fab, OSAT & designSemicon India (Semicon 2.0 approved 15-Jul-2026), the C2S/TTDF design programmes and commercial OSAT (Kaynes Semicon, Sanand) plus the India Semiconductor Mission build the first domestic silicon base; fabless design (Signalchip, Saankhya/Tejas) is the open prize. The memory sub-assembly (SSD/module) route is also opening.

Risk

Display & camera modulesIndia has no front-end display-panel fab and imports ~$4.4 bn of panels; display assembly is ~13.5% and camera ~10% of a phone’s BOM, and both are only ~25% localised. Together with the chipset/memory, non-localised parts are ~55–60% of BOM. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Module sub-assembly is startingDixon’s 74:26 HKC display-module JV (~₹1,100 cr, 24 mn mobile displays phase-1, commercial Q4FY27) and Minda’s ECMS display-module approval; Dixon Q-Tech camera modules scaling 70→180–190 mn units; TCL CSOT’s Tirupati open-cell plant (India’s only) is on the block. Dixon projects this lifts mobile DVA toward ~40%.

Risk

Memory price shockDRAM/NAND prices rose ~40–50% YoY on AI data-centre demand, forcing handset price hikes, deferring replacement demand and pushing Q3 CY2026 shipments down 26% to a decade low. Normalisation is not expected before H1-2027. Resilience: structural (a global cycle India cannot control).

Opportunity

Premiumisation & value mixHigher prices are shifting mix to premium and lengthening replacement cycles (3.5→4.3 yrs), lifting ASPs ($282, +8%). Finance-led purchasing, trade-in/buyback and accessories attachment (chargers, TWS, cases) are the offsetting demand pools; 4G is re-entering the value band below ₹20,000.

Risk

Cells & chargersConsumer Li-ion cells are ~fully imported (cell = ~75–80% of battery cost); the global leader (ATL) and the Munoth–Cosmx JV are unlisted, and the charger leader (Salcomp) is unlisted. ACC-PLI, aimed at EV cells, has commissioned only 1.4 GWh of 50 GWh. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Cell & charger localisationECMS is funding Li-ion cell lines (ATL Haryana; Munoth, with Cosmx taking 26%), anode material (NPSPL/Epsilon), acetylene black (PCBL) and electrolyte additives (Acutaas). Exide and Amara Raja are building EV/industrial cell capacity. Local cell + charger capacity is the deepest import-substitution prize in the handset BOM.

Risk

Shallow component depth & thin marginsAssembly is high-volume and thin-margin (Dixon mobile & EMS ~3.5% operating margin), and PLI-LSEM’s expiry removes a cushion. India is a final-market supplier (US/UAE), not embedded in dense East-Asian component trade, so value addition stays ~23%. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

MPMS + ECMS reward depthThe ₹62,500 cr MPMS pays +1.5% for domestic sourcing and +3% for Indian-brand design/R&D; ECMS (raised to ₹40,000 cr) funds 106 component projects. Together they shift policy from assembly to components — the structural fix for both margin and DVA.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the memory (DRAM/NAND) and SoC dies inside every phone, and one layer up, the display panels, image sensors and Li-ion cells that India still cannot make at scale — plus the precision manufacturing equipment (SMT, bonding, injection) used to build the modules.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Final assembly is high-volume, thin-margin (~3.5% operating margin at Dixon) — value leaks upstream to components and silicon.
  • Value that stays home: PCBA (96% DVA), battery packs (95%), vibration motors (80%), USB cables (60%), mechanics (20%), chargers (20%).
  • Localisation wave (ECMS) is pulling display/camera modules, PCB, passives and magnets onshore — Dixon projects mobile DVA toward ~40%.
  • Accessories (~$7.5 bn, ~8.5% CAGR) and the ~1 bn-device installed base are durable domestic pools.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Dixon Technologies NSE/BSE: DIXON — largest smartphone maker by volume (19% CY2025); FY26 revenue ₹48,893 cr (+26%), mobile & EMS ₹44,257 cr; 32–33 mn smartphones; camera (Q-Tech) & display (HKC) modules
  • Amber Enterprises NSE/BSE: AMBER — entering handsets via Oppo/OnePlus/realme JV (8 mn units yr-1, to 15–16 mn)
  • PG Electroplast NSE/BSE: PGEL — mobile mfg (Pune/Greater Noida, ~5 mn units/yr) within an EMS/ODM platform (FY26 revenue ₹5,288 cr)
  • Kaynes Technology NSE/BSE: KAYNES · Syrma SGS SYRMA · BPL · Centum CENTUM — PCB, flex, coils, passives, filters under ECMS
  • Uno Minda UNOMINDA (display module) · Motherson MOTHERSON & Hindalco HINDALCO (enclosures) · Permanent Magnets & PCBL (magnets, cell materials)
  • Tata Elxsi TATAELXSI — handset design & R&D services
Pools that leak to imports
  • No listed pure-play: SoC/chipset, memory, RF front-end, image sensor, front-end display panel, consumer Li-ion cell, charger/adapter, and test/certification.
  • Bottleneck nodes: Core Silicon & RF, Display & Touch Module, Camera Module, Battery Cell, PCB/PCBA.
  • Nearest proxies: EMS/component integrators (Dixon, Kaynes, Syrma, Centum); foreign/private players hold the rest (Foxconn, Tata Electronics, Bhagwati, Salcomp, ATL, Munoth, TCL CSOT).
