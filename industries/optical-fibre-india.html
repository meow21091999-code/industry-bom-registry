00 Digest short summary · full detail in the sections below
Snapshot

Optical fibre (drawing) — turning preform into 125 µm fibre. India has large draw capacity but depends on imported preform. Scope: India, NSE/BSE.

Process & equipment
  • A draw tower and furnace pull fibre from preform; dual-layer acrylate coating, proof testing and colouring/ribbonising follow.
  • Draw towers, furnaces and winders are imported; consumables include UV resins, inks and helium.
Bottlenecks
  • Preform — the upstream choke point (65–75% imported; years).
  • Draw-tower equipment imported (no listed equipment pure-play).
  • Specialty / bend-insensitive grades (G.657) and precision ceramic ferrules dominated by China/Japan.
  • Consumables and process gases imported.
Value pools
  • Value concentrates in preform, not in drawing.
  • STL is the only fully integrated Indian player.
  • Fibre drawing is competitive and lower-margin.
  • Localisation: integrated fibre, specialty grades, ferrules.
Watch items
  • Anti-dumping duty on single-mode fibre (to ~Aug-2028).
  • HFCL / Finolex fibre-capacity ramp.
  • BharatNet Phase-III and FTTH demand.
  • Data-centre fibre pull.
Coverage gaps

No listed pure-play: draw-tower equipment, ceramic ferrules, specialty grades, coating resins/process gases — nearest: STL/HFCL (fibre), foreign equipment makers.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Preform — the upstream choke point65–75% of preform is imported (China, Japan); domestic capacity meets only ~25–35% of demand, and >70% of the value-chain profit pool sits in this one step.

Opportunity

India’s second & third integrated playersSTL is already integrated; Finolex has commissioned captive preform and HFCL is building 300→600 MTPA (₹580 cr → ~₹1,800 cr total) by 2028–29.

Risk

Draw-tower equipmentTowers, induction furnaces and winders are imported (Nextrom, SG Controls); there is no Indian listed equipment pure-play and capex lead times are long.

Opportunity

Local equipment buildSIMCO and Machine & Controls build towers, colouring lines and FRP lines in India — an under-owned, pick-and-shovel play on the fibre capex cycle.

Risk

Specialty & bend-insensitive gradesG.657 and specialty fibre are harder to draw and were historically imported; consistency and yield separate winners from the pack.

Opportunity

ADD protectionThe 5-year anti-dumping duty (Aug-2023) covers dispersion-unshifted single-mode fibre including bend-insensitive G.657 — a premium-mix tailwind for domestic drawers.

Risk

Precision ceramic ferrulesSub-micron zirconia ferrules are dominated by China and Japan; India largely grinds imported blanks, so the connector end of the chain stays import-dependent.

Opportunity

Make-in-India ferrulesDomestic firms are moving from grinding to full powder→sinter→grind manufacturing — a small but strategically placed import-substitution niche.

Risk

Capacity vs consumption~100 mn fkm of capacity against ~46 mn fkm of domestic use makes India export-dependent and exposed to the global fibre price cycle and dumping.

Opportunity

Global demand cycleAI data centres, 5G densification and BharatNet 2.0 have fibre makers sold out through 2026; India is already a top-2 global producer and a major exporter.

Risk

Consumables & process gasesUV coating resin, colouring inks and helium are imported — small line items with outsized supply-chain risk if logistics tighten.

Opportunity

Chemistry localisationCoating and ink chemistry is a genuine import-substitution gap; industrial-gas distribution in India is already deep.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the preform itself — India draws world-scale fibre from imported glass. A step that generates over 70% of the value-chain profit pool is controlled by fewer than ten firms worldwide, and only one Indian company is fully backward-integrated into it.
