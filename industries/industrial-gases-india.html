Snapshot

India's industrial-gas industry sells oxygen, nitrogen and argon made by cryogenic air separation, plus hydrogen, CO2, acetylene, helium and specialty blends — delivered three ways: on-site build-own-operate plants (48% of value), merchant liquid by tanker, and packaged cylinders. It is the invisible input to steel, refining, pharma, solar and now semiconductors.

Process & equipment
  • Spine: filter → main air compressor → molecular-sieve prepurification → cold box (brazed aluminium plate-fin exchangers) → turboexpander → double-column cryogenic distillation → argon column → liquid storage → tanker or cylinder filling.
  • Hardware that matters: oil-free centrifugal compressors, turboexpanders, PFHEs, distillation columns, cryogenic pumps, vacuum-brazing furnaces, helium leak detectors.
  • Non-cryogenic routes: PSA/VPSA for small N₂/O₂; SMR and electrolysis for H₂; recovery plants for CO₂.
Bottlenecks
  • Process IP and cold-box hardware — design ownership sits with Linde, Air Products, Air Liquide; no Indian turboexpander or PFHE capability. Resilience: years.
  • Helium — 100% imported, no substitute, 7–10 days of inventory. Resilience: structural.
  • Electronic specialty gases — ~25 of ~50 gases will stay import-only. Resilience: years.
  • Power price — ~24% of supplier revenue; the single largest controllable cost. Resilience: weeks.
  • Rare gases (Ne/Kr/Xe) — Indian ASUs mostly lack recovery skids; import from China/Ukraine/Russia. Resilience: structural.
Value pools
  • On-site BOO contracts are the annuity: 15–20-year take-or-pay, ~23% of the leader's revenue but the most durable cash flow.
  • Merchant liquid carries the pricing power when regional supply is tight; distance-limited to roughly 200–300 km.
  • Specialty and electronic gases earn the highest margin per cubic metre and are the least localised — the open white space.
  • Cryogenic equipment is India's genuine export win: INOX India earns ~59% of revenue abroad at ~24% EBITDA margin.
  • Leaking to imports: helium, rare gases, ~38 specialty gases, cold-box internals, turboexpanders and large centrifugal compressors.
Market & growth
  • Value (nominal): 6.7% 5-yr CAGR (2020–25), 6.9% 3-yr, +7.6% last year — [CALCULATED] from a US$1,020m→1,410m series.
  • Volume: 4.4% 5-yr CAGR, +4.2% last year (10.0→12.4 mn tonnes) — so roughly a third of value growth is price and mix.
  • Forecasts cluster at 6–7.5% to 2028–31; published market sizes conflict on scope from US$1.31bn to US$3.81bn. [CONFLICTING DATA]
Watch items
  • Qatar Ras Laffan helium force majeure (Mar-2026); repairs guided at 3–5 years. [LIVE]
  • Gas Cylinder (Third Amendment) Rules 2026 notified 21-Sep-2026 — CNG/CBG mobile refuelling units. [REPORTED-T1]
  • Linde India's SEBI / Supreme Court related-party and business-allocation matter — NSE valuation report dated 16-Mar-2026, impact indeterminate. [LIVE]
  • INOX AP's ₹500 cr Dholera electronic-specialty-gas hub, targeted live 12–18 months from Oct-2025. [LIVE]
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play in helium, electronic specialty gases, rare-gas recovery or ASU process design — the four highest-value import nodes are unlisted or foreign.
  • The market leader, INOX Air Products (₹3,048 cr revenue, ~50% operating margin), is a 50:50 INOX–Air Products JV and is not listed.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

ASU process IP, cold box and turboexpanderIndia runs thousands of tonnes per day of ASU capacity but Linde, Air Products and Air Liquide own the design of almost all of it. There is no Indian cryogenic turboexpander capability and no brazed aluminium plate-fin heat exchanger capability; domestic designers reach only about 5,000 Nm³/h against the 2,000–5,250 TPD units built at steel mills. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Fabricate-here, license-the-processLinde India already fabricates columns up to 7 m wide, 45 m long and 170 t at Taratala, and INOX India builds large cryogenic tanks on its own technology. The realistic play is cold-box and column fabrication plus erection for licensed designs, then turboexpander and PFHE joint ventures — a pool worth the rotating-equipment share of every new ASU.

Risk

Helium — 100% imported, no substituteIndia consumed about 3.4 million cubic metres in 2025 with zero domestic production; over half traditionally comes from Qatar, whose Ras Laffan complex suffered force majeure in March 2026 and is guided at 3–5 years to repair. Inventories are 7–10 days because of boil-off, and prices rose 35–50% within weeks. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Multi-origin contracts, recovery and recyclingStallion India's January-2026 tie-up with Sharjah Oxygen for RAS Gases product is the template for a listed helium-import franchise. Zero-boil-off MRI technology, on-site reclamation and closed-loop recovery at fabs turn scarcity into a service business; domestic extraction from West Bengal and Jharkhand gas is below the ~0.2% economic threshold and 5–10 years away.

Risk

Electronic specialty gasesA fab needs more than 50 ultra-high-purity gases; INOX Air Products makes about 12 and is adding 10, leaving roughly 25 that will stay import-only. Purity runs to parts per billion, the gases are toxic, flammable or corrosive, and no fab can afford a millisecond of downtime. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

The one node with a live policy subsidy and a live buildSemicon 2.0 (Cabinet, July 2026) extends incentives to semiconductor materials, chemicals and gases; INOX AP's ₹500 cr Dholera hub is under construction. Purification, packaging, analysis and audit-readiness — not molecule synthesis — are the bankable first steps, and India's first UHP nitrous oxide plant already exists.

Risk

Power priceElectricity is roughly 24% of supplier revenue and the dominant cash cost of an ASU; Linde India spent ₹129.7 cr on power and fuel against ₹694.4 cr of revenue in Q1 FY27. A tariff revision or a lost open-access approval moves merchant margins immediately. Resilience: weeks for on-site contracts (pass-through), months for merchant.

Opportunity

Captive renewables and open accessLinde India has taken stakes in renewable SPVs (Avaada, FPEL Surya, Zenataris) purely to buy power; Air Water's Chennai plant covers ~40% of its electricity with solar. Green-power-linked ASUs become both a cost hedge and a selling point for green-hydrogen and green-steel customers.

Risk

Rare gases (neon, krypton, xenon)These come out of the same cold box, but Indian ASUs mostly do not have recovery skids, so the country imports from China, Ukraine and Russia — three jurisdictions with active export-control or conflict risk. Xenon and krypton are ~36% and ~67% semiconductor-use gases globally. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Retrofit recovery onto every new ASURare-gas recovery is a small incremental capex on a ₹750 cr plant and yields the highest value per cubic metre in the industry. With roughly 20 large ASUs announced or under construction, attaching recovery skids is a near-term, high-return localisation play — currently with no Indian supplier.

Risk

Demand concentration in steelSteel and metals are the largest end-use, and on-site contracts are anchored on SAIL, Tata Steel, JSW and AM/NS expansions. A steel capex pause idles multi-thousand-tonne plants that cannot be relocated. Resilience: months–years (contract-backed, take-or-pay).

Opportunity

Diversify into solar and semiconductorsINOX AP already supplies Tata's TP Solar, ReNew and Waaree, and Air Water reads semiconductor nitrogen demand as the next leg. Each new demand cluster — PV, OSAT, display, food cold chain, data-centre cooling — is a chance to re-price merchant gas upward.

Deepest hidden import dependency: not the gas — the machine that makes the gas. India can fabricate tanks, tankers and cylinders competitively and even exports them, but the cold box internals (PFHE), the turboexpander and the large oil-free centrifugal compressor that decide an ASU's efficiency and lead time are all imported, as is the process design itself. A 2,150 TPD plant is ₹750 cr of which the value-dense core is foreign; the same is true of the specialty-gas packages that a fab needs.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • On-site BOO contracts — 15–20-year take-or-pay, power cost passed through, capital-heavy; ~23% of INOX AP's revenue but the most durable cash flow in the industry.
  • Merchant liquid — ~67% of the leader's revenue; pricing power when regional supply is tight, capped by a ~200–300 km economic delivery radius.
  • Specialty, electronic and rare gases — highest margin per cubic metre, lowest domestic share, and now subsidised by Semicon 2.0.
  • Cryogenic equipment — India's real export franchise: INOX India at 59% exports and ~24% adjusted EBITDA margin.
  • Packaged cylinders — ~10% of leader revenue; fragmented, last-mile, thin margin but sticky.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Linde India NSE: LINDEINDIA — ₹2,530.64 cr FY26 revenue, ₹548.97 cr PAT; the only listed entity with both gas production and ASU project engineering
  • Ellenbarrie Industrial Gases NSE: ELLEN — ₹341.58 cr FY26 income, ₹104.4 cr PAT; the only listed gas pure-play
  • INOX India NSE: INOXINDIA — ₹1,632 cr FY26 revenue, ₹388 cr adj EBITDA, ₹1,514 cr backlog
  • Everest Kanto NSE: EKC and Time Technoplast NSE: TIMETECHNO — cylinders and Type IV composite cascades
  • Stallion India Fluorochemicals NSE: STALLION — refrigerants plus the only listed helium-sourcing arrangement
Pools that leak to imports
  • Helium (100%), neon/krypton/xenon (mostly), and ~25 of ~50 semiconductor gases.
  • Cold-box internals, turboexpanders and large centrifugal compressors — no Indian supplier.
  • Process-design royalties to Linde, Air Products and Air Liquide on nearly every large ASU in the country.
