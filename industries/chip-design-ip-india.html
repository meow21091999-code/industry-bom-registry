Snapshot

Chip design and IP is the layer where silicon is conceived and proved before any factory touches it: EDA software, licensed IP blocks, and the design services and fabless companies that turn a specification into a GDSII file. India holds about a fifth of the world’s design engineers and almost none of the tools or core IP — that asymmetry is the whole story.

Process & equipment
  • The spine is a software flow: architecture → RTL → verification (60–70% of engineering effort) → synthesis → place-and-route → sign-off → GDSII, then tape-out.
  • No factory is needed to design. The physical kit is EDA licence servers, emulation hardware (Cadence Palladium holds 55–60% of advanced-node emulation) and a compute farm.
  • Realisation needs a foundry or MPW shuttle plus an OSAT; India’s first fab (Tata–PSMC Dholera) is guided to 2028, so essentially every Indian design tapes out abroad today.
Bottlenecks
  • EDA is a three-vendor chokepoint — Synopsys holds >90% of static timing sign-off, Siemens Calibre >85% of physical verification. Resilience: YEARS.
  • Processor IP is a tollbooth: Arm collected US$2.61 bn of royalties in FY2026. RISC-V is the only credible escape route.
  • Capital gates the proof-of-concept: US$3–5 mn for a POC, US$20–25 mn at 28 nm, US$750 mn+ at 4/2 nm.
  • Demand, not capability, binds: 24 DLI projects and 25 tape-outs, but buyers must requalify to switch vendors.
  • Talent is priced by the captives — ~60% of senior VLSI engineers sit in Bengaluru.
Value pools
  • Value sits upstream: EDA + IP is an US$18 bn pool with software margins, and the NSE/BSE universe has zero exposure to it.
  • India’s captured pool is design services — MosChip ₹467 cr (FY25), Sasken ₹1,113 cr (FY26), Cyient Semiconductors US$25.7 mn — thin-margin and headcount-linked.
  • Cyient Semiconductors lost US$11.2 mn EBIT on US$25.7 mn revenue in FY26: the cost of moving from services to owned silicon.
  • Owned product (ASSP/turnkey) is the only Indian pool with gross margin above 40%; Cyient guides 40%+ GM at ~4× revenue by FY31.
  • OSAT and packaging (Kaynes, CG Power, SPEL) capture the physical end; IP royalties and EDA renewals leak abroad entirely.
Market & growth
  • No India-only chip-design-and-IP revenue series exists — [UNAVAILABLE]. Nearest scoped proxy: India ER&D services, semiconductor & electronics vertical, 12.85% CAGR 2026–31 [REPORTED-T2].
  • India ER&D services overall: US$147.3 bn (2026) → US$238.6 bn (2031), 10.14% nominal CAGR [REPORTED-T2]. Value, not volume.
  • Global EDA + IP: US$18 bn (2025) → US$28–30 bn (2030) [REPORTED-T3], ~9–11% [CALCULATED]; semiconductor IP alone US$9.3 bn (2025) → US$18.6 bn (2032) at 10.2% [REPORTED-T2].
  • Volume is not measurable here: there is no Indian design-start or tape-out unit series. Tape-outs (25 under DLI) are the physical proxy.
Watch items
  • DLI 2.0 contours unresolved: ~₹5,000 cr outlay, pari-passu matching capital, CDAC IP co-ownership, 3× clawback if IP leaves India [LIVE, as of Sep-2026].
  • Semicon 2.0 notified 31-Aug-2026 — ₹1.27 lakh cr across six pillars including design.
  • Tata–PSMC Dholera: commercial production guided 2028, 28 nm by 2029.
  • C2i Semiconductors sold to Infineon (Aug-2026) — the first DLI exit, and the first real test of the IP-retention clause.
Coverage gaps

No NSE/BSE exposure to EDA, sign-off, emulation, interface IP, verification IP, foundry tape-out or design compute. Listed exposure is design services (9 companies), turnkey ASIC (2), IP-adjacent (2) and OSAT (3).

01 System Overview Level 0 → six subsystems
clickable BOM tree in section 03

Chip design is not a factory industry — it is a software-and-IP industry with a physical tail. Level 0 is the designed chip plus the reusable blocks and tools that make it. Six subsystems follow: A Design Creation (architecture, RTL, verification, physical design, analog layout); B EDA Tools (front-end simulation and synthesis, back-end place-and-route and sign-off, emulation); C Semiconductor IP (processor cores, interface and foundation blocks, verification IP, analog and power IP); D Design Compute & PDKs (licence servers, compute farms, foundry process design kits and standard-cell libraries); E Design Services & Talent (offshore design centres, captive GCCs, upskilling); and F Realisation (foundry or MPW tape-out, OSAT packaging, ATE test and silicon validation).

India’s position is inverted relative to the value chain: it is strong in E and competent in A, weak and largely absent in B and C, and only now building F. That is why the industry’s economics look like services rather than like software, and why the policy debate (DLI 2.0) is really about who owns the IP.

02 Value-Chain Ladder & Supplier Tiers foundational inputs → aftermarket · Tier-4 → OEM
Tier 4 · Foundational
ISA specifications and architecture licences (Arm architecture, x86, RISC-V International) · EDA mathematics (solvers, timing and device models, formal engines) · foundry PDKs and design rules · standard-cell and memory-compiler libraries · compute (CPU/GPU farms, licence servers) · design talent [STRUCTURAL]
Tier 3 · Tools & reusable blocks
EDA point tools and flows (simulation, formal, synthesis, P&R, DRC/LVS, STA, power) · processor IP (Cortex, Neoverse, SiFive, Andes, InCore) · interface and foundation IP (PCIe, CXL, UCIe, HBM PHY, DDR, SerDes, USB) · verification IP · analog and power IP · emulation and prototyping hardware [STRUCTURAL]
Tier 2 · Design creation
Micro-architecture and RTL · functional verification and DFT (60–70% of engineering effort) · synthesis and timing closure · physical design and sign-off · analog and mixed-signal layout · firmware, BSP and post-silicon validation India’s core strength
Tier 1 · Productisation
Turnkey ASIC (concept to working silicon) · fabless product companies and ASSPs · chiplet and SiP integration · package co-design · silicon bring-up and characterisation · customer qualification the margin layer India is trying to reach
OEM · System
System companies and OEMs that specify custom silicon — telecom and networking, automotive, industrial, surveillance, metering, defence, data-centre and consumer · design wins are decided here, not in the fab
Realisation
Foundry and MPW shuttle (TSMC, GlobalFoundries, UMC, SCL Mohali 180 nm) → OSAT (assembly, packaging, final test) → ATE test and qualification → certification (AEC-Q100, ISO 26262, security Common Criteria)
Aftermarket · Life cycle
Respins and ECOs · IP re-licensing and per-unit royalties (the annuity that pays for the next design) · EDA subscription renewals · design reuse and derivative SKUs · failure analysis and obsolescence management

Tier ladder follows the discrete-manufacturing form, with one adaptation: Tier 4 here is standards, mathematics and PDKs rather than mined material — the “raw material” of a designed chip is specification and physics, not commodity input.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

EDA is a three-vendor chokepointSynopsys, Cadence and Siemens EDA take over 85% of an US$18 bn design-software-and-IP market; at advanced nodes Synopsys holds >90% of static timing analysis and Siemens Calibre >85% of physical verification. A US licence requirement on EDA sales to China in 2025 lasted about six weeks before being rescinded — proof that the tool layer is a switch a government can throw. Resilience: YEARS.

Opportunity

Tool access is already subsidised — the gap is commercial EDADLI has given EDA access to 103 fabless companies and logged ~2.25 crore tool-hours. India has no commercial EDA vendor at any node. A credible play is not “replace Synopsys” but domain-specific automation — the space InCore attacks from YAML-to-RTL rather than RTL-to-GDSII, and where AI-assisted design-flow startups are now forming.

Risk

Processor IP is a tollboothArm took US$4.92 bn of revenue in FY2026, US$2.61 bn of it royalties, and its architecture sits in roughly half the compute of the top cloud firms’ CPUs. An Indian fabless company that rents its core pays a royalty on every unit shipped, in perpetuity, in dollars. Resilience: MONTHS to switch ISA, YEARS to switch ecosystem.

Opportunity

RISC-V is the one open door, and India is inside itRISC-V reached roughly 25% penetration and ~20 bn cumulative cores by end-2025, with the commercial RISC-V market projected at 24.3% CAGR to 2032. India already has silicon-proven cores (InCore’s Azurite/Calcite, Mindgrove’s Secure IoT at 28 nm) — the missing piece is design wins and a royalty base, not the core itself.

Risk

Tape-out capital and foundry access gate everythingA proof-of-concept chip costs US$3–5 mn; a 28 nm product US$20–25 mn; 4/2 nm US$750 mn+. Foundries charge a premium for small volumes on 12–18 month lead times, and a leading-edge respin costs US$50–100 mn. DLI caps design support at ₹15 cr per application — roughly one POC.

Opportunity

MPW shuttles and SCL are the cheap on-rampMulti-project-wafer shuttles and SCL Mohali’s 180 nm line already carried 254 student and startup tape-outs (175 at SCL, 79 abroad). India’s first commercial fab (Tata–PSMC, Dholera) brings 28 nm onshore by 2029 — a genuine domestic tape-out path for the first time.

Risk

Demand, not capability, is the binding constraint24 DLI projects and 25 tape-outs have produced capability without buyers: switching from Qualcomm, NXP or ST means retesting and requalification for the customer. C2i Semiconductors, a DLI beneficiary, was sold to Infineon within two years because the Indian ecosystem could not take its product to a global buyer. Resilience: MONTHS–YEARS.

Opportunity

Regulated import substitution is the beachheadIndia restricted uncertified internet-connected CCTV cameras on security grounds, creating room for ~2 mn “designed-in-India” chips a year; metering, PoS terminals, drones and defence add captive demand where a local vendor has a compliance advantage rather than a price one. Mindgrove is targeting exactly this with ~6 clients in biometrics, CCTV and metering.

Risk

Policy design is itself a riskDLI 2.0 proposes pari-passu matching of VC money, CDAC co-ownership of IP on supported designs, and a 3× clawback if IP moves offshore. Only five or six VC funds are active in Indian semicon, so a matching requirement pushes startups toward the narrowest possible capital pool. [LIVE]

Opportunity

The capital gap is the addressable marketIndian chip-design VC averaged US$4.35 mn a round against a US$3–5 mn POC cost; global chip-design startup investment was US$12.2 bn in 2025 against India’s 0.2% share. Any structure that de-risks the first silicon — royalty financing, milestone grants, anchor customers — converts a policy problem into a financing product.

Risk

Talent is priced by the captivesGlobal GCCs offer stock and salaries a startup cannot match; ~60% of India’s senior VLSI workforce sits in Bengaluru, and the sector needs ~85,000 more engineers by 2032. A design engineer lost to a captive is a schedule slip, not a hire.

Opportunity

Training capacity already exists and is underusedC2S has trained 67,000–68,000 students across 315 institutions with 254 tape-outs, and DLI logged ~2.25 crore EDA tool-hours. The bottleneck is conversion into product companies, not classroom capacity.

06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Tools and IP capture the economics of the whole industry without taking its risk. EDA vendors earn 85%+ gross margins and grow ~13% a year against ~7% semiconductor R&D growth; Arm converts about 95% of revenue into gross profit on a design-once-licence-forever model.
  • Design services capture revenue but not margin. They are headcount-linked, priced per engineer-month, and exposed to the customer’s R&D budget cycle — which is exactly what hit FY26.
  • Owned silicon is where the multiple lives. A fabless ASSP business earns 40%+ gross margin; a design-services business earns 12–25% EBITDA at best.
  • OSAT captures the physical tail — real revenue, contract-manufacturing margins, valued as capacity rather than capability.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • MosChip Technologies NSE/BSE: MOSCHIP — [REPORTED] India’s first listed fabless company: design services, IP services and turnkey ASIC; TSMC Design Centre Alliance partner, 200+ SoC tape-outs; revenue ₹466.84 cr FY25 (+59%), EBITDA 12.8%.
  • Sasken Technologies NSE/BSE: SASKEN — [REPORTED] Chip-to-cognition engineering: analog and mixed-signal design, SoC verification, validation; revenue ₹1,113 cr FY26 (+102%, M&A-driven), EBITDA 7.9%.
  • Cyient NSE/BSE: CYIENT — [REPORTED] Cyient Semiconductors is a wholly owned fabless product company: US$25.7 mn FY26 revenue, EBIT −US$11.2 mn; 74% of Kinetic Technologies adds ~US$40 mn; targets 40%+ gross margin and ~4× revenue by FY31.
  • L&T Technology Services NSE/BSE: LTTS — [REPORTED] Complex SoC design, turnkey SoCs at 3/4/5 nm for hyperscalers; US$1.23 bn FY26 revenue, EBIT 14.5%; silicon is a minority of a diversified portfolio.
  • Tejas Networks NSE/BSE: TEJASNET — [REPORTED] Owns Saankhya Labs (unlisted, non-material): SDR chipsets and an indigenous multi-core DSP architecture — the closest listed exposure to Indian processor IP.
  • CG Power NSE/BSE: CGPOWER — [REPORTED] Unlisted subsidiary Axiro designs RF/mmWave and GaN front-end IP in India; a minor segment of a power-equipment company.
Pools that leak to imports
  • EDA licences — 100% imported; even the subsidised tools under DLI are foreign licences.
  • Processor and interface IP — Arm, Synopsys DesignWare and Cadence IP hold >75% of global IP revenue; India’s share of that pool is negligible.
  • Foundry and mask — every Indian design tapes out abroad; masks and wafers are dollar-denominated and bought at small-volume premia.
  • Emulation and prototyping hardware — Cadence Palladium holds 55–60% of advanced-node emulation; no Indian vendor.
