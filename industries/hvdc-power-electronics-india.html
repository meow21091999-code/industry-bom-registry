Snapshot

HVDC converter stations and grid power electronics — the AC↔DC interface that moves bulk renewable power over distances where AC stops paying. India runs ~19,375 ckm / 33,500 MW, with a 32.3 GW NEP pipeline (14.5 GW under implementation) — but the pipeline is being reshaped: the 5 GW Leh–Kaithal HVDC leg was dropped for a ₹31,428 cr HVAC scheme on 29-Oct-2026, and a 6 GW ±800 kV VSC bipole entered through the ₹68,816 cr 1150 kV corridor.

Process & equipment
  • Converter transformers are custom-built, individually type-tested and 18–36 months out; valve halls are erected and tested on site.
  • Valves are thyristor (LCC) or IGBT/MMC (VSC); the 800 kV VSC technology cleared by NCT in 2026 shifts new schemes toward IGBT content and a harder control-software layer.
  • HVDC cable needs DC-rated XLPE compound and pre-moulded joints — neither made in India; CPRI HV test bays gate every new entrant.
Bottlenecks
  • Valves and converter control code — a 3-player global oligopoly; resilience: years.
  • Power semiconductors — no Indian high-power fab; SiCSem's SEZ approval (15-Sep-2026) and CG Semi's OSAT do not change that.
  • Converter transformers — 18–36 month leads; global OEM books full to 2030.
  • EHV bushings and insulators — 2–3 global suppliers; Chinese-linked relief expires 23-Jun-2028.
  • HVDC working capital — Hitachi's H1 FY27 operating cash flow fell to ₹91 cr from ₹874 cr on contract assets.
Value pools
  • Converter stations — Hitachi Energy + BHEL on Khavda–Nagpur and Bhadla–Fatehpur; GE Vernova took Barmer-II (~US$1.5 bn, booked Q3 FY27).
  • Converter transformers — the single largest equipment item (~40% of project capex by the entrant's own account); TARIL holds the first Indian-origin order.
  • Valves — Hitachi Chennai and GE Vernova's Chennai LCC/VSC line; the margin stays with the parent's IP.
  • Cables — Sterlite, KEI and Polycab build EHV capacity; DC compound still leaks abroad.
  • Developers — PGCIL, Adani and IndiGrid hold 25–35 year annuities; equipment margin is where the value sits.

  • Chinese-linked procurement exemption lapses 23-Jun-2028 [REPORTED-T1: DoE order, 24-Jun-2026].
  • Ladakh HVAC award (₹31,428 cr, 48- and 60-month schedules) and the 1150 kV corridor award (₹68,816 cr, 72 months) — both TBCB, both unfloated.
  • Barmer-II booking in Q3 FY27 and the first 800 kV VSC tender, expected 2027.
Coverage gaps

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Converter valves & control codeA 3-player global oligopoly (Hitachi / GE / Siemens) owns the valve and the proprietary control software; Indian assembly is not sovereignty. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Domestic valve linesBHEL supplies thyristor valves from Bengaluru for Khavda–Nagpur and Bhadla–Fatehpur; Hitachi Energy assembles HVDC valves in Chennai and is adding a line at Chengalpattu (₹1,000 cr, Jul-2026); GE Vernova is building an LCC/VSC valve line in Chennai (early-2027). Local content 30% → 60% by FY35.

Risk

Biddable schemes, not demandThe binding constraint has moved from demand to procurement: the 5 GW Leh (Pang–Kaithal) HVDC scheme was abandoned on 29-Oct-2026 after equipment makers raised technical, design and contractual objections to a 4,700 m, −35 °C site, and was replaced by a ₹31,428 cr HVAC scheme. No HVDC terminal tender has been floated since Barmer. Resilience: months to years, because the substitute is a different technology, not another supplier.

Opportunity

The replacement work is bigger and more domesticThe HVAC substitute is a ₹25,226 cr Part-I package (400 kV GIS pooling station with four synchronous condensers, 273 km quad line) plus ₹6,202 cr Part-II — content that Indian OEMs can build today, and it pulls synchronous condensers into the same demand pool as STATCOMs. Meanwhile the ₹68,816 cr 1150 kV corridor adds a 6 GW ±800 kV VSC bipole that would otherwise have needed synchronous condensers at Bhadla-IV.

Risk

Power semiconductorsNo Indian fab for high-power thyristors / IGBT / SiC; ~60–65% of converter silicon imported; a Chinese export control would bite directly.

Opportunity

OSAT today, SiC fab nextCG Semi's OSAT (Sanand) began commercial production 4-Jul-2026 but its G2 plant is now guided to commercial output in 2028-29; SiCSem's ₹3,406 cr SiC unit was cleared by the Falta SEZ approval committee on 15-Sep-2026 (91.2 m SiC MOSFETs/yr planned); CDIL is expanding high-power discrete capacity; MeitY + MoP + POWERGRID run an indigenous SiC-MOSFET drive for VSC-HVDC. None of these is HVDC-class thyristor wafer capacity, which is the actual valve input.

Risk

Converter transformersCustom-built, individually tested, 18–36 month lead times; only a handful of Indian plants are qualified for HVDC-grade units. Global OEM order books are full to 2030.

Opportunity

A widening Indian clubTARIL became the first Indian-origin private maker to win an HVDC converter-transformer order (₹53.33 cr, Dec-2025) and is scaling 40,000 → 75,000 MVA; BHEL, Hitachi and CG hold capability.

Risk

HVDC cables (XLPE DC)The DC-rated XLPE insulation compound and the joints/terminations have no domestic production; heavy import reliance, and no domestic subsea cable capability.

Opportunity

Greenfield cable plantsSterlite Electric (Vadodara, up to 400–550 kV HVDC/HVAC), KEI (Sanand Phase-II EHV by Mar-2027; HVDC-ready, full spectrum by FY27) and Polycab (EHV by 2027) are building the domestic base.

Risk

EHV bushings, insulators & CRGOBushings have only 2–3 suppliers worldwide; CRGO electrical steel runs a ~30% national shortfall. These gate converter-transformer and switchgear output.

Opportunity

Import relief + localisationThe Centre granted a two-year exemption (Jul-2026) for select Chinese transformer-component suppliers (bushings, insulators) with India plants, to hold schedules; TARIL and others are backward-integrating CRGO processing.

Risk

Working capital in contract assetsHVDC is billed on milestones, so the equipment supplier funds the build: Hitachi Energy India's H1 FY27 operating cash flow fell to ₹91 cr from ₹874 cr as the two HVDC projects sat in contract assets. GE Vernova T&D, with no HVDC revenue yet, generated ₹10.1 bn of H1 cash — the contrast is the whole risk.

Opportunity

Who can afford to hold the assetBalance-sheet strength becomes a competitive weapon: the two suppliers with net cash can carry three HVDC builds at once, and developers (PGCIL, Adani, IndiGrid) earn a regulated or TBCB annuity on the same spend — the only layer that converts HVDC capex into predictable cash.

Risk

Skilled manpower & test capacityHVDC commissioning engineers and HV type-test bays (CPRI-led) are named binding constraints; capacity can be bought, people and test slots cannot.

Opportunity

Qualification moatScarce, qualification-gated roles and NABL/CPRI test slots carry durable pricing power; Hitachi's GTIC and GE Vernova's Noida lab deepen the talent base.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the machine tools, vacuum/oil-processing plants and HV test bays used to build HVDC converter transformers and valves themselves — procurement lead times of 2–4 years for high-end manufacturing systems, and effectively the true gate on how fast India can localise.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Converter stations — the two live consortia are billing: Bhadla–Fatehpur ~10% of contract value recognised, Khavda–Nagpur ~4–5%; Barmer-II (~US$1.5 bn) books in Q3 FY27.
  • Valves — Hitachi Chennai assembly plus the Chengalpattu line; GE Vernova Chennai LCC/VSC line (early-2027). The margin follows the parent's IP, not the Indian line.
  • Converter transformers — TARIL holds the first Indian-origin order (₹53.33 cr, Dec-2025) and prices the node at ~40% of project capex; capacity 40,000 → 75,000 MVA.
  • Cables — Sterlite, KEI and Polycab build EHV capacity; the DC compound still leaks abroad.
  • Developers — PGCIL, Adani and IndiGrid earn the 25–35 year annuity on the same capex.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Hitachi Energy India NSE/BSE: POWERINDIA — Over half of India's HVDC links; co-supplying Khavda–Nagpur; Q2 FY27 revenue +63.5%, operational EBITDA 16.5%, record backlog ₹33,335 cr
  • GE Vernova T&D India NSE/BSE: GVT&D — ~23% of India operational HVDC; L1 on Barmer-II 6 GW LCC terminals (Sep-2026); Q2 FY27 EBITDA 27.1%, backlog ₹21,443 cr, no HVDC revenue before FY28
  • Siemens Energy India NSE/BSE: ENRIN — One of three qualified HVDC suppliers in India; first VSC link
  • BHEL NSE/BSE: BHEL — India's first indigenous ±800 kV station; thyristor valves from Bengaluru; transmission order book ~₹13,600 cr of a ₹2.58 L cr total
  • Transformers & Rectifiers (India) NSE/BSE: TARIL — First Indian-origin private HVDC converter-transformer order (₹53.33 cr, Dec-2025); empanelment follows a clean service record
  • CG Power NSE/BSE: CGPOWER — Power transformers & reactors up to 765 kV; CG Semi OSAT live
Pools that leak to imports
  • No domestic high-power semiconductor fab; XLPE-DC compound and accessories are foreign; EHV bushings largely imported.
  • Bottleneck nodes: Converter Valves (Thyristor / IGBT / MMC), Power Semiconductors (Thyristor / IGBT / SiC), Converter Transformers & Reactors, Control & Protection Systems (MACH / SCADA), HVDC Cables (MI / XLPE DC), HVDC-grade XLPE DC Compound & Accessories.
