Snapshot

Optical fibre cable — cabling fibre into land, FTTH, ADSS and OPGW cables. India is a large maker and a net exporter; the constraint is upstream preform. Scope: India, NSE/BSE.

Process & equipment
  • Fibre is buffered, stranded around FRP/aramid strength members with water-blocking yarn, sheathed in PE/PVC and armoured.
  • Cabling / SZ-stranding lines and OTDR/PMD/CD test gear are the key equipment.
Bottlenecks
  • Preform & bare fibre (upstream restricted; 18–24-month expansion cycle).
  • Strength members — aramid (no Indian maker) and FRP.
  • BharatNet Phase-III fixed-price squeeze; Chinese overcapacity and dumping.
  • Machinery & test equipment imported; AI data-centre demand skew.
Value pools
  • Cable assembly is competitive and thin-margin.
  • Value sits in preform/fibre above it.
  • India's export-hub role adds value.
  • Localisation: FRP/aramid strength members, cabling machinery.
Watch items
  • BharatNet Phase-III execution.
  • Preform price trajectory.
  • Anti-dumping duty on single-mode fibre.
  • Data-centre fibre demand.
Coverage gaps

No listed pure-play: aramid yarn, water-blocking gel, sheath compounds, cabling machinery, test gear — nearest: FRP (Aksh), the cable makers.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Preform & bare fibrePreform capacity is concentrated (<10 players globally) with an 18–24-month expansion cycle; preform spiked from $55 to $120–140/kg. [REPORTED]

Opportunity

Domestic preform integrationHFCL is investing ~₹580 cr in a preform facility; Finolex has commissioned Phase 1 (~100 t); STL already draws preform from sand. [REPORTED]

Risk

Strength members & FRP/aramidAramid yarn has no Indian maker (Teijin/DuPont); FRP capacity is concentrated and was named among cost-pressure inputs in BharatNet Phase-III. [REPORTED]

Opportunity

Backward integrationAksh runs the world’s largest single FRP-rod facility (~2 mn km/yr); HFCL (Hosur) and Polycab (Halol) make FRP/ARP rods in-house. [REPORTED]

Risk

BharatNet Phase-III fixed-price squeezeG.652D cable roughly doubled (₹300–325 → ₹700–800 per fibre-km) inside two years; vendors under fixed-price 10-year contracts sought force majeure. [REPORTED]

Opportunity

Multi-year volume certainty₹1.39 L cr programme; HFCL won a ₹2,666 cr RVNL UP-West package — volume visibility even as margins compress. [REPORTED]

Risk

Chinese overcapacity & dumpingChinese fibre capacity utilisation is low and export pricing creates structural pressure; sub-standard fibre has entered as raw material. [REPORTED]

Opportunity

Tariff & localisation tailwindTightening tariff / non-tariff barriers on Chinese cable and domestic-preference in BharatNet / 5G procurement favour Indian makers. [REPORTED]

Risk

Machinery & test equipmentCabling / SZ-stranding lines and OTDR / PMD test gear are imported (Austria, USA, Belgium, Switzerland) with no Indian pure-play. [INFERENCE]

Opportunity

Capacity-expansion pullThe ~₹950 cr+ industry capex wave (HFCL, Finolex, VTL) is a direct demand signal for upstream machinery localisation. [INFERENCE]

Risk

AI data-centre demand skew30–35% of world fibre demand is shifting to data centres; domestic telco capex stays lumpy and ARPU-sensitive. [REPORTED]

Opportunity

High-fibre-count premiumIBR / ribbon cables command premium margins — HFCL’s 3456-fibre micro-duct IBR and STL’s 864-fibre ribbon target this segment. [REPORTED]

Deepest hidden import dependency: the glass preform — the semiconductor-grade glass rod from which every fibre is drawn. Even with HFCL and Finolex building preform lines, India remains a net importer of preform, and it is the one input whose expansion cannot be dialled up to meet a demand spike.
