Snapshot

India builds warships and submarines well and commercial ships barely at all: 0.5% of the world order book, rank 12, against China's 64%. A ₹69,725 cr policy package approved in September 2025 is the first serious attempt to close a cost gap that has kept Indian yards out of global commercial tenders. Scope: newbuild and repair, India, NSE/BSE universe, depth-first on hull & steel, propulsion, marine electrical and repair/dry-docking.

Process & equipment
  • Plate cutting, block fabrication, sub-assembly, erection on slipway or in dock, launch, afloat outfitting, sea trials.
  • Key machinery: CNC plasma/oxy-fuel cutting, plate rolling, robotic and SAW welding lines, 50–600 t goliath and ELL cranes, floating and graving docks, shiplifts, blasting/painting halls.
  • Repair spine: docking, hydroblasting, robotic hull scanning, NDT, engine and propulsion overhaul, sea acceptance re-trials.
Bottlenecks
  • Main propulsion plant — imported or licence-assembled; resilience YEARS.
  • Shipbuilding-grade and armour-grade steel — two-PSU concentration; MONTHS.
  • Dry docks and goliath cranes — 44 dry docks nationally, longest build lead time in the industry; YEARS.
  • Combat systems — ~50–65% indigenous, the largest remaining warship gap; YEARS.
  • Certified welders, NDT technicians and naval architects; YEARS.
  • Working capital — both large PSUs ran negative operating cash flow in FY26 despite reporting profit.
Value pools
  • Ship repair is the cleanest pool: 34–44% PBIT margin versus 5–12% in newbuild — CSL, SDHI, MDL submarine refits.
  • Warship newbuild captures value but leaks it: equipment and materials are ~65% of ship cost and propulsion is largely imported.
  • Deck machinery, marine valves and marine switchboards are genuinely domestic and exportable — GRSE, Marine Electricals.
  • Steel and special alloys stay with SAIL and MIDHANI; the localisation opportunity is plate width and armour grades, not tonnage.
  • The ₹69,725 cr subsidy accrues to whoever holds the order book — concentrated in four listed yards.
  • Shipbreaking credit converts Alang scrap into newbuild demand — a pool no other shipbuilding nation has.
Market & growth
  • Value: listed-yard revenue ₹21,259→25,310 cr FY25→FY26, +19.1% nominal [CALCULATED].
  • Volume: GT output 40,923→57,637 GT in 2025, +40.8% [REPORTED-T1].
  • Order book 1.1 mn CGT, 0.5% of global; deliveries 2026–30 ~2× 2021–25 [REPORTED-T2].
  • 3-year and 5-year value CAGR [UNAVAILABLE] — no consistent India series; revenue is delivery-lumpy.
  • Growth is policy-driven and price-led: the newbuilding price index is 185.15 (Jun-2026), +33% on Jun-2021.
Watch items
  • SBFAS/SbDS valid to 31-Mar-2036; SBFAP contract window closed 31-Mar-2026.
  • Five greenfield clusters (TN, AP, GJ, MH, OD) hold conditional in-principle approval; three anchor MoUs signed.
  • P-75I (₹70,000 cr+, MDL–TKMS) and NGC (GRSE L1, ~₹25,000 cr) contract signatures are the two live order triggers.
  • Vadinar repair cluster CCEA-approved 05-May-2026, completion targeted 2030.
Coverage gaps

No listed pure-play in ship recycling, marine classification/design services, or marine training. Alang recyclers, Hindustan Shipyard and Goa Shipyard are unlisted; main-engine OEMs (MAN ES, Wartsila, MTU) are foreign-owned.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Main propulsion plantMain diesel engines, gas turbines, gearboxes and propellers are bought from MAN ES, Wartsila, MTU/Rolls-Royce and HD Hyundai; GRSE's own summary is that the ‘move’ package is indigenous in everything except the propulsion plant. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Licensed manufacture + a Make-I engineKirloskar’s ₹270 cr Make-I 6 MW marine diesel at >50% IC, GRSE’s MTU/Rolls-Royce Ranchi plant and the KSSL–GRSE propulsion MoU are the three live paths from licence assembly to design authority.

Risk

Shipbuilding-grade & armour steelCommercial plate is domestic, but wide plate, low-temperature-toughness grades and DMR-249/armour steel concentrate in SAIL and MIDHANI; a new yard cannot qualify an alternative mill quickly. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Plate width and armour gradesThe gap is not tonnage but width, thickness range and armour qualification — a mill-upgrade opportunity for SAIL/JSW/AMNS rather than a new entrant.

Risk

Dry docks, goliath cranes & berthsIndia reported 44 dry docks plus 9 port docks in FY25; the largest is SDHI’s 662 m × 65 m. A dock or a 600 t goliath crane takes 3–7 years to deliver and gates every downstream order. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

SbDS brownfield capital support25% capital assistance for docks, shiplifts, fabrication and automation; 10 applications, ₹3,706 cr eligible cost, four already holding in-principle approval (TNSL, SDHI, CSL, Mat Marine).

Risk

Combat systems — the ‘fight’Navy-wide indigenisation is ~90% float / ~60% move / ~50% fight; GRSE puts the ‘fight’ category at ~65% on P17A. Radars, sonars, EW and high-end weapons remain the largest warship import. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Indigenous sensor and weapon tiersBEL, BDL and the private missile houses are the capture vehicles; the 30 mm naval gun (GRSE + BHSEL, >60% IC) shows the model — gun-by-gun substitution.

Risk

Certified skillsWelders, NDT technicians, naval architects and marine engineers are the constraint that cannot be bought at any price on a two-year horizon; SbDS funds the India Ship Technology Centre for exactly this reason. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Training capacity at the cluster levelA 6.4 employment multiplier makes skilling the cheapest political lever: five greenfield clusters plus the IMU technology centre, if built on schedule, are the supply response.

Deepest hidden dependency: the main propulsion plant, and the working capital that sits behind it. India indigenises the hull, the outfit and the ship’s survival systems almost completely, then imports the machinery that makes it move — while both large PSU yards ran negative operating cash flow in FY26 (MDL −₹2,891 cr; CSL −₹1,234 cr) because warship revenue is recognised at delivery. Order book, subsidy and dock capacity are all real; the binding constraint on converting them into returns is propulsion sovereignty and cash conversion, not steel.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Repair & refit — 34–44% PBIT margin against 5–12% in newbuild.
  • Warship integration — the yard captures schedule and design-change margin, but ~65% of ship cost is bought-in equipment and materials.
  • Domestic equipment niches — deck machinery, marine valves, switchboards/IPMS.
  • Special steel — SAIL and MIDHANI capture the warship-grade premium.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Cochin Shipyard — largest repair earner and the deepest commercial/export book.
  • Mazagon Dock — submarines and destroyers; highest-value refits (MRLC).
  • Garden Reach — broadest platform range plus deck machinery, valves and engine overhaul.
  • Swan Defence — largest dock, first Indian ammonia dual-fuel order.
  • Marine Electricals — the only listed pure-play marine electrical tier-1.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Main engines, gearboxes, propellers — MAN ES, Wartsila, MTU, Hyundai.
  • High-end navigation, automation and combat sensors.
  • OEM repair spares and service kits.
  • Commercial vessel designs and classification rules.
  • Goliath cranes, shiplifts and shipyard automation.
