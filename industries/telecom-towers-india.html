DG Digest — at a glance short summary; full detail below
Snapshot
  • India’s passive layer: ~0.86 mn towers (FY26) with power, batteries, shelters and in-building systems attached. Scope: India, NSE/BSE, investment research.
  • Rent-plus-energy annuity, with a live renewal cliff.
Process & equipment
  • Galvanised lattice or monopole sections on RCC foundations, fitted with a DC plant, battery bank, genset, shelter and cooling.
  • The gating asset is the galvanising line and the erection crew — fabrication was gas-constrained in Q1 FY27.
Bottlenecks
  • Contract renewal: up to 15% of Indus revenue rides on the Jio tenancy renewal; YEARS to replace.
  • Fabrication and galvanising — domestic but gas-exposed; MONTHS.
  • LFP cells and rectifier semiconductors — imported; packs are local. MONTHS–YEARS.
  • Site power and water, named by brokers as the binding constraint on utilisation.
Value pools
  • Rent is the pool and it stays domestic: Indus and Altius hold most tenanted towers.
  • Price is leaking — revenue per tower −2.0% YoY against co-location growth of 5.9%.
  • Active sharing is the new pool: the July-2026 DCIP authorisation lets a towerco host RAN and complete IBS.
  • O&M is the durable one — ₹50 bn FY26 to ₹73–76 bn FY31.
Market & growth
  • Volume: 0.86 mn towers FY26 to 1.04–1.09 mn FY31, 4–5% CAGR [REPORTED-T2]; 3.9–4.9% computed [CALCULATED].
  • Value: passive spend ₹2.0–2.1 tn cumulative FY26–31, nominal; tower capex falls to ₹250–275 bn by FY28P [REPORTED-T2].
  • Last year: Indus FY26 revenue ₹32,493 cr, +7.9%; Q2 FY27 +4.0% on co-locations +5.9% — the gap is price/mix [REPORTED-T1].
  • 3-year value CAGR [UNAVAILABLE]; third-party forecasts conflict (5.1%, 7.6%, 2.37%) — scopes differ, not averaged.
Watch items
  • 20-Jul-2026 — Network Authorisation Rules: IP and DCIP scope, IRU barred.
  • H2 FY27 — Jio tenancy renewal decision; up to 15% of Indus revenue.
  • Dec-2026 — Ambit expects a 15% tariff hike, the next capex trigger.
  • FY27 — Indus Africa rollouts in Nigeria, Uganda and Zambia.
Coverage gaps
  • No listed pure-play in shelters, DC air-conditioning or site energy software; nearest is Exicom.
  • Tower fabrication has one listed pure-play (Salasar); the larger fabricators are unlisted.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Contract renewal at the top of the book Up to 15% of Indus revenue rides on the Reliance Jio tenancy renewal, with ~5,000 exits already modelled in H2 FY27; Vodafone Idea is over 30% of revenue and its capex depends on the SBI-led $3.5 bn debt raise. [REPORTED-T2]

Opportunity

Africa is the deliberate second leg Indus has approvals and anchor orders in Nigeria, Uganda and Zambia, with annual capex under 4% of EBITDA and debt funding; higher customers-per-tower (4.54k vs 1.06k in India) means faster payback. [REPORTED-T2]

Risk

Tower fabrication is gas-exposed Tower manufacturing was hit at the start of Q1 FY27 by constrained gas supplies from West Asia, and battery supply was affected even after tower supply recovered. [REPORTED-T2]

Opportunity

Domestic galvanising capacity Salasar runs one of the world’s larger galvanising baths, holds a ~₹2,500 cr order book and targets ₹1,800 cr FY27 revenue, with monopole capability up to 3 m diameter. [REPORTED-T2]

Risk

Energy is the largest cost and a negative-margin pass-through The energy spread was −4.6% in Q1 FY27 and −4.8% in Q2 FY26; power and fuel exceeded energy reimbursement in every recent quarter. Ambit names power and water linkages, not utilisation, as the main towerco risk. [REPORTED-T2]

Opportunity

Solarisation and ESCo Indus reached ~46,000 solar-access sites and cut diesel 13% YoY in Q1 FY27; an ESCo structure moves the capex off the towerco balance sheet. [REPORTED-T1]

Risk

Battery chemistry transition The VRLA-to-LFP pivot moves the value from a domestic lead-acid duopoly to imported cells; cell supply was disrupted in Q1 FY27 and China’s material-export regime remains the exposure. [REPORTED-T2]

Opportunity

Cell localisation, not pack assembly Amara Raja has crossed 1 GWh of lithium telecom deployment and is building a Telangana cell plant; Exide is scaling Li-ion through Exide Energy Solutions — the first domestic cell, not the first domestic pack, is the prize. [REPORTED-T2]

Risk

Tenancy ratio and ARPT are both deflating The industry tenancy ratio fell to 1.42 in FY25 from 1.47 in FY23 as new sites came up single-tenant; Indus revenue per tower fell 2.0% YoY. Crisil sees only 1.46–1.48 by Mar-2028. [REPORTED-T2]

Opportunity

5G loading and densification 5G BTS reached 593,244 by end-Sep-2026 and 5G subscribers 463 mn; loading existing sites and small-cell densification add revenue at low incremental cost, and Vi’s ₹450 bn capex plan is the swing factor. [REPORTED-T1]

Risk

Regulation now decides the business model The Network Authorisation Rules 2026 replaced IP-I with IP and created DCIP, but bar IRU-style asset transfers — a firmer posture that limits long-term financing structures for passive assets. [REPORTED-T1]

Opportunity

A licence to move up the stack DCIP scope covers RAN, complete IBS, WLAN and last-mile transmission, so a towerco can lease active elements it was previously forbidden to own — the first genuine revenue-line extension since tower sharing itself. [REPORTED-T1]

Deepest hidden dependency: not the steel and not the shelter — the LFP cell, the rectifier power semiconductor and the gas supply behind the galvanising line. India fabricates the tower, assembles the battery and builds the power plant; the three layers that decide cost, uptime and schedule all sit outside the country or outside the industry’s control.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • The rent annuity is the pool and it is domestic — Indus and Altius hold the bulk of India’s tenanted towers.
  • Price is leaking: revenue per tower −2.0% YoY against co-location growth of 5.9%.
  • Energy is a negative-margin pass-through (−4.6% spread, Q1 FY27), so solarisation defends margin rather than creating a pool.
  • O&M is the durable growth pool: ₹50 bn FY26 → ₹73–76 bn FY31 at 8–9% CAGR, growing as the base ages.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Indus Towers NSE/BSE: INDUSTOWER — 270,480 towers, 440,153 co-locations, tenancy 1.63; Q2 FY27 revenue ₹8,516 cr, ROCE 19.1%
  • Altius Telecom Infrastructure Trust BSE: 543225 — ~2.58 lakh sites, 315,000+ tenancies; Q1 FY27 revenue ₹6,025 cr, PAT ₹375 cr, ₹4.02/unit distribution
  • Bharti Hexacom NSE/BSE: BHARTIHEXA — two-circle towerco; 25,704 network towers at Mar-2024
  • GTL Infrastructure NSE/BSE: GTLINFRA — ~26,000 towers, financially stressed
  • Salasar Techno Engineering NSE/BSE: SALASAR — tower and monopole fabrication; FY26 revenue ₹1,511.9 cr, order book ~₹2,500 cr
  • Exicom Tele-Systems NSE/BSE: EXICOM — SMPS telecom power systems and site controllers
Pools that leak to imports
  • LFP and lithium cells — packs are made in India, cells are not.
  • Rectifier modules and power semiconductors inside the DC plant.
  • Small-cell and DAS silicon (Qualcomm-class platforms) behind the IBS layer.
  • Bottleneck nodes: TowerCo Operation, Macro Tower Structure, Site Power System, Batteries, Diesel Genset.
