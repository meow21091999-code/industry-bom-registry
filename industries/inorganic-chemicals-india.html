02Digestshort summary
Snapshot

India's basic inorganic chemical complex — the alkali and acid backbone that feeds alumina, glass, textiles, water treatment, steel and agriculture. A process industry, so the BOM is a recipe and energy balance, not an assembly tree. It spans chlor-alkali, soda ash, industrial gases, mineral acids and phosphate fertilisers. FY27 has split the complex in two: chlor-alkali prices sit at a five-year low but face a pending import-quota regime and firming chlorine, while the phosphate chain's costs have been blown open by the West Asia conflict and the Strait of Hormuz disruption.

Process & equipment
  • Chlor-alkali: brine purification → membrane-cell electrolysis, ~2,150–2,200 kWh/t; electricity is the dominant cost, and Epigral reports coal up 60–70% over three to four months.
  • Soda ash: Solvay route, then compaction to dense grade. GHCL runs the lowest-cost position at a 24.7% Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin while Chinese Solvay plants run below cost.
  • Gases: cryogenic air-separation units (ASU) and PSA — Linde's 250 tpd Dahej ASU is due in FY27; acids: DCDA sulphur-burn plus smelter by-product capture.
Bottlenecks
  • Rock phosphate / phosphoric acid / ammonia / sulphur — imported; DAP at ~$900–930/t CFR against a ~$510/t breakeven; MONTHS–YEARS.
  • Potash — 100% imported; STRUCTURAL, no localisation path.
  • Chlorine imbalance — chlorine is priced domestically and has reset from negative to roughly zero; MONTHS.
  • Soda-ash import surge — the binding constraint on realisations; relief is a recommendation, not a notified quota; WEEKS–MONTHS.
  • Turnkey equipment and membranes (ASU cold boxes, cell technology, DCDA / PA licences) — YEARS.
Value pools
  • Caustic soda is still a thin spread: Grasim's chemicals EBITDA fell 11% YoY in Q2 FY27 despite 15% revenue growth, on power costs.
  • Value keeps migrating into chlorine derivatives — Grasim's chlorine integration reached 64% in H1 FY27, guided to ~67% by FY27 exit, with CPVC (50 KTPA, Aug-2026) and ECH (50 KTPA, Oct-2026) commissioned at Vilayat.
  • Soda ash is the best-earning listed node but the pool is policy-set: GHCL held 24.7% with net cash while Tata's Industrial Essentials posted a ₹120 cr EBIT loss.
  • Industrial gases remain the most attractive pool — contract-locked and growing 6–7% — but the bulk leaks to unlisted MNC joint ventures.
  • In phosphates the pool is the subsidy: with DAP landed near $900/t against an MRP of ₹27,000/t, NBS plus the ₹3,500/t package still leaves roughly ₹23,000/t of under-recovery; only backward-integrated producers keep the spread.
  • Localisation prize: rock-phosphate / phosphoric-acid capacity, downstream chlorine integration, and the 2.169 mtpa of announced soda-ash capacity that would take India to ~6.82 mtpa by FY2030.
Market & growth
  • Value (traded market): USD 44.6 bn in 2024 to USD 74.09 bn by 2033, a 5.8% nominal CAGR [REPORTED-T3: Custom Market Insights, India inorganic chemicals market, base year 2023, updated Jan-2026]. Scope: traded inorganic chemicals sold in India — acids, alkalis, industrial gases and fertiliser intermediates; organic chemicals and petrochemicals excluded. Value only; there is no volume series at this scope.
  • Horizons off that series, all three at the same reported rate: 5-year (2024→2029) 5.8% nominal, 3-year (2024→2027) 5.8%, last year (2023→2024) 5.8% — USD 42.16 bn to USD 44.6 bn [CALCULATED from the reported 2023 and 2024 sizes and the reported CAGR]. Growth here is price and mix, not physical: the volume series below runs slower.
  • A second, differently scoped rupee series sits beside it and is not blended: ₹4.9 lakh crore (FY25) to ₹6.4 lakh crore (FY29), ~6.9% nominal CAGR [CALCULATED from the FY25 and FY29 endpoints; REPORTED-T3: industry research report, Sep-2025]. A third house puts India's inorganic-chemicals CAGR at 3.95% [CONFLICTING DATA] and a fourth scopes India far narrower, at USD 16.3 bn (2025) to USD 28.4 bn (2035) at 5.7% [REPORTED-T3: Chemical Industry Insights, Oct-2026]. The definitions differ; the figures are shown, never averaged.
  • Volume is the honest series for a process industry: caustic soda output was 50.20 lakh t in FY25 at 78.4% utilisation on 64.04 lakh t of capacity [REPORTED-T2: AMAI], with FY27 demand guided to low-double-digit growth, 600,000–750,000 t of incremental demand and a further 400,000–500,000 t from 3 mtpa of new alumina capacity [REPORTED-T2: Ind-Ra, Sep-2026]. Soda ash consumed 44.17 lakh t in FY25, up 7.0% [REPORTED-T2: DGCI&S / AMAI via IIFT brief, Jul-2026], and demand is put at 4.5–5% a year FY27–30 [REPORTED-T2: Ind-Ra].
  • Drivers: alumina (9.96 Mt FY26 → 13.66 Mt FY30) and solar glass for both alkalis; a soda-ash pipeline of 2.169 mtpa (GHCL 1.10, RSPL 0.50, Tata 0.41, Tuticorin 0.16) taking installed capacity to ~6.82 mtpa by FY2030 from ~4.65 mtpa in FY2026 [REPORTED-T2: Ind-Ra]; against ~2 mtpa of new caustic capacity landing in FY28–29, which is when today's tight utilisation reverses.
Watch items
  • Correction from the prior run: the soda-ash case has moved from "safeguard-QR probe initiated 16-Mar-2026" to final findings on 4-Nov-2026 recommending a two-year country-wise quota (754,992 t in Year 1, 792,740 t in Year 2). It is still a recommendation — watch the Gazette notification and the EDI-port quota mechanics. [LIVE]
  • NBS Rabi 2026-27 notified 11-Nov-2026: N ₹94.21, P ₹66.11, K ₹2.38, S ₹10.42/kg for 1-Oct-2026 to 31-Mar-2027 — phosphorus up from ₹47.96/kg a year earlier. Kharif 2027 rates fall due around April 2027. [LIVE]
  • West Asia conflict / Strait of Hormuz: ammonia up ~60% and sulphur ~50% since March 2026, and India imports over 80% of both from the Gulf. A reopening or a further escalation re-prices the entire phosphate chain. [LIVE]
  • Supply-side swings: GHCL's ₹6,500 cr / 1.1 MMT greenfield and Tata's Mithapur +350 kt dense soda ash (Q3 FY2028); the one-year MIP on clear float glass, which supports domestic glass demand for soda ash. [LIVE]
Coverage gaps

Bulk industrial gases (INOX Air Products, Air Liquide India, Air Water India all unlisted), rock-phosphate mining, natural soda ash, RSPL/SOKA, the IFFCO cooperative and Indian Potash Limited, and the turnkey equipment layer have no NSE/BSE coverage. The listed potash names are users, not producers.

04Value-chain ladder & supplier tiersfeedstock → end use
Raw material
Salt & brine (domestic), limestone (domestic), sulphur — imported rock phosphate — imported potash — imported natural gas.
Refining
Brine purification, elemental-sulphur burning (DCDA), and smelter SO₂ by-product capture from zinc / copper / lead smelting.
Basic inorganic
Caustic soda, chlorine, hydrogen, soda ash, H₂SO₄, H₃PO₄, HNO₃, O₂ / N₂ / Ar.
Derivative
PVC / CPVC, chloromethanes, chlorotoluenes, hydrogen peroxide, DAP / NPK / SSP, sodium bicarbonate.
End use
Alumina & aluminium, float & solar glass, textiles, soaps & detergents, pulp & paper, water treatment, steel, agrochemicals, pharma, food.
Supplier tiers
  • Tier-4 feedstock (salt, sulphur, rock phosphate, potash) → Tier-3 basic inorganic → Tier-2 derivative → Tier-1 integrated producer → end-use OEM.
  • Aftermarket: membrane replacement, spares, ZLD / effluent, and circularity (smelter acid, gypsum).

07Bottlenecks — risk & opportunitydual-readable
Risk
Rock phosphate / phosphoric acid import dependence

Almost all rock phosphate, phosphoric acid and ammonia are imported. DAP feedstock is hostage to Jordan / Morocco pricing, and DAP importers face losses the subsidy only partly covers.

Opportunity
Domestic PA capacity & upstream rock stakes

GSFC's Sikka phosphoric + sulphuric acid expansion, Coromandel's 45% stake in Senegal's Baobab Mining, and the rock-phosphate price-linkage notification are the localisation plays. Resilience class MONTHS–YEARS.

Risk
Potash — 100% import dependent

~2 MMT/yr sourced from Russia, Israel, Belarus and Jordan, with no domestic substitute. Resilience class STRUCTURAL.

Opportunity
None at the material layer

There is no localisation path for potash; the only levers are agronomic efficiency and long-term offtake contracts. This is a node to avoid, not to build.

Risk
Chlorine imbalance

Caustic and chlorine are co-produced ~1:0.89; weak chlorine offtake forces caustic output cuts. India is caustic-centric with low chlorine integration. Resilience class MONTHS.

Opportunity
Downstream chlorine integration

Grasim's captive chlorine use reached 53%; GACL commissioned India's largest chlorotoluenes plant; DCM Shriram is building a chlorine pipeline to Aarti. This is the single biggest value-migration in the complex.

Risk
Soda-ash import surge

China's ~10 MMT Inner Mongolia surplus and Turkish / US capacity additions have pushed imports to ~25% of demand, eroding domestic realisations. Resilience class MONTHS (AD / MIP protected).

Opportunity
Solar-glass demand & domestic capacity

India's solar-glass capacity scaling toward 40 GW needs ~400 kt extra soda ash. GHCL's ₹6,500 cr / 1.1 MMT greenfield and Tata's Mithapur +350 kt dense capture the import gap.

value_chain10%
The complex sits mid-chain and leaks value to feedstock: rock phosphate, potash, ammonia and sulphur are imported, and the domestic layer earns a conversion spread. Where the layer has moved up — Grasim’s chlorine integration to ~67% by FY27 exit, smelter by-product acid, GHCL’s low-cost soda ash — returns are visibly better, so the score stays at 3 rather than 2.
