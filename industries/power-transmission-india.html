Snapshot

Bulk transmission at 220 kV and above — lines, substations, HVDC, cables, EPC and the regulated or TBCB developer layer. 5,10,814 ckm and 1,492.9 GVA operate at Aug-2026 against a ₹9.16 L cr plan to FY32; the equipment that carries it is the tightest link.

Process & equipment
  • Line build is civil-first and domestic; substation equipment is OEM-built to order and type-tested, at 18–24 month lead times.
  • HVDC goes out in split packages — Barmer-II as converter terminals (GE Vernova T&D), synchronous condensers (BHEL) and separate line packages.
  • Hidden imports sit under the equipment: CRGO, bushings, pressboard, CTC, tap changers, semiconductors, and the vacuum and HV test plant that builds transformers.
Bottlenecks
  • EHV transformers and reactors — 18–24 months, prices up 70–80% since 2021; ~300 GVA of capacity is coming. Months.
  • CRGO — 90% imported, 40–50 kt domestic against 400–450 kt of demand. Structural on source.
  • HVDC converter equipment — three global suppliers, one per corridor. Years.
  • GIS/GIB, 765 kV breakers, and right-of-way delays that already cut PGCIL’s capitalisation guidance.
Value pools
  • The developer layer: a 35-year TBCB annuity on ₹2.20 L cr of works in hand, at an 82% EBITDA margin.
  • EHV transformer OEMs now hold the best returns — CG Power’s Power Systems earned 25.0% PBIT on sales.
  • EPC and towers stay domestic and thin-margin; value leaks at components, not at the line.
  • Localisation bets: TARIL’s ₹1,250 cr bushings/pressboard/CTC plants, CG’s ₹300 cr bushing line, and the SiC-MOSFET programme begun Sep-2026.
Market & growth
  • Volume, 5-year forward: 1,451 → 2,345 GVA by FY32 = 8.3% CAGR [CALCULATED] — a policy target, not a trend.
  • 3-year historical 7.1%, last year +8.4% [CALCULATED], on record FY26 additions of 1,13,013 MVA.
  • Value: ~₹81,000 cr/yr planned for FY26–31 against ~₹46,500 cr/yr actually spent in FY24–26 [REPORTED-T2], nominal. Growth is almost entirely physical; the only price/mix wedge is transformer inflation.
Watch items
  • Hitachi Energy India Q2 FY27 results, 12-Nov-2026 — whether 16% margins hold against copper up 46% year-on-year.
  • CRGO anti-dumping case (initiated 22-Jun-2026): a duty would raise transformer cost.
  • PGCIL’s ₹67,233 cr TBCB pipeline, and the ₹31,000 cr Pang scheme being reworked into EHVAC after the 29-Oct-2026 NCT meeting.
Coverage gaps

No NSE/BSE pure-play in CRGO, IGBT/SiC modules, EHV bushings or tap changers; BHEL is the only listed synchronous-condenser maker.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

CRGO electrical steel~90% imported; consumption of 4.0–4.5 lakh t against domestic output of 40–50 kt. DGTR opened an anti-dumping probe on 22-Jun-2026 against China, Japan, Korea and Russia, with the PUC/PCN notice issued 13-Aug-2026 and no duty notified as of Nov-2026. Resilience: structural on source, months on price.

Opportunity

Domestic GOES capacityJSW JFE Electrical Steel Nashik is the sole eligible domestic producer and would capture the entire duty benefit; TARIL has bought a controlling stake in a CRGO processing unit, moving one step down the same chain.

Risk

EHV transformer and reactor capacityLead times have stretched to 18–24 months from 8–12; prices are up 70–80% since 2021 on limited supplier competition; bushings, pressboard and CTC are the sub-components that bind next. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Capacity build-out and backward integration~300 GVA of transformer capacity is announced — CG Power’s Sehore plant (35 units a month, first unit 4-Sep-2026), TARIL to 75,000 MVA, Kanohar 19,200 → 37,200 MVA — while TARIL’s ₹1,250 cr bushings/pressboard/CTC plants target Q1 FY28.

Risk

HVDC converter equipmentA three-player global oligopoly; one terminal supplier per corridor and 48 months to first pole on Barmer-II. Global capacity cannot run many large schemes at once. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Localised HVDCGE Vernova T&D took the Barmer-II terminal (LoA 3-Nov-2026) and BHEL the two synchronous condensers (7-Nov-2026); Khavda V-A ran as a BHEL–Hitachi consortium; ~22 HVDCs are planned, so the qualification set has room to widen.

Risk

Power semiconductors and GIS60–65% of STATCOM core components (IGBT, SiC, HV capacitors) are imported, and the GIS/GIB and 765 kV breaker supplier base is thin — both named as the constraints that follow transformers. Resilience: years for devices, months for GIS.

Opportunity

Device localisation is now fundedCEA listed 73 imported power-sector items for localisation; MeitY, MoP and POWERGRID launched a SiC-MOSFET programme in Sep-2026 (36 months, ~13,000 pilot modules); CG’s Vilholi GIS is going from 228 to 468 equivalent bays.

Risk

Right-of-way, land and testingPGCIL cut FY27 capitalisation guidance to ₹25,000–30,000 cr explicitly on RoW and land, not equipment; market-rate compensation still needs state adoption. HV short-circuit and high-power test capacity remains concentrated. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Testing slots and R&M annuityQualification-gated test capacity and certified HV skills carry durable scarcity value; the installed base is 25–40 years old in places, which builds a repair and refurbishment market (TARIL’s first HVDC transformer repair order from PGCIL).

Deepest hidden import dependency: not the transformer but the plant that builds it — vacuum/oil-processing and drying systems, winding machines, HV test bays and the tooling behind them, with 2–4 year procurement lead times for large-power-transformer manufacturing lines.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • The developer layer — a 35-year TBCB annuity on ₹2.20 L cr of PGCIL works in hand and ₹67,233 cr of live pipeline.
  • EHV transformer and switchgear OEMs — the node where pricing power has hardened most; CG Power’s Power Systems earned 25.0% PBIT on sales.
  • HVDC terminals — four TBCB schemes transferred, split PGCIL 55% / AESL 45% of tariff, with equipment to a three-player set.
  • EPC and towers — domestic and competitive; EHV substation work earns ~15% against ~13% on distribution.
  • Export and China+1 — Indian OEMs booking global grid orders on 2–4 year lead times abroad.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Hitachi Energy India NSE/BSE: POWERINDIA — backlog ₹32,222 cr (Jun-2026), record; Q1 FY27 revenue +68.6%, Op EBITDA 16.0%
  • CG Power & Industrial Solutions NSE/BSE: CGPOWER — Power Systems backlog ₹18,964 cr (+81%); Q2 FY27 intake +137%
  • GE Vernova T&D India NSE/BSE — Q2 FY27 revenue ₹2,030 cr (+32%), orders +52%; Barmer-II HVDC terminal LoA
  • Siemens Energy India NSE/BSE — T&D ~68% of a ₹18,430 cr order book; Kalwa doubling to 30,000 MVA
  • Transformers & Rectifiers (India) NSE/BSE: TARIL — capacity to 75,000 MVA; ₹1,250 cr backward-integration plants
  • Power Grid Corporation NSE/BSE: POWERGRID — Q2 FY27 PAT ₹3,867 cr, EBITDA margin 82%; capex guidance ₹37,000–40,000 cr
Pools that leak to imports
  • CRGO (90% imported), power semiconductors (60–65% of STATCOM core), EHV bushings, pressboard and OLTCs.
  • Bottleneck nodes: EHV Power Transformers & Reactors, HVDC Converter Stations & Control, STATCOM · Synchronous Condensers · FACTS, GIS / AIS Switchgear, CRGO Electrical Steel, Power Semiconductors (IGBT / SiC).
