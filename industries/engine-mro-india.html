Snapshot

Engine MRO is the largest, hardest and most OEM-controlled pool in aviation maintenance. A shop visit is ~60–70% material and ~80% of engine maintenance cost is OEM-controlled parts — so India's low labour cost buys little here. India's commercial engine overhaul is effectively one government shop (AIESL) plus Safran's new Hyderabad LEAP centre; ~80% of demand still goes overseas.

Process & equipment
  • Disassembly to modules → clean → NDT → repair → heat-treat/coat → machine → balance → assemble → test-cell run.
  • Gating inputs: LLPs, HPT/HPC airfoils, OEM parts data and the Engine Shop Manual.
  • Equipment: test cells, balancing rigs, vacuum furnaces, 9-axis plasma spray, CMM, engine stands.
Bottlenecks
  • OEM-controlled parts, data & LLP monopoly — years.
  • Test-cell capacity and critical mass (needs 2,000–2,500 aircraft) — years.
  • CAR-66 engine-CRS licence anomaly — years.
  • Part lead times >12 months — cyclical.
Value pools
  • OEM shops (CFM/GE/Safran, P&W) capture the material margin.
  • Premier/licensed MROs (LHT, AFI-KLM, MTU) capture shop-visit value.
  • USM/PMA/DER economy is the independents' margin lever.
  • India's domestic pool is thin: AIESL + Safran SAESI + IndiGo (building).
Market & growth
  • Global engine MRO ~$50bn (2024), ~40–50% of total MRO [REPORTED-T1].
  • LEAP shop visits ~600–800 (2025) → 5,000+ (2040), ~14% p.a. [CALCULATED from IATA].
  • India: engine + APU ~60% of MRO outlay; ~80% of engine MRO overseas [REPORTED-T2].
  • Value nominal; volume = shop visits. Scope conflicts on India engine-MRO sizing [CONFLICTING DATA].
Watch items
  • Safran SAESI operational 2026 (300 LEAP visits/yr).
  • IndiGo–CFM MoU (Jul-2026) includes engine-MRO support.
  • IATA–CFM open-access agreement renewed Jan-2026.
  • AIESL LEAP capability target 2030–31; privatisation live.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

OEM-controlled parts, data & LLP monopoly~80% of engine cost is OEM material; LLPs are 40–60% of the parts bill. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

IATA–CFM open accessFree ESM, PMA/DER recognition (renewed Jan-2026) opens the independents' route.

Risk

Test-cell capacity & critical massA restoration needs a cell; GE's India CEO puts viability at 2,000–2,500 aircraft. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Cells as leverageTest cells are the cheapest high-leverage capex (AIESL has 3); Safran's bench adds capacity.

Risk

CAR-66 engine-CRS anomalyDGCA requires a licensed person to issue the engine CRS, deviating from EASA; AIESL calls this the biggest bottleneck. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Certification reformAligning CAR-66 with EASA Part-66 unlocks shop ramp-up.

Risk

Part lead times >12 monthsNew-parts scarcity stretches TAT and AOG exposure. Resilience: cyclical.

Opportunity

USM / PMA / DER30–50% cheaper parts; module swaps avoid full LLP replacement.

Deepest hidden dependency: the OEM's parts and data — not the hangar or the labour. India can build the test cell; it cannot yet buy the LLP at will. The route in is the IATA-CFM open ecosystem plus an OEM-partnered shop (Safran), not a standalone Indian overhaul line.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • OEM shops (CFM/GE/Safran, P&W) — material margin.
  • Premier/licensed MROs — shop-visit value.
  • USM/PMA/DER traders — the independents' margin.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • HAL — defence engines, SAFHAL, LEAP forged parts.
  • IndiGo — building engine MRO.
  • PTC / Azad — engine parts & airfoils (upstream).
Pools that leak to imports
  • Engine MRO — ~80% overseas.
  • LLPs, airfoils, FADEC/ECU & OEM data.
  • USM/rotable pool — thin in India, long TAT.
