Snapshot

Optical transceivers & modules — the pluggable 100G/400G/800G/1.6T optics in every transport port, switch line-card and AI-cluster fabric. It is the fastest-growing and most import-dependent layer of the optical stack: India is a large and fast-growing demand market (AI data centres, 5G backhaul, DCI) but designs and fabs none of the two chips that matter — the PAM4 DSP and the laser die. Scope: India, NSE/BSE. Re-analysed 2026-10-24 (delta pass over the 2026-10-08 entry).

Process & equipment
  • Optical engines are assembled from imported DSPs, laser dies and PICs; TO-can/OSA build, sub-micron active alignment, firmware, thermal packaging, burn-in and high-speed test are the key steps.
  • Key machinery: die/wire bonders, active-alignment stations, automated optical & electrical test (BERT, OSA, high-speed scopes) and thermal burn-in chambers — imported.
  • India’s first real module-assembly capacity is being built now: the Dixon–Gemtek JV (Oct-2026) and GX Quantum Photonics (Dec-2026).
Bottlenecks
  • Optical DSP / retimer ASICs (PAM4) — Broadcom/Marvell/Credo; no Indian design or fab. Resilience: YEARS.
  • Light sources EML/VCSEL/DFB — InP/GaAs laser-die fab absent in India; global InP-EML demand exceeds supply ~30% in 2026. Resilience: YEARS–STRUCTURAL.
  • Silicon-photonics PIC engines — imported; Indian design just starting (IIT-Madras PDK, Apr-2026). Resilience: YEARS.
  • High-speed 800G/1.6T test & qualification capacity is thin in India; lead times for 800G-class components ran 14–20 weeks.
  • Import duty ~44% on finished modules (HS 8517 62 90); SFP classification has been litigated.
Value pools
  • Value sits in the DSPs, laser dies and PICs — all imported; the top-5 module vendors hold ~72% of global revenue and Chinese firms >70% of 800G+.
  • India’s listed value-add is module/assembly integration (new), fibre & cable, connectors and network systems.
  • The AI data-centre build (1.8 GW → 6 GW by 2029) is the domestic pull; DCI is the fastest-growing application.
  • Localisation path: assembly (Dixon–Gemtek, GX) → OSA/BOSA → PIC/DSP design.
Market & growth
  • India optical-transceiver market: USD 0.73 bn (2026) → 0.82 bn (2027) → 1.73 bn (2033), ~13.25% CAGR (2027–33) [REPORTED-T3, MarkNtel]. A second scope puts India optical interconnect at $938.6 mn (2026) → $3,569 mn (2033), 21% CAGR [REPORTED-T3, GrandView]. Value, nominal; the two scopes differ and are shown, not averaged.
  • Current-year global read: Ethernet optics $16.5 bn (2025) → $26 bn (2026), +60% [REPORTED-T2, LightCounting]; ~$80 bn by 2031.
  • Volume: India data-centre capacity 1.8 GW (H1-2026) → ~6 GW (2029) [REPORTED-T2]; 800G-and-above rose from 19.5% of global module shipments (2024) to >60% (2026) [REPORTED-T3].
  • Growth is demand/adoption-led: module ASPs fall 12–18%/yr, so value growth is mix-led (800G/1.6T, SiPh, coherent) rather than pure price.
Watch items
  • Dixon–Gemtek JV (60:40) to make SFP transceivers + BOSA in India; completion target 3-Feb-2027. [LIVE]
  • GX Quantum Photonics ₹500 cr SFP/TOSA/ROSA plant — production from Dec-2026; ECMS phase-1 ₹100 cr approved. [LIVE]
  • US FCC — final rule FCC 26-50 (adopted 22-Jul-2026, published 11-Sep, effective 13-Oct-2026) bars new authorisations for devices containing logic-bearing components from Covered-List firms; optical transceivers are not named and the large Chinese vendors stay off the list, though a separate new-model transceiver ban reported in Aug-2026 remains under consideration. Correction from the prior run: this was carried as “considering a ban” — the rule is now in force and narrower than feared. [REPORTED-T2]
  • India data-centre capacity on track for ~2 GW by end-2026; >$38 bn committed in H1-2026. [LIVE]
  • Aksh Optifibre under CIRP; NCLAT stayed the IRP’s further steps (30-Jun-2026). [LIVE]
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play across optical DSP, laser dies, photodetectors or PIC design. Nearest listed: Dixon (new module JV), Tejas/HFCL (systems & integration), STL (connectors), HFCL/STL/Birla/Aksh (fibre & cable). New module assembly is otherwise private (GX, CloudPhotonix, LightSpeed Photonics).

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Optical DSP & retimer ASICsNo Indian design or fab of PAM4 DSPs; 400G/800G/1.6T engines rely on Broadcom, Marvell and Credo silicon. Export controls and allocation risk sit here, and the AI boom has driven record DSP backlogs.

Opportunity

Fabless DSP designDesign-led PLI plus India’s chip-design talent base make a fabless optical-DSP play the highest-value localisation frontier — a design-in win at a hyperscaler qualifies the whole module.

Risk

Light sources — EML / VCSEL / DFBInP/GaAs laser-die fabrication is absent in India; dies come from Coherent, Lumentum, Broadcom, Sumitomo. Global InP-EML demand exceeded supply by ~30% in 2026 and 800G-class component lead times ran 14–20 weeks.

Opportunity

Compound-semiconductor seedIndia’s compound-semi / OSAT push could seed laser-die packaging and test even before wafer fab — the same play that built the LED-packaging base.

Risk

Module integration was only final assemblyUntil Oct-2026 India’s value-add was integrating imported engines into housings — thin margin, low IP — with no listed module pure-play.

Opportunity

Integration → design — the base is now being builtDixon–Gemtek (60:40 JV, SFP + BOSA, target Feb-2027) and GX Quantum Photonics (₹500 cr, SFP/TOSA/ROSA, from Dec-2026) start the assembly base; HFCL’s data-centre connectivity plant (₹215 cr, 2.7 lakh assemblies/yr, by Sep-2027) deepens it.

Risk

High-speed test & qualification800G, 1.6T and coherent test capacity is limited in India; buyers rely on overseas labs, stretching qualification cycles and time-to-deploy.

Opportunity

Local test & burn-in as a serviceQualification-gated labs carry durable scarcity value and are a cheaper entry point than fab; IIT-Madras’ PPIC test engine (Apr-2026) is a first domestic step.

Risk

Import dependence, concentration & duty85–90% of 100G+ demand by value is imported, China-dominant (7 of the top-10 vendors; >70% of 800G+); finished modules carry ~44% total duty incidence and SFP classification has been litigated.

Opportunity

Import substitution under ECMS/PLIHigh-count cable, connectors and preform lines (HFCL ₹1,800 cr; STL ₹3,000 cr) plus the new SFP/BOSA assembly plants target the substitutable layers, with the US FCC’s China-module review as a re-shoring tailwind.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the InP/GaAs laser dies and the PAM4 DSP silicon inside every 400G/800G/1.6T module — the two chips India neither designs nor fabs today. Module assembly is now genuinely localising (Dixon–Gemtek, GX); these two layers cannot, in the current window, and they carry the highest value per module.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Value sits in the DSPs, laser dies and PICs — all imported; the top-5 module vendors hold ~72% of global module revenue.
  • India’s value-add is final assembly (now being built), plus fibre/cable, connectors and systems.
  • The AI data-centre wave (1.8 GW → 6 GW by 2029) is the big domestic opportunity; DCI is the fastest-growing application.
  • Localisation path: module integration → OSA/BOSA → PIC/DSP design.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Dixon Technologies NSE/BSE: DIXON — [REPORTED] 60:40 JV with Taiwan’s Gemtek to make SFP transceivers + BOSA in India; completion target 3-Feb-2027; the first listed Indian module-assembly exposure.
  • Tejas Networks NSE/BSE: TEJASNET — [REPORTED] Designs & manufactures optical transport and packet-optical systems; 400G/800G coherent DWDM; hyperscaler/DCI and BSNL 4G; design-led PLI approved.
  • HFCL NSE/BSE: HFCL — [REPORTED] Data-centre optical connectivity (MMC/SNMT assemblies, ₹215 cr plant by Sep-2027); fibre/cable; ₹29,000 cr order book.
  • Sterlite Technologies (STL) NSE/BSE: STLTECH — [REPORTED] Optical connectivity & interconnect (connectors, patch cords, MPO, pre-terminated systems); $1.11 bn hyperscaler optical deal.
  • Polycab India NSE/BSE: POLYCAB — [INFERENCE] Wires & cables major with optical-fibre cable capacity; data-centre fibre a growing but smaller part of the mix.
  • Birla Cable NSE/BSE: BIRLACABLE · Aksh Optifibre NSE/BSE: AKSHOPTFBR — [REPORTED] Optical-fibre cable makers; Aksh is under CIRP.
  • ITI Limited NSE/BSE: ITI · Bharat Electronics NSE/BSE: BEL — [INFERENCE] Networking/transmission and optronics, minor segments of larger businesses.
Pools that leak to imports
  • No listed pure-play across DSP, laser dies, photodetectors, PIC design and high-speed test — nearest: Dixon (module JV), Tejas/HFCL (systems), STL (connectors).
  • Bottleneck nodes: Optical DSP & Retimer ASICs (PAM4), Light Sources (EML/VCSEL/DFB), Silicon Photonics / PIC Engines, Coherent Optics (400ZR/800ZR DCO), Test, Burn-in & Qualification.
  • The assembly margin stays domestic but is competitive and duty-exposed.
