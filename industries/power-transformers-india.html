Snapshot
  • ~₹40,000 cr market (FY26 estimate) on a T&D build-out that added 1,13,013 MVA in FY26; power-transformer production rose 31.6%.
  • Backlogs are at records (Hitachi ₹32,222 cr, GE Vernova ₹21,443 cr, CG Power ₹21,820 cr standalone, TARIL ₹7,237 cr); imported inputs and test capacity bind, not orders.
Process & equipment
  • CRGO coil slit and cut-to-length to ±0.1 mm, step-lap mitered, stacked; lamination processing is becoming a specialist step (Vilas, 36,000 MTPA).
  • HV disc and LV foil windings from paper/Nomex-insulated copper or aluminium CTC; drying to <0.5% moisture, vacuum oil filling.
  • Routine and type testing to IS 2026/IEC 60076 on winding machines, vapour-phase ovens, impulse and short-circuit labs. Capacity is cheap: CG Power adds 10,000 MVA for ₹68 cr.
Bottlenecks
  • CRGO — ~90% imported; DGTR probe live with no finding; domestic capacity from FY2028.
  • RIP bushing cores ~95% imported (420/550 kV localised only in Q4 FY28); vacuum OLTCs >80% imported.
  • Qualification: ~50% of transformers fail short-circuit testing first time, so accredited labs set lead time.
  • Record copper (LME ~US$13,950/t) is squeezing firm-price books — Voltamp lost 543 bps of gross margin.
Value pools
  • Finished EHV transformers keep the most: CG Power Power Systems 25.0% PBIT, GE Vernova 28.6% PBT, TARIL 16.1% EBITDA, Hitachi 16.0%.
  • Pricing power splits by voltage — 400/765 kV and HVDC earn it; 220 kV and industrial units compete on price (industry margin 8–10%).
  • OLTC: smallest pool, highest switching cost, no qualified Indian maker.
  • Bushings are localising fastest (Yash +95% revenue, ₹760 cr book) but the RIP core still leaks abroad.
  • CRGO core stays strategically decisive but its pricing power is fading; lamination processing is capital-light and thin (7.1% PAT).
Market & growth

  • 3-year value CAGR 15.5% (₹30,000 cr FY24 → ₹40,000 cr FY26) [CALCULATED], with volume outpacing value — realisation per MVA is falling.
  • 5-year value: [UNAVAILABLE]; volume: AC transformation capacity +~7% a year since FY19 [REPORTED-T1].
  • FY27 target 1,58,339 MVA (+40%); NEP successor plan ₹7.93 lakh cr to 2035-36; managements guide 25–45% growth.
Watch items
  • DGTR CRGO finding — retrospective duty sought, unpublished at 8-Nov-2026.
  • Hitachi Energy India Q2 FY27 print, 12-Nov-2026.
  • Barmer–South Kalamb HVDC booking (GE Vernova L1) and the ~₹1.0 lakh cr HVDC pipeline.
  • Commissioning: TARIL ~35,000 MVA (Nov-2026), Bharat Bijlee 36,000 MVA (Jan-2027), Shilchar 14,000 MVA (Apr-2027).
Coverage gaps
  • No listed vacuum-OLTC pure-play; bushings covered only by Yash (BSE).
  • Tanks, radiators and gauges have no listed pure-play; domestic CRGO sits in the unlisted JSW–JFE JV.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

CRGO electrical steel (core)~90% of India's 4.0–4.5 lakh t requirement is imported (CN/JP/KR/RU) against 40–50k t of domestic capacity; the DGTR anti-dumping case opened 22-Jun-2026 with retrospective duty sought and no finding yet. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Domestic GOESJSW–JFE takes total capacity to 350,000 tpa (Nashik 250k + Vijayanagar 100k) from FY2028, and Vilas Transcore has tripled lamination capacity to 36,000 MTPA with POWERGRID approval to 220 kV.

Risk

HV bushings (OIP/RIP)RIP capacitive cores are ~95% imported and OIP bushings are also short of capacity, so bushing availability gates EHV deliveries. Resilience: months, improving.

Opportunity

RIP localisationFour Indian makers are building localised RIP capacity — Yash (revenue +95% to ₹194.5 cr, ₹760 cr order book, 245 kV localised from Q4 FY27), CG Power, Hitachi India and MIM — with 420/550 kV by Q4 FY28.

Risk

On-load tap changer (OLTC)Advanced vacuum OLTCs are >80% imported on a Reinhausen-led oligopoly, on the one moving part whose failure is a transformer outage. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Domestic OLTCBHEL and EMR/Easun MR make and retrofit OLTCs, but no indigenous vacuum OLTC is qualified at competitive scale — the largest unaddressed substitution pool in the chain.

Risk

Copper at a recordLME copper settled at ~US$13,950/t on 6-Nov-2026 (+35% YoY, the highest since 1960), and windings are 35–45% of active material cost; firm-price order books are already absorbing it (Voltamp −543 bps gross margin, Atlanta −201 bps EBITDA, Shilchar −1,049 bps YoY). Resilience: weeks on pricing, months on contracts.

Opportunity

CTC capacity and price-variation clausesApar, Precision Wires, Vidya Wires and now Vilas are adding conductor and lamination capacity, and since March 2026 new inquiries are being quoted on price-variation terms — the margin shock is a legacy-book problem, not a permanent one.

Risk

Qualification and test capacityOnly ~50% of transformers pass short-circuit testing first time and 765 kV qualification needs 3–4 years of field performance, so accredited high-power lab capacity — not plant capacity — sets delivery dates. Resilience: months to years.

Opportunity

In-house test capabilityCG Power's Sehore line covers 220–1,200 kV, TARIL runs a 1,200 kV UHV lab and completed dynamic short-circuit testing on ten units, and Atlanta holds eight NABL-accredited labs — test capacity is becoming a moat rather than a queue.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the core, bushing and tap-changer stack — CRGO electrical steel (~90% imported, domestic capacity only from FY2028), RIP bushing cores (~95% imported) and advanced OLTCs (>80% imported). India now builds the whole transformer and imports almost none of the finished unit (power-transformer imports fell to ₹177 cr in FY26), but it still buys the heart and the control.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Finished EHV transformers — the largest pool, and still widening: CG Power Power Systems 25.0% PBIT, GE Vernova T&D 28.6% PBT, TARIL 16.1% EBITDA, Hitachi Energy 16.0% operational EBITDA.
  • Pricing power is now split by voltage — 400/765 kV and HVDC command it; 220 kV and industrial transformers compete on price, and industry-wide margins are only 8–10% (CRISIL).
  • Backward integration (CTC, pressboard, bushings, lamination processing) is the margin lever, but it converts a high-margin OEM into a low-margin component maker: Vilas runs a 7.1% PAT margin on laminations.
  • OLTC retains the highest switching cost in the chain on the smallest revenue base.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • Hitachi Energy India NSE:POWERINDIA — Q1 FY27 revenue ₹2,494 cr, backlog ₹32,222 cr
  • GE Vernova T&D India NSE:GVT&D — Q2 FY27 revenue ₹2,027 cr, PBT 28.6%, backlog ₹21,443 cr
  • CG Power NSE:CGPOWER — Power Systems Q2 FY27 revenue ₹1,769 cr, PBIT 25.0%
  • TARIL NSE:TARIL — pure-play, Q2 FY27 revenue ₹709 cr, EBITDA 16.1%, backlog ₹7,237 cr
  • Voltamp / Shilchar / Atlanta / Bharat Bijlee — pure-plays growing 23–55% with margins compressing 2–10 pp
Pools that leak to imports
  • CRGO core — ~90% imported; domestic from FY2028.
  • RIP bushing cores — ~95% imported; localised 420/550 kV only in Q4 FY28.
  • OLTC — >80% imported, no qualified Indian maker.
  • Distribution transformers — the one finished-goods leak, imports +89% to ₹2,085 cr in FY26.

economics20%
OEM economics are strong but no longer uniformly so: CG Power's Power Systems segment earned 25.0% PBIT on ₹1,769 cr in Q2 FY27 (up 430 bps YoY), GE Vernova T&D ran a 28.6% PBT margin, TARIL 16.1% EBITDA and Hitachi Energy 16.0% in Q1 FY27 — while Voltamp, the most industrial-exposed name, lost 543 bps of gross margin and 393 bps of EBITDA margin in the same quarter on input costs and legacy fixed-price orders. Industry-wide CRISIL put operating margins at only 8–10%, so the returns are concentrated in EHV/utility-facing capacity, not spread across the sector.

competitive15%
The moat is qualification, not capital. Only about half of transformers pass short-circuit testing at the first attempt and 765 kV qualification needs 3–4 years of field performance [REPORTED-T2, Choice Institutional, Oct-2026]; HVDC remains a two-player contest (Hitachi Energy and GE Vernova, with BHEL in consortium). But the field is widening — Voltamp's Jarod EHV plant went live on 7-Oct-2026, CG Power's Sehore plant on 4-Sep-2026, and Atlanta's 765 kV line is running at 7–8% utilisation — so the oligopoly is being contested at 400 kV rather than at 765 kV/HVDC.
4
value_chain10%
India now performs essentially every transformer step from core slitting to impulse testing, and finished power-transformer imports have collapsed to ₹177 cr in FY26 (−60% YoY) — the system-level chain is domestic. The leaks are the same three and they are narrowing unevenly: CRGO (~90% imported, domestic capacity only from FY2028), RIP bushing cores (~95% imported, with four Indian localisers building), and vacuum OLTCs (>80% imported, no qualified Indian maker). Distribution-transformer imports, by contrast, nearly doubled to ₹2,085 cr in FY26.
4
capital5%
Capex per MVA stays modest and brownfield-led — CG Power adds 10,000 MVA for ₹68 cr, TARIL adds ~35,000 MVA for about ₹150 cr — but the model is working-capital heavy on 18–24 month execution cycles (TARIL debt/equity 0.35x, ROCE 23.3%; CG Power annualised ROCE 27%), and the customer book is dominated by state utilities and TBCB EPCs.
3
technology5%
765 kV manufacture is established across five or six makers and 1,200 kV testing exists in-house (CG Power's Sehore line covers 220–1,200 kV; TARIL runs a 1,200 kV UHV lab), but HVDC converter technology sits with two firms and design validation still queues behind scarce accredited short-circuit and impulse capacity — which is why lead time, not know-how, sets the pace.

Current opportunity60.0 / 100
cycle15%
Demand sits at a cyclical high (peak demand hit a record 270.82 GW in summer 2026; 400/765 kV orders are being booked for FY29–30 execution) while ~315 GVA of announced capacity lands across FY27–FY30 — CG Power, TARIL, Voltamp, Bharat Bijlee, Shilchar and Siemens Energy India are all commissioning or expanding. FY26 TBCB ordering was thin (16 schemes vs 45 in FY25) and the HVDC pipeline of ~₹1.0 lakh cr is not yet awarded, so the risk is a margin peak in FY27–28 rather than a demand peak.
