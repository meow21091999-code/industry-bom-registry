Snapshot

Robot software spans on-robot autonomy (SLAM, motion planning, perception), fleet orchestration, digital twins, PLC/OT integration and cloud. India is genuinely strong here — the counterweight to its hardware import gaps — with a large fleet-software cluster and strong engineering-services firms, though most value is still services-led.

Process & equipment
  • On-robot: SLAM stack, motion planner, perception (vision/LiDAR fusion) on ROS2 + edge compute (Jetson/embedded).
  • Fleet: traffic control, task scheduling, VDA5050 interop, WMS/ERP integration.
  • Tools: simulation (Gazebo/Isaac), digital twin, MLOps for edge models.
Bottlenecks
  • Edge-AI compute hardware (Jetson/boards) — imported.
  • Talent: AI, control, integration engineers in short supply.
  • Fragmentation: no unified standards/safety framework.
  • Hardware-software integration — prototypes to production.
Value pools
  • Fleet-management SaaS and RaaS software (ARAPL FleetOps, Vrutti, RovifyLab).
  • Engineering services: Tata Elxsi, Cyient, KPIT.
  • Digital twin and AI-vision are the fast-growing product pools.
Market & growth
  • The software pool (nominal, T3): India robot software was US$223.4 mn in 2023 and is projected at US$1,312.3 mn by 2030 — a 27.7% value CAGR (2024–2030); scope = on-robot control and programming software, simulation/offline programming and fleet-management software [REPORTED-T3: Next Move Strategy Consulting, 24-Feb-2025]. Treated as a single-source estimate, not a consensus.
  • Envelope: India’s whole robotics market was US$1.1 bn in 2025, heading to US$4.6 bn by 2030 — an implied ~33% value CAGR — with imports at 85–90% of robotic component value. [REPORTED-T2: Avendus Capital via Times of India, 28-Oct-2026] [CALCULATED: (4.6/1.1)^(1/5)−1]
  • Volume: India industrial-robot deployments are estimated at ~26,000 units (2025) rising to ~95,000 units by 2035, a 13.69% volume CAGR, against a 12.17% value CAGR over the same period — value trails volume because average unit prices are falling. [REPORTED-T2: Next Move Strategy Consulting, 24-Nov-2025]
  • Drivers: warehouse and quick-commerce automation is the strongest opportunity, with payback on low-cost automation down to 1.5–2 years from several years a decade ago [REPORTED-T2: Avendus Capital]; software is the fastest-growing offering segment within industrial robotics [REPORTED-T2: NextMSC]; and the National Strategy for Robotics targets global leadership by 2030 [REPORTED-T2: IMARC].
Watch items
  • Productisation from services to licensed platforms.
  • National Robotics Mission's software/sovereignty pillar.
  • VDA5050/ISO alignment enabling exports.
Coverage gaps

No listed robotics-software pure-play; the listed names are engineering-services or conglomerate segments. The dense fleet-software cluster is almost entirely private.

Why this is India's strongest layer: software needs no imported magnet or reducer. India's fleet-software cluster, engineering-services depth and IT-hardware integration skill make autonomy the one robotics layer where the value pool is domestically captured — the hardware gaps upstream are the mirror image.
