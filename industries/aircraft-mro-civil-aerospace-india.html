Snapshot

India's commercial fleet is set to triple to ~2,250 aircraft by 2035, but MRO is a services business and civil components a discrete-manufacturing one. India is competent at line and base airframe checks and is scaling component manufacturing, yet ~80% of MRO value still flies to Singapore, Dubai and Europe — because engines and components, not hangars, hold the value.

Process & equipment
  • MRO: hangars, docking, NDT, engine test cells, component benches; C/D checks and redelivery.
  • Components: forging, investment casting, HIP, vacuum heat-treat, 5-axis machining, surface treatment.
  • Gating inputs: aerospace-grade Ti/superalloys, avionics LRUs, OEM licences and data.
Bottlenecks
  • Engine MRO — OEM-controlled parts; resilience YEARS.
  • Component MRO 'missing middle' — ~100% abroad; YEARS.
  • Avionics LRUs & software — imported; MONTHS–YEARS.
  • Certification & skills — CAR-66 engine-CRS anomaly, 25–30% attrition; YEARS.
  • Aerospace-grade materials — newly domestic; MONTHS–YEARS.
Value pools
  • Engine components manufacturing is the cleanest domestic pool — PTC/Aerolloy (melt–cast–forge), Azad, Bharat Forge.
  • Aerostructures (Dynamatic, Aequs) capture build-to-print margin, exported.
  • EWIS/harness (Rossell, DCX) is export-strong and tariff-light.
  • Base airframe MRO stays domestic but leaks margin to imported spares.
  • Engine & component MRO value leaks to OEM shops (Safran, GE, P&W, RR) and global hubs.
  • Airport/SEZ landlords (GMR, Adani) capture the physical-infrastructure rent.
Market & growth
  • India MRO value ~$4.4bn (2025) → ~$5.7bn (2030) [REPORTED-T2] · value CAGR ~5.3% [CALCULATED].
  • Govt/NITI series: $1.7bn (2021) → $4.0bn (2031), ~8.9% value CAGR [REPORTED-T1].
  • Airbus view: broader airframe+engine+component pool ~$3bn → $9.5bn by 2035 [REPORTED-T2].
  • Volume: fleet ~880 → ~2,250 aircraft (2025→2035), ~9.9% p.a. [CALCULATED]; last-year MRO value +12.5% [REPORTED-T2].
  • Scope conflict — sources differ on what counts; treat the range, not a point [CONFLICTING DATA].
Watch items
  • Safran SAESI Hyderabad operational 2026 (300 LEAP visits/yr) — first OEM engine MRO in India.
  • IndiGo–CFM 1,000+ LEAP MoU (Jul-2026) with engine-MRO support.
  • Draft DAP 2026 'Owned by India' — IC 50→60%, offsets narrowed to MRO & tech.
  • Budget 2026-27 Nil BCD on components; MRO tool BCD waiver extended to 31-Mar-2028.
Coverage gaps

No listed pure-play in certification/AME training, cabin seats or component-MRO accessories. Engine MRO is OEM-captured; component MRO is largely unlisted (Horizon, Haveus, Magnum).

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Engine MRO & OEM-controlled parts~50% of MRO value, ~80% of engine cost is OEM materials; entry needs an OEM licence. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

OEM-partnered engine shopsSafran SAESI (300 LEAP visits/yr) and IndiGo–CFM make the OEM the partner, not the competitor.

Risk

Component MRO 'missing middle'Fuel systems, pneumatics, actuation, avionics LRUs, landing-gear accessories — 'nearly hundred per cent' abroad. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Component repair depthWheels/brakes, NDT and landing-gear shops (Sika, Horizon, Haveus, Magnum) are the nearest domestic step.

Risk

Avionics LRUs & softwareHigh-end LRUs, FADEC modules and data licences remain outside India. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

Avionics repair + DOAThales Gurugram, and India's avionics houses (Data Patterns, BEL) plus DGCA Design Organisation Approvals.

Risk

Certification & skillsCAR-66 engine-CRS anomaly and 25–30% attrition; certified engineers cannot be bought quickly. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

DGCA/EASA work-share & trainingSafran trains 100 technicians/yr; 87+ DGCA AMOs; mutual-recognition is the lever.

Risk

Aerospace-grade materialsTi sponge, superalloy and composite prepreg were largely imported. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

Melt–cast–forge integrationPTC/Aerolloy (VIM+VAR+4,500t press), MIDHANI and Bharat Forge's ring mill close the loop.

Deepest hidden dependency: the OEM-controlled engine parts, avionics LRUs and their data licences. India is building hangars and test cells faster than it is building component and software sovereignty — infrastructure without control of the maintenance pipeline.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Engine components manufacturing — the cleanest domestic pool (PTC/Aerolloy, Azad, Bharat Forge).
  • Aerostructures — build-to-print export margin (Dynamatic, Aequs).
  • EWIS/harness — export-strong, tariff-light (Rossell, DCX).
  • Airport/SEZ rent — the physical layer (GMR, Adani).
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • PTC Industries — Ti/superalloy melt–cast–forge for LEAP.
  • Azad Engineering — turbine blades & actuator assemblies; >₹6,500 cr order book.
  • Dynamatic / Aequs — aerostructures at scale.
  • Rossell Techsys — EWIS for Boeing/LM; ~98% export.
  • HAL — defence ROH + LEAP forged parts.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Engine MRO — OEM shops & global hubs.
  • Component MRO — ~100% abroad.
  • Avionics LRUs, FADEC, composite prepreg, Ti sponge.
  • Spares & rotables — thin domestic pool, long TAT.
