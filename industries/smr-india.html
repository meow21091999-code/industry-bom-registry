Snapshot

India’s indigenous SMR programme — BSMR-200 (220 MWe PWR), the SMR-55 and the newly blueprinted BSMR-300, plus a 5 MWth HTGCR for hydrogen — under the ₹20,000 cr Nuclear Energy Mission, targeting five units by 2033 against a 100 GWe-by-2047 goal. The core technology sits at pilot/demo stage; no Indian SMR is operating. The AEC chairman (Oct-2026) expects ₹7.5 lakh cr of private investment and 30 GW from private players by 2047.

Process & equipment
  • The nuclear island is fabricated to order, not mass-produced — ApuRVA-alloy RPV forgings and steam generators are built on heavy presses.
  • Zircaloy-clad UO₂ fuel is made at NFC’s ore-to-core plant; reactor C&I uses the indigenous NUCON PLC/SCADA stack.
  • Heavy forging and steam generators are mature (PHWR-proven); SMR module integration is early-commercial at best. NPCIL commissioned an indigenous pump testbed at Tarapur in 2026.
Bottlenecks
  • Certification-gated vendor base — a small pool of ASME-qualified suppliers gates the schedule (years).
  • Heavy forging — a single integrated nuclear-grade shop (L&T–NPCIL JV).
  • Reactor C&I — ECIL is the sole integrated house and is unlisted.
  • Fuel-cycle front-end, Class-1 skilled trades, and the liability/financing regime.
Value pools
  • The nuclear island and fuel cycle capture the most value and the deepest moats.
  • Turbine island is BHEL-dominated; C&I is high-margin but unlisted; EPC is thin-margin.
  • KSB’s nuclear order book is ₹1,235 cr (Jun-2026); ASHVINI floated a ₹28,000 cr nuclear-island EPC package for 4×700 MWe Mahi Banswara.
  • Nuclear capex is ~₹25 cr/MW (AEC chief, Oct-2026); BSMR-300 target ~₹30 cr/MW. Localisation is the direction of travel (ApuRVA, indigenised primary coolant pumps).
Market & growth
  • There is no revenue market for Indian SMRs yet — no unit is under construction, so the addressable frame is the nuclear build-out: installed capacity 8.78 GW (Oct-2026) → ~22 GW by 2031-32 → 100 GW by 2047 [REPORTED-T1: DAE/PIB and Lok Sabha answer]. That is a ~16.5% CAGR to 2031-32 and ~12.3% to 2047 [CALCULATED].
  • Capital behind it: ₹15 lakh cr to add 91 GW by 2047 [REPORTED-T1: CEA chairman, 14-Oct-2026]. [CONFLICTING DATA]: TERI puts the requirement at ₹23–25 lakh cr and NITI Aayog / DAE at ~USD 210–217 bn; the spread is scope — whether fuel-cycle, grid and decommissioning are included.
  • SMR-specific commitment: ₹20,000 cr Nuclear Energy Mission, five indigenous SMRs by 2033, on sanctioned project costs of ₹5,960 cr for BSMR-200 (220 MWe — ₹27.1 cr/MW), ₹7,000 cr for a twin SMR-55 (110 MWe — ₹63.6 cr/MW) and ₹320 cr for the 5 MWth HTGCR [REPORTED-T1: PIB, Jul-2026; CALCULATED for the per-MW figures]. Those three units are the entire near-term SMR market.
  • Drivers: the 100 GW-by-2047 mission with 46 GW reserved for PSUs, private players and JVs; the SHANTI Act opening the sector to private participation; and brownfield siting for SMRs at retiring fossil plants and captive industrial sites.
Watch items
  • SMR-55 DPR released 1-Oct-2026 (NPCIL/BARC) — a planning milestone, not construction approval.
  • BSMR-300 DPR released 14-Aug-2026; BSMR-200 awaiting government A&F sanction (AEC/ETG cleared).
  • AEC chief, 11-Oct-2026: ₹7.5 lakh cr private investment and 30 GW by 2047 expected.
  • NPCIL BSR RFP drew a single proposal (Hindalco) by the 31-Mar-2026 deadline.
Coverage gaps
  • Reactor design/IP (BARC/NPCIL), fuel (NFC), uranium (UCIL), zircon (IREL), heavy water (HWB) and reactor C&I (ECIL) are all unlisted. No NSE/BSE pure-play SMR developer.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Certification-gated vendor baseClass-1 nuclear qualification takes years; a small pool of ASME-qualified suppliers gates the schedule. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

N-stamp incumbents scaleL&T, Kirloskar Brothers and KSB can capture a widening order book as fleet mode ramps — KSB’s nuclear order book already stands at ₹1,235 cr (Jun-2026).

Risk

Heavy forging capacityA single integrated nuclear-grade forging shop (L&T–NPCIL JV) is the pinch point for large RPV/SG forgings. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Indigenous ApuRVA alloyAlready developed with Indian industry — an export and SMR-module opportunity.

Risk

Reactor C&I electronicsECIL is effectively the sole fully-integrated nuclear C&I house — and it is unlisted. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Indigenous NUCON platformSanctions-tested and could extend to SMR digital I&C — a private-sector white space.

Risk

Fuel-cycle front-endUranium, enrichment, zircaloy and heavy water sit in unlisted DAE units; some UOC is imported. Resilience: months–years.

Opportunity

NFC / UCIL / IREL expansionPlus thorium and ANEEL-fuel optionality (L&T–CCTE) — the import-substitution case. PFBR first criticality (Apr-2026) advances the thorium route.

Risk

Skilled tradesClass-1 welders, NDT inspectors and QA engineers bind tighter than materials and cannot be bought quickly.

Opportunity

Training pipelinesBHEL / MTAR / L&T qualification pipelines are a durable moat for incumbents.

Risk

Liability & financingPrivate capital stayed cautious on first projects under the old liability regime. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

SHANTI Act 2025Graded liability (₹100–3,000 cr), supplier liability removed, statutory AERB and private participation unlock SPV models — AEC expects 30 GW from private players by 2047.

Deepest hidden dependency: the qualified-vendor base and the unlisted fuel cycle. Most of the critical path — reactor design/IP (BARC/NPCIL), fuel fabrication (NFC), uranium (UCIL), zircon (IREL), heavy water (HWB) and reactor C&I (ECIL) — runs through entities with no NSE/BSE listing. The technology is indigenous; the investability is not.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • The nuclear island and fuel cycle capture the most value and the deepest moats.
  • Turbine island is BHEL-dominated; C&I is high-margin but unlisted; EPC is thin-margin.
  • Nuclear capex is ~₹25 cr/MW (AEC chief, Oct-2026); the BSMR-300 target is ~₹30 cr/MW.
  • Localisation is the direction of travel (ApuRVA alloy, indigenised primary coolant pumps, NPCIL’s Tarapur pump testbed).
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • KSB Limited NSE: KSB — sole domestic supplier of Primary Coolant Pumps (RSR 400/2) on 18 of 24 operating reactors; nuclear order book ₹1,235 cr (Jun-2026); ₹500 cr Kaiga 5&6 order; Shirwal capacity +20%
  • MTAR Technologies NSE: MTAR — fuel machining heads, coolant channel assemblies, drive mechanisms and water-lubricated bearings; DAE supplier 45+ years
  • Walchandnagar Industries NSE: WALCHANNAG — calandria, end shields, fuel magazines and dump tanks; nuclear was ~40% of OEM revenue in FY24
  • Larsen & Toubro NSE: LT — CRDMs, fuel rod sub-assemblies, fuel transfer arms and nuclear-island EPC; nuclear is a small share of consolidated revenue
  • BHEL NSE: BHEL — 44 nuclear steam generators — the highest by any Indian maker; reactor headers and the turbine island
  • ISGEC Heavy Engineering NSE: ISGEC — heat-exchanger vendor in the NPCIL procurement list
  • MIDHANI NSE: MIDHANI — special stainless steel plates (304L NAG) for the DAE
Pools that leak / stay unlisted
  • Reactor design/IP (BARC/NPCIL), fuel (NFC), uranium (UCIL), zircon (IREL), heavy water (HWB) and reactor C&I (ECIL) are all unlisted. No NSE/BSE pure-play SMR developer.
  • ASHVINI’s ₹28,000 cr nuclear-island EPC package (4×700 MWe Mahi Banswara) is the largest near-term domestic order pool; bids closed Sep-2026, technical bids opened Oct-2026.
