Snapshot
  • India's ammunition base is the volume core of its defence industry — roughly 158 mn small-calibre, 5 mn medium-calibre and 2 mn artillery rounds a year — and the pool moving fastest from DPSU monopoly to private supply.
  • Munitions India still dominates at ₹10,954 cr of FY26 revenue; around it a new private tier of shell-body forgers, energetics makers and loitering-munition firms has formed in under three years.
Process & equipment
  • Small calibre: brass strip → blanking and cupping → draw → heading → primer and bullet seating on high-speed LAP lines.
  • Large calibre: billet → forge → machine → heat-treat for the empty body, then melt-pour or press-fill, fuze and charge assembly, then DGQA proof.
  • Energetics: nitration and stabilisation, ball-powder or extruded propellant, primer and initiator assembly — the equipment here is imported.
Bottlenecks
  • Nitrocellulose and propellant powder: ~70% of defence-grade NC imported, one domestic source, propellant prices ~5× their 2019 level.
  • Primers and initiators: single-source domestically, imported at scale — a hard veto on any filling line.
  • Filling and LAP capacity lags body forging; CAG found ₹1,053 cr of ammunition unissuable because sister factories fell short on ₹276 cr of propellant.
  • Licensed capacity sits idle: of 40–46 explosives licences only ~15–17% of holders have built plants.
Value pools
  • Filling and energetics keep the margin — scarce, qualified, and priced by a single buyer but with years of booked capacity.
  • Shell bodies are the fastest-growing but thinnest pool: five listed forgers entered in twelve months, prices LME-indexed at ₹20,000–26,100 a unit.
  • Fuzes and base-bleed units are the value-dense export line — a base-bleed body exports near ₹69,000 against ₹20,000–26,000 plain.
  • Brass strip and cups leak to three unlisted specialists (Rashtriya Metal, Mittal, Consolidated Coin); the listed exposure is copper cathode, one step upstream.
  • The export premium is the real prize: Europe's 155 mm shortage took defence exports to ₹38,424 cr in FY26, +62.66%.
Market & growth
  • Value: 4.93% CAGR to 2031 at USD 929 mn → 1,240 mn [REPORTED-T2, Ken Research, domestic consumption only]; 10.8% CAGR to 2031 at USD 2.45 bn → 4.44 bn [REPORTED-T2, MarketsandMarkets, wider scope].
  • KPMG's ₹7,057 cr (2023) → ₹11,981 cr (2032) series states 4.93% but its own endpoints imply ~6.1% [CALCULATED] — treat the figure as a range.
  • Volume, nominal: small calibre 158 → 225 mn rounds 2023-32 = 3.9% CAGR; medium 5.1 → 7.8 mn = 4.7%; heavy 1.8 → 2.8 mn = 4.9% [CALCULATED from KPMG/IISS].
  • Last year, value: defence exports +62.66%; MIL revenue +33.35%; Solar defence revenue +94% — price, mix and export all pulling the same way.
Watch items
  • 19-Nov-2026: Army RFP for 37,680 × 155 mm HE boat-tail shells closes (EMD ₹20.59 cr).
  • Jan-2027: Adani medium-calibre line (8 mn rounds/yr) starts; primer and propellant plants due during 2027.
  • Sep-2027: Agneyastra Energetics' pour-fill facility completion target.
  • Standing: the Army's push to convert ~40 idle explosives licences, and any movement on a second domestic nitrocellulose source.
Coverage gaps
  • No listed player in primers, nitrocellulose, fuze electronics, proof ranges, ammunition machinery or demilitarisation — the highest-barrier nodes are entirely unlisted or state-owned.

How to read this page
  • Level 0 is ammunition and cartridges as consumed by the Indian armed forces, CAPFs and police, plus the export book. Architecture is hybrid: discrete assembly (bodies, cases, fuzes) wrapped around a process industry (energetics chemistry).
  • Red nodes are HIGH priority by cost share, concentration, capital or import-substitution relevance; amber nodes are MEDIUM. The tree is clickable in section 03.
What sits underneath
  • Eight Level-1 systems: small calibre, medium calibre, large calibre, propellants/explosives/primers, cases and fuze hardware, filling and proof, loitering munitions, and the machinery/materials/aftermarket layer.
  • Two of them — energetics and primers — gate everything downstream, which is why the whole chain's resilience is measured in months-to-years rather than weeks.

05Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunitydual-readable
Risk

Nitrocellulose & propellant powder~70% of defence-grade NC is imported and the balance comes from one domestic source; propellant went from under US$10/kg (2019) to ~US$50/kg (2025). Resilience: months to years.

Opportunity

A second NC line is a marketVajrashakti's 800 tpa line (2,400 tpa planned) targets roughly a quarter of the shortfall against a ~4,780 t/yr need; a domestic NC and ball-powder base would also serve European demand.

Risk

Primers & initiatorsImported at scale from the US and Europe, single-source at home; a primer shortfall stops a filling line as surely as a propellant one. Resilience: months to years.

Opportunity

25,000 tpa of primer and propellantAdani's committed plant, plus Agneyastra's energetics complex, convert a strategic hole into domestic capacity by 2027 — and into export supply thereafter.

Risk

Filling capacity lags body capacityCAG found ₹1,053 cr of ammunition unissuable because sister factories fell short on ₹276 cr of propellant: a faster forging line adds no replenishment without filling and energetics behind it. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Filling is the margin nodePrivate pour-fill and LAP capacity (Solar, Premier, Agneyastra) captures the highest value-add in the chain and is the least contested by imports.

Risk

Licensed but idle capacityOf 40–46 explosives licences issued, only ~15–17% of holders have built plants — blocked by land, water, ₹200–400 cr of imported equipment and order visibility. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

The conversion playConverting idle licences into operating plants (or a state-built common primer/propellant facility) is the cheapest capacity India can add — and the Army has formally asked MoD for it.

Risk

Materials baseAntimony is 100% imported, copper ore and lead are import-dependent, toluene capacity is thin, and brass cups are made by three unlisted specialists. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Upstream substitutionHindustan Copper's cathode, Hindalco's strip and India's brass-scrap recycling can displace imported strip; antimony and lead remain the genuinely hard gaps.

Risk

Qualification and proof throughputDGQA acceptance and range time gate every new line; five forgers are queued behind the same proof capacity. Resilience: months.

Opportunity

Third-party test capacityPrivate, accredited proof and environmental-test capacity would shorten the qualify-to-bill cycle for every entrant — currently an unserved market.

06Value pools & cost waterfallwhere value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Filling and energetics: scarce, qualified, and booked years ahead — the highest-margin node in the chain.
  • Fuzes and base-bleed: value-dense export hardware, roughly 2.5–3× the price of a plain shell body.
  • Shell bodies: the fastest-growing revenue pool and the thinnest margin — five listed entrants, LME-indexed pricing.
  • Small calibre: largest volume, lowest value per round, brass and primer inputs dominating cost.
  • Exports: the premium pool, created by Europe's 155 mm shortage rather than by Indian demand.
Who captures it (NSE/BSE)
  • Energetics and filling: Solar Industries (₹2,634 cr FY26 defence revenue, ₹18,000 cr defence backlog), Premier Explosives (₹1,569 cr order book).
  • Bodies and forging: Goodluck, Balu Forge, Hilton, Sigma, Bharat Forge (KSSL).
  • Integration at scale: Adani Enterprises (via Adani Defence) and Reliance Infrastructure (via Reliance Defence) — both conglomerate wrappers, immaterial to consolidated revenue today.
  • Unlisted and decisive: Munitions India (₹10,954 cr revenue, ₹28,939 cr order book), SMPP, Vajrashakti, Economic Explosives' parentage, Rashtriya Metal, Mittal, Consolidated Coin.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Nitrocellulose (~70% of need), primers and initiators, antimony, and much of the copper ore and lead.
  • Ammunition-specific machinery: cupping and draw presses, nitration plant, LAP automation — European and Chinese.
  • Fuze electronics and proximity sensors, where no Indian listed supplier is disclosed.
