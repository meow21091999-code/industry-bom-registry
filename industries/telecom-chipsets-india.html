Snapshot

Telecom chipsets — baseband SoCs, RF transceivers and the RF front-end modules inside every handset, router and radio — are the highest-value node in the telecom stack and the one India imports almost entirely. India designs some of this silicon (Saankhya, Signalchip, WiSig, Silizium) and now packages chips at scale, but it neither fabs the dies nor owns the design toolchain. Scope: India, NSE/BSE. Re-analysed 2026-10-25 (delta pass over the 2026-10-08 entry).

Process & equipment
  • Design (EDA/IP) → wafer fab (still absent in India; Dholera due 2028) → OSAT assembly, test and packaging — the only reachable domestic layers today.
  • Key machinery: EDA tool grid, mask/MPW prototyping, wafer sort and final-test, wire-bond and flip-chip bonders, burn-in — largely imported; the first Indian OSAT lines are now running at Sanand, Surat and Mohali.
  • RF front-end needs compound-semiconductor (GaAs/GaN) epitaxy and SAW/BAW filter lines — none in India yet.
Bottlenecks
  • Baseband / modem SoC chokehold — Qualcomm, MediaTek and Unisoc hold ~75–80% of India smartphone SoC value. Resilience: YEARS–STRUCTURAL.
  • RF front-end & compound semiconductors — no GaAs/GaN substrate, epitaxy or SAW/BAW line. Resilience: YEARS.
  • No commercial fab — Dholera (28–110 nm) commissions 2028; every Indian-designed chip is fabbed abroad. Resilience: YEARS.
  • EDA tools and core IP — foreign duopoly (Synopsys/Cadence/Siemens) plus Arm. Resilience: STRUCTURAL.
  • Design-to-revenue gap — 24 DLI projects, 25 tape-outs, but almost no commercial silicon revenue yet; sovereign demand is thin.
Value pools
  • Chip design is the main value driver — up to ~50% of value-add and 20–50% of BOM cost — and India’s listed exposure is still thin.
  • Value sits in the baseband SoC, the RF FEM and the RF filter, all imported; India captures design services, OSAT packaging and RAN system integration.
  • The localisation prize is the RF front-end (compound semi) and OSAT/packaging — both now the focus of Semicon 2.0.
  • OSAT costs in India run ~20–30% below peers — a genuine cost edge in the one layer India can scale fastest.
Market & growth
  • India semiconductor market US$62 bn (2026) → US$155 bn (2031), ~20% CAGR (value, nominal) [REPORTED-T3, Kotak MF]. A second scope: ~US$64 bn (2026) → US$200 bn (2035) [REPORTED-T3, EY-IESA]. Scopes shown, not averaged.
  • Telecom is the second-largest end-segment of that demand after mobile: ~US$13.2 bn by FY30 [REPORTED-T3, IDTA].
  • Volume/import read: 90–95% of India’s semiconductor consumption is imported; imports were US$30.3 bn in FY25, a 23% CAGR from FY17 [REPORTED-T3]. OSAT/ATMP capacity 15 mn chips/day (CY26) → 48 mn/day (CY28) [REPORTED-T3].
  • Growth is demand- and policy-led, not price-led; the memory-price squeeze cut India smartphone shipments −20% YoY in Q3 2026 (a decade low), so unit volumes and market value are moving differently.
Watch items
  • Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 cr) notified Aug-2026 — design IP must be co-owned by a state agency and stay in India. [LIVE]
  • IMC 2026: government targets indigenous alternatives for 10–20 critical imported network technologies, chipsets included; 136 TTDF R&D projects (~₹500 cr). [LIVE]
  • Tata–PSMC Dholera fab on track for first wafer in 2028; Tata Jagiroad OSAT pilot output reported 3-Oct-2026. [LIVE]
  • Kaynes and CG Semi Sanand OSAT lines in commercial production; WiSig opened commercial orders for a made-in-India O-RAN 32TR Massive-MIMO radio (volume 2027). [LIVE]
Coverage gaps
  • No NSE/BSE pure-play across baseband, RFIC, FEM, filters, substrates, fab or EDA. Nearest listed adjacents: OSAT/packaging (SPEL, Kaynes, CG Power), design services (MosChip) and RAN systems (Tejas, Reliance/Jio).

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Baseband / modem chokeholdQualcomm, MediaTek and Unisoc supply over 80% of India’s smartphone SoCs (MediaTek ~45% of units in Q3 2026, Qualcomm dominant in premium); design-in is locked by IP, reference designs and band certification.

Opportunity

Policy-funded designSaankhya’s 5G SoC (DLI-approved), Signalchip’s 5G/4G base-station SoCs, WiSig’s NB-IoT SoC and the TTDF Chipset Mission’s 5G modem + RF brief are the first serious domestic attempts; Semicon 2.0 puts design first among six pillars.

Risk

RF front-end & compound semiconductorsGaAs/GaN substrates, SAW/BAW filters and power amplifiers are largely imported; India has no substrate, epitaxy or filter line, so the highest-value block of the radio BOM leaks abroad.

Opportunity

First domestic RF designsSilizium Circuits’ GaAs LEO/GNSS and 5G LNA/FEM designs (DCIS + DLI + TTDF funded), Axiro’s GaN lines and a 20-company Indian fabless RF cohort point at the RF front-end as the most reachable import-substitution prize.

Risk

Packaging was absentIndia historically had no advanced packaging; chip assembly and test were done entirely offshore, adding cost and lead time and capping domestic value-add.

Opportunity

OSAT build-out is now realFive of twelve ISM plants are in commercial production — Micron (Feb-2026), Kaynes Semicon (Mar-2026), CG Semi (Jul-2026), CDIL and Suchi (Sep-2026) — alongside incumbent SPEL. Indian OSAT costs run ~20–30% below peers.

Risk

EDA & IP dependencyDesign flows, reusable IP cores and tools are foreign-controlled (Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens EDA, Arm), carrying export-control and pricing risk; India supplies design services but owns little of the tool and core-IP layer.

Opportunity

Design-services depth → owned IPMosChip, Tata Elxsi and the design-services base, with ~20% of the world’s chip-design engineers, are what DLI (24 projects, 25 tape-outs, 140+ IP cores) and Semicon 2.0’s state-co-owned-IP rule aim to convert into owned silicon.

Risk

Fab gap & thin sovereign demandThere is no commercial fab in India yet — Dholera commissions only in 2028 — and domestic anchor demand is weak, pushing Tata, Kaynes and CG Semi to chase overseas customers instead.

Opportunity

Mature-node fab and a widening ecosystemTata–PSMC Dholera (28–110 nm, 50k wafers/month) targets telecom-relevant mature nodes from 2028; Applied Materials (US$5 bn), Lam Research (~₹10,000 cr), Fujifilm, JSR, Sumitomo and INOX are building the equipment/materials layer India lacked.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the design toolchain, the foundry and the compound-semiconductor substrate. Even a chip fully designed in India is still built on foreign EDA, fabbed on a foreign wafer, and — until the Sanand/Surat/Mohali OSAT lines, only recently — packaged abroad.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Where value is captured
  • Chip design is the main value driver — up to ~50% of value-add and 20–50% of BOM cost, and 30–35% of global semiconductor sales via the fabless segment.
  • Value sits in the baseband SoC, RF FEM and RF filter — all imported; India’s capture is design services, OSAT packaging and RAN system integration.
  • The localisation prize is the RF front-end (compound semi) and OSAT/advanced packaging; Indian OSAT costs run ~20–30% below peers.
  • Localisation path: OSAT/packaging → RF front-end design → baseband/RAN SoC → mature-node fab.
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • SPEL Semiconductor NSE/BSE: SPELS — [REPORTED] India’s oldest IC assembly & test house (BSE-listed since 1988); wafer sort, assembly, final test and turnkey services.
  • Kaynes Technology NSE/BSE: KAYNES — [REPORTED] Wholly-owned Kaynes Semicon runs the Sanand OSAT, in commercial production since 31-Mar-2026; a minor segment of the listed entity.
  • CG Power & Industrial Solutions NSE/BSE: CGPOWER — [REPORTED] Unlisted subsidiary Axiro designs RF/mmWave and GaN front-end; the CG–Renesas–Stars OSAT (CG Semi) began commercial packaging 4-Jul-2026. Minor segment.
  • Tejas Networks NSE/BSE: TEJASNET — [REPORTED] Owns Saankhya Labs (unlisted, non-material); SDR/RAN chipsets, indigenous 4G/5G RAN; DoT design-led PLI approved.
  • MosChip Technologies NSE/BSE: MOSCHIP — [REPORTED] Semiconductor and system design services — SoC design, semiconductor IP and turnkey ASIC.
  • Reliance Industries (Jio Platforms) NSE/BSE: RELIANCE — [REPORTED + INFERENCE] Built an ~80% indigenous 5G radio/core stack; the underlying silicon is sourced from NXP / Intel / Qualcomm.
Pools that leak to imports
  • No listed pure-play across baseband, RFIC, FEM, filters, substrates, fab or EDA — nearest adjacents: OSAT/packaging (SPEL, Kaynes, CG Power), design services (MosChip), RAN systems (Tejas, Reliance).
  • Bottleneck nodes: Baseband SoC & Modem, RF Front-End Module (FEM), Power Amplifier (GaAs/GaN), RF Filter (SAW/BAW), Compound-Semiconductor Substrate, OSAT/Advanced Packaging, EDA Tools & IP.
  • ~90–95% of India’s semiconductor consumption is imported; the telecom-chipset import bill is ~US$4 bn/yr and the TTDF mission exists to close it.
