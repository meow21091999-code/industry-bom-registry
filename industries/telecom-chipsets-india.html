00 Digest short summary · full detail in the sections below
Snapshot

Telecom chipsets — baseband SoCs and RF front-end modules, the silicon inside every radio, router and handset. Almost fully imported; no NSE/BSE pure-play. Scope: India, NSE/BSE.

Process & equipment
  • Design (EDA/IP) → wafer fab (absent in India) → OSAT packaging/test.
  • India's reachable layers are fabless design and OSAT assembly; compound-semi substrates (GaAs/GaN) are imported.
Bottlenecks
  • No listed pure-play.
  • Baseband / modem chokehold (Qualcomm/MediaTek/Unisoc).
  • RF front-end and compound semi — no GaAs/GaN substrate or SAW/BAW line.
  • Packaging only now emerging (OSAT); EDA & IP dependency; no fab.
Value pools
  • The highest-value node in the whole telecom stack — and it leaks entirely to imports.
  • Domestic value-add is design services and OSAT.
  • Localisation prize: the RF front-end is the most reachable layer.
Watch items
  • TTDF chipset mission progress.
  • Kaynes OSAT ramp.
  • India fab / ATMP projects.
  • DLI / C2S funding.
Coverage gaps

No listed pure-play across baseband, RFIC, FEM, filters, substrates and EDA — nearest adjacents: OSAT/design services (Kaynes, CG Power, SPEL, MosChip, Tejas).

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

No listed pure-playIndia's telecom-chipset value chain has no NSE/BSE pure-play; investors can only reach it through indirect or conglomerate exposures.

Opportunity

Listed adjacents are formingSPEL (OSAT), Kaynes (OSAT/ATMP), CG Power (Axiro design + CG Semi), Tejas (Saankhya chipsets) and MosChip (design services) are the emerging access points.

Risk

Baseband / modem chokeholdQualcomm, MediaTek and Unisoc supply over 80% of India's smartphone SoCs; design-in is locked by IP, reference designs and band certification.

Opportunity

Policy-funded designSaankhya's 5G SoC (DLI-approved), WiSig's NB-IoT SoC and the TTDF Chipset Mission's 5G modem + RF brief are the first serious domestic attempts.

Risk

RF front-end & compound semiGaAs/GaN substrates, SAW/BAW filters and power amplifiers are largely imported; India has no substrate or filter line.

Opportunity

First domestic RF designsSilizium Circuits' GaAs 5G LNA/FEM (DCIS + DLI + TTDF funded) and Axiro's GaN lines point at the RF front-end as the most reachable import-substitution prize.

Risk

Packaging was absentIndia historically had no advanced packaging; chip assembly and test were done entirely offshore, adding cost and lead time.

Opportunity

OSAT build-outKaynes Semicon Sanand (India's first packaged multi-chip module, Oct 2025), CG Semi, Micron and Tata ATMP — alongside incumbent SPEL — are building the back-end.

Risk

EDA & IP dependencyDesign flows, reusable IP cores and tools are foreign-controlled (Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens EDA, Arm), carrying export-control and pricing risk.

Opportunity

Design-services depthMosChip, Tata Elxsi and HCL, with roughly 1.25 lakh chip engineers and 3,000+ advanced designs, are a base the TTDF mission aims to convert into owned IP.

Risk

Fab gapThere is no commercial leading-edge fab in India, so chipsets must be fabricated abroad (TSMC and others): "designed in India" is not yet "made in India".

Opportunity

Mature-node fabs comingTata-PSMC Dholera (28–65 nm) and CG Semi are aimed at telecom-relevant mature nodes, with initial production expected around 2028–29.

Deepest hidden import dependency: the design toolchain, the foundry, and the compound-semiconductor substrate. Even a chip fully designed in India is still built on foreign EDA, fabbed on a foreign wafer, and — until recently — packaged abroad.
