Snapshot

Cell manufacturing is the largest value pool in the battery chain and India's widest import gap: ₹41,667 cr of cells imported in FY26 against roughly 1 GWh installed of the 40 GWh awarded under PLI-ACC. LFP dominates BESS; the binding constraints are materials, machinery and scale — not demand.

Process & equipment
  • Process train: slurry mixing → coating & drying → calendering → slitting/die-cutting → stacking or winding → tab welding → electrolyte filling → formation → ageing → grading.
  • Cell-line equipment is ~55–60% of a 5 GWh plant's cost and >75% of global capacity is Chinese (Wuxi Lead, Yinghe, Hangke, Hymson).
  • India's live value-add remains module, pack and container assembly; cell making is four commissioning projects, not an industry yet.
Bottlenecks
  • Cathode & anode — ~90% of LFP CAM and >90% of graphitisation sit in China; both are on its restricted-export list.
  • Cell-line machinery — the equipment categories are separately controlled; the legal suspension text still reads 10 Nov 2026 though the political truce runs to 10 Jan 2027.
  • Technology licences — commissioning support and process know-how are the gate, not just machines.
  • Scale economics — ₹8–12/Wh against ₹6–7/Wh imported; breakeven around 10 GWh.
  • Execution — 1 GWh installed of 40 GWh awarded, zero incentive claimed, and the 10 GWh grid-scale tender slipped to 10 Nov 2026.
Value pools
  • Cell-line machinery — the strongest pool in the chain (pricing power, cash conversion, switching costs) and the one with no Indian listed pure-play; it leaks in full.
  • Cathode CAM (35–45% of material cost) — Himadri (HSCL, first 2,000 tpa by Q3 FY27), Gujarat Fluorochemicals (FLUOROCHEM), Tata Chemicals.
  • Anode AAM — HEG Advanced Materials (HEGAM, 20,000 tpa COD Jun-2027, 44,000 tpa by Mar-2029); Epsilon and TACC unlisted.
  • Cells — Ola (OLAELEC), Exide (EXIDEIND), Amara Raja (ARE&M), Waaree (WAAREEENER), Reliance (RELIANCE); the largest revenue pool and the thinnest margin.
  • Separator & electrolyte — TCPL (TCPLPACK, ₹125 cr line, early 2028); Neogen (NEOGEN) + GFL; electrolyte is the one input routinely made in India today.
Market & growth
  • Demand, volume: India's advanced-chemistry cell demand was 28 GWh in 2025, projected at ~272 GWh by FY2030 — a 57.6% five-year volume CAGR [CALCULATED from the IESA/IEEFA series, 2026]. Scope: ACC/Li-ion cells across EVs (~60%) and stationary storage (~40%); lead-acid excluded.
  • Supply, volume: 1 GWh installed of 40 GWh awarded [REPORTED-T1: Ministry of Heavy Industries, Feb-2026], or ~2 GWh on the wider commissioned count [REPORTED-T2: IESA/CES, Jul-2026], against 178 GWh announced outside PLI [REPORTED-T2: BESS News, 26-Oct-2026]. The gap, not the demand, is the market.
  • Value, nominal: India's Li-ion battery market ran US$5.61 bn in 2025 → US$6.73 bn in 2026 → US$15.17 bn by 2031, a 17.65% CAGR (2026–31) [REPORTED-T2: Mordor Intelligence, Jul-2026]; last-year growth 20.0% [CALCULATED]. A second house models 22.0% to 2032 [CONFLICTING DATA: Ken Research, Aug-2026]. Three-year horizon: [UNAVAILABLE] — neither series carries a 2028 point.
  • The growth that is actually measured is the import bill: ₹41,667 cr in FY26 (+64% y/y) and ₹15,721 cr in Q1 FY27 (+94%) [REPORTED-T1: commerce ministry data]. Value growth here is volume plus price, not domestic substitution.
  • Drivers: the 50 GWh PLI-ACC programme, a ~260 GWh BESS tender pipeline, and a prospective battery-component scheme offering up to 50% capex support [REPORTED-T2: Economic Times, 4-Nov-2026].
Watch items
  • 10 Nov 2026 — China's Announcement 70 suspension lapses on its own text; the truce was extended to 10 Jan 2027 on 28-Sep-2026 but no amending instrument had been published as of 4-Nov-2026.
  • 10 Nov 2026 — the 10 GWh grid-scale ACC-PLI tender closes (extended from 13-Oct); technical bids open 11-Nov-2026. Correction from the prior run, which reported it closed on 13-Oct.
  • 1 Jan 2027 — China abolishes the 9% export VAT rebate on lithium-ion batteries (cut to 6% from Apr-2026), a direct cost increase on imported cells.
  • Battery-component scheme (CAM, AAM, separator, foil) under final evaluation at up to 50% capex support; not yet notified.
Coverage gaps
  • No listed pure-play for cell-line machinery (Chinese, Korean and German OEMs). Cell entities are unlisted subsidiaries (Ola Cell Technologies, Exide Energy Solutions, Agratas, Reliance New Energy).
  • Testing and certification has no listed exposure at all.

05 Bottlenecks — Risk & Opportunity dual-readable: every risk is a substitution play
Risk

Cathode & anode (CAM / AAM)China holds ~90% of LFP cathode and >90% of graphitisation; both materials are on its restricted-export list. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

Domestic CAM & anodeHimadri's first 2,000 tpa lands Q3 FY27, HEG's 20,000 tpa in Jun-2027 and Epsilon's 30,000 tpa in 2028 — against 422 kt of CAM and 237 kt of AAM needed by 2032, per MHI's own capacity gap.

Risk

Cell-line machinery & the 10 Nov 2026 cliff>75% of global Li-ion equipment capacity is Chinese; winding, stacking, filling, hot-press, formation and grading machines sit on China's dual-use list. The legal suspension text still reads 10 Nov 2026; the political truce was extended to 10 Jan 2027 but not written into the instrument. Resilience: months (requalification) to years (no substitute supplier at scale).

Opportunity

Machinery duty relief & integration~85 categories of cell-making machinery are BCD-exempt to 31 Mar 2029, and Indian pack/module-line integrators (Semco, Itech) are the natural bridge to a domestic equipment base.

Risk

Scale economicsIndian cells cost Rs 8-12/Wh against Rs 6-7/Wh imported — a 30-50% premium, with management guides clustering at 15-25% once materials localise. Breakeven is around 10 GWh. Resilience: years.

Opportunity

Policy-pulled demandCEA's storage mandate, the 20% DCR on VGF BESS and grid-forming rules create captive volume; PLI-ACC disbursements begin FY28.

Risk

Technology licences & skilled manpowerNearly all players rely on Chinese, Korean or Japanese technology licences, now restricted; Chinese-engineer visa delays slow commissioning; LFP process know-how is tacit. Resilience: structural.

Opportunity

In-house developmentAgratas developed LFP in-house after concluding Chinese licensing was near-nil; Indian process-engineering depth builds with each line commissioned.

Risk

Policy execution1 GWh installed of 40 GWh awarded five years in, no incentive claimed by any beneficiary, and the 10 GWh grid-scale tender extended to 10 Nov 2026 after 261 of 265 pre-bid queries drew boilerplate answers. Resilience: months (policy), years (capacity).

Opportunity

A component scheme with real moneyA battery-component scheme offering up to 50% capex support is in final evaluation — the first policy instrument aimed at the material layer rather than the cell layer.

Deepest hidden dependency: the cell-making machinery itself — electrode coaters, stackers, formation cabinets and HV test rigs — is Chinese-dominated and export-control exposed, so it is the true gate on how fast India can localise cells, ahead even of the materials.
06 Value pools & cost waterfall where value is captured · and which pool leaks to imports
Cost waterfall (ranked)
  • Cell-line machinery — ~55–60% of a 5 GWh plant's capex; imported in full.
  • Cathode CAM — 35–45% of material cost; up to 50–55% of cell cost.
  • Anode AAM — 12–18% of material cost, power-heavy to make.
  • Separator 4–6%, electrolyte 8–12%, foils and casing 5–8% (TCPL, IndexBox estimates).
  • Cell conversion margin — negative at 5 GWh, breakeven ~10 GWh [DERIVED from the plant-economics disclosures].
Where value is captured
  • Machinery OEMs capture the most per rupee of plant built, and none of it is Indian.
  • Cathode CAM — Himadri (HSCL), Gujarat Fluorochemicals (FLUOROCHEM), Tata Chemicals.
  • Anode AAM — HEG Advanced Materials (HEGAM), Himadri; Epsilon/TACC unlisted.
  • Separator & electrolyte — TCPL (TCPLPACK); Neogen (NEOGEN) + GFL.
  • Foils & casing — Hindalco (HINDALCO) battery-grade Al/Cu foil.
  • Cells — Ola (OLAELEC), Exide (EXIDEIND), Amara Raja (ARE&M), Waaree (WAAREEENER), Reliance (RELIANCE).
Who captures it (India-listed)
  • The material layer is where listed exposure actually sits: HSCL, FLUOROCHEM, HEGAM, NEOGEN, TCPLPACK, HINDALCO — and each is pre-revenue or early-revenue on this node today.
  • The cell layer is where the revenue is, but the operating entities are unlisted subsidiaries; the listed parents carry it as a segment, not as the business.
Pools that leak to imports
  • Cell-line machinery, in full — no listed pure-play, no domestic OEM at scale.
  • Cathode and anode material today, against 422 kt / 237 kt of 2032 requirement.
  • Separator until 2028 at the earliest; copper foil at 80–85% imported.
